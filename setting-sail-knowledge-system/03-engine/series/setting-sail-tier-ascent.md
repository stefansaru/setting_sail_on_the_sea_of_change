## The protagonist — "The Grounded One"
**Adam** (`CHR-06`) — the **Purist boy saved in the S1 finale**
([[setting-sail-tier-descent|S1E20]]): cured of a mortal illness by emergency cyborg implants, his
**[[progressive-digitalization|digitalization begun]]** against his people's vows. Years on, he is a young
man living among the [[setting-sail-tier-descent|Natural Purists]] (`FAC-02`) but **marked** — he carries
the Grid in his body. His name (Hebrew *adam*, "man", tied to *adamah*, "earth") is the thesis: the
creature of clay who eats the Grid's apple and is expelled from the mortal garden.

- **Archetype — "The Grounded One":** the exact inverse of S1's **"Unsynced Mind."** He starts with
  *everything* S1-Raul spent 20 episodes climbing down to reach — a mortal body, an un-mediated mind, a
  grounded community — and **gives it away, rung by rung, for gifts that feel like grace.**
- **Recurring guide:** **Raul** (`CHR-02`), who ended S1 as a **0% [[the-ghost-entity|Ghost]] in a shell**
  embedded with the purists. He is to Adam what the [[the-perspective-lens-ui|Teddy-Bear AI Guide]] was to
  him — except Raul has *been* to the bottom and back, and watches Adam climb the wrong way. *(Whether
  Raul's counsel is fully trustworthy is a held question — S2's `CHR-03` echo.)*
- **Antagonist:** **Vance** (`CHR-04`, S1-reserved, now active) — a merit-holder and a **Ghost** (`Q-90`), whose worldview is
  *total optimization + accountability are right; mortality is a bug to be patched.* The credible "**what if
  Raul was wrong?**" The tempter who offers Adam the keys to save his dying mother — for the last of his
  flesh. → [[intent-patch]], [[intention-in-context-law]].
- **The mother:** **Naomi** (`CHR-11`) — the Purist who broke her own vow to save Adam in S1E20, and is now
  **aging toward a mortal death in real-time** while Adam dilates away from her. Her *"eleven good ones"*
  (S1E19) is the season's clock.
- **The one who stays:** **Ruth** (`CHR-07`) — Adam's betrothed. When he climbs she stays with Naomi to the end,
  and she is the one who sends E10's message: *"She's dying. Come home."* (`Q-97`).

## Narrative arc — the trap of the climb
Adam ascends because he must: the implants that saved him **need the Grid** to live, and the village can't
service them. The first ascent is for *maintenance* — and up there, for the first time in his life, **the
pain stops.** That is the first bite. From there each tier offers a sweeter subtraction: no hunger, no
fatigue, no fear; then no dreams; then risk-free pleasure; then the [[parallel-pedagogy|Merging]] (lose the
singular mortal "I", gain a brilliant fused mind); then dilation enough to **outrun death itself.** Every
gift the audience reads as a loss the seduced Adam cannot. The **body-clock** decays as he climbs (the cost
is always flesh), and **mortal time keeps running below him:** dilation gives him more life per real hour,
never more real hours, so every subjective decade still costs her real time. By the time he can save his
mother, her whole remaining life has nearly run out.

**The spine, inverted:** S1's four Acts mapped the [Sea-of-Change phases](#the-sea-of-change--inverted-the-seductive-calm) A→D (harbor → flare → ocean → horizon). S2 runs the **mirror voyage** — *out of* the harbor of
the flesh, *into* the seductive calm of the Grid, and the storm is that the calm is **empty.** The
[[the-doughnut-principle|Doughnut]] resolves not as Raul's earned **solid** ring but as its tragic twin —
**balance bought one breath too late** (`Q-19`).

### The body clock — Progressive Digitalization (inverted valence)
Same organic→synthetic vector as S1 ([[progressive-digitalization]]) — but where Raul's decay was **fate**,
Adam's is **purchase**: he spends the very mortality his people died to keep.

| Act | Body state | Trigger |

**Where his body is (`Q-91`).** From E3 Adam's body rests in a **rented city capsule** while his mind climbs; it is
why the Dream Hunger (E6) can reach him and why he cannot simply walk home (`WR-B6`). He leaves it once, in E19,
on a licensed Sabbatical (`Q-72`).

**The real-time clock (`Q-48`).** S2E1 opens in the last months of Naomi's eleven years. On the series clock (T =
real years after S1E14, [[the-seed-fleet]]): S1E19's reading was at about T+8.5, so S2E1 is about T+19 and S2E20, her
death, about T+19.5, with Adam about 19. The fleet made landfall about nine and a half years earlier, 4.4 light-years out (`Q-52`). The climb itself fits inside her
last few months: about 3 months at Tier 1 (~15 years), 3 days at Tier 2 (~30 years), 2.4 hours at the Apex (24
years). That's about 70 subjective years for him, and one season at a bedside for Ruth. Ruth's E10 message comes
about four real hours before the end: about an hour more at Tier 2 (~150 days for him), 2.4 hours at the Apex, and
a descent of **about 40 real minutes** (`Q-93`).

### The glyph — the issued ring that never fills *(`Q-12` mirror)*
Raul's glyph ran **hollow → fractured → solid.** Adam's runs the tragic inverse:
**absent** (a purist has *no Lens* — raw, un-mediated mortal sight) → **issued hollow** (the Grid draws him
the same "Camel's Doughnut" at his convert-Merging) → **cold-complete** (at the Apex it closes into a
perfect, eternal, *meaningless* ring) → **cracked** (Ep 20: it shatters back open, and a single warm,
finite, flickering arrow appears — **the Mortal Doughnut**, balance held for one mortal breath, then gone).

### The Sea of Change — inverted: the seductive calm
- **Phase A′ — Leaving the Harbor of the Flesh (Ep 1–5).** The warm, low-key [[lighting|Caravaggio]] mortal
  village; raw un-mediated sight. The Sea (the Grid) calls him *up* with the promise of an end to pain. He
  unmoors from the body. → the inverse of S1's [[internalizing-the-exterior|Cultural Harbor]].
- **Phase B′ — The Sweetness (Ep 6–10).** The Grid's gifts arrive as grace; the high-key utopia he was
  warned about feels like *home*. The "storm" is hidden inside the calm — the gifts are subtractions. Mid-
  season: the **Merging** hollows the singular self. → inverts S1's [[the-shadow-grid-cooling|solar flare]].
- **Phase C′ — The Machine Behind the Curtain (Ep 11–15).** Climbing toward his mother's cure, Adam reaches
  the governance and law layer — and sees what runs it ([[the-distributed-president]], **Vance**, the
  [[the-personal-blockchain|chain]], the [[data-graveyards|Graveyards]]). The seduction **curdles.**
- **Phase D′ — The Apex, and the Reckoning (Ep 16–20).** He reaches Tier 3 — the eternity he climbed for —
  and it is hollow; mortal time has run out below. The tragic descent to be present for one mortal death.

## Season 2 — "The Mortal Ledger" (20-episode blueprint)
Format per episode: **Setting · Plot · UI/Visual hook.** Every 2.5-min script keeps the

### Act I — The First Bite (Ep 1–5) · *Tier 0 → 1 · 90% organic · Phase A′: leaving the harbor of the flesh*
1. **The Marked Boy** — *Tier 0, the Purist Village (`PLC-06`).* Cold open: Adam's fevered memory of S1E20, lossy
   and blurred, a mother's face and a chrome hand, the choice itself smeared past reading; the only way it is ever
   seen (a-ha #7, `WR-R4`; the boy who stared at Raul in S1E18). Nearly eleven years after his implants, Adam is a young man
   among the purists, half-belonging: the Grid-tech in his body sets him apart, and his mother **Naomi** is
   aging, and **Ruth** (`CHR-07`), who means to marry him, is waiting. On Naomi's lintel, a notch cut for each year since the implants: ten, and room for one more (a-ha #3).
   **Raul** (Ghost in a shell) watches over him. His implant **glitches** — a crack of cold blue
   Grid-light in the warm firelit village. *UI:* **no Lens** — raw, low-key [[lighting|Caravaggio]] mortal
   sight (the inverse of S1E1's pristine Tier-3). → mirrors [[setting-sail-tier-descent|S1E1]].
2. **The Maintenance Ascent** — *Tier 0 → 1.* The implants are failing; only the Grid can service them. Adam
   makes his **first ascent** — and the moment he crosses up, his years-old implant-pain **vanishes.** The
   first gift is *relief.* *UI:* his **first Lens** is installed — a single clean Context circle blooms over
   raw sight. The first thread it draws (his own stress trips the trigger, `WR-L3`) runs down the village feed to Naomi, amber, tagged **LOOP FORMING**: it is
   love, and the machine can't tell (a-ha #8; S1E3b's thread returns). → inverts S1E2's infant waking.
3. **The Painless World** — *Tier 1.* Offered a "temporary" residency for ongoing care, Adam is relieved of
   every discomfort he was raised to *endure* — hunger, cold, fatigue, fear. His body goes into a **rented city
   capsule** (`Q-91`), its bay tag reading `RENTED`: the first time his flesh waits while he lives elsewhere. It feels like grace; the
   audience sees the **flattening** of a man who knew weight. *UI:* Intention squares light with wants he
   never had ([[standardized-vices|standardized comfort]]). On his care chart, the compound his implants run on is
   credited to one open chain: **KAVANA · PUBLIC** (a-ha #9 reminder: S1E20's vial, given with no donor, made chrome,
   and that chrome is his implants, `Q-49`, `Q-119`; a-ha #10 reminder: she published after all). → inverts [[internalizing-the-exterior|S1E4]].
4. **The Garden Behind Glass** — *Tier 1, looking down.* Adam watches the village by feed and sees it the
   Grid's way — small, poor, *failing* its metrics. *UI:* his Lens auto-tags his own mother **"Cellular
   decay — advanced"** — he is now the chrome scanner S1-Raul was in [[setting-sail-tier-descent|S1E19]],
   pointed the wrong way. Naomi refuses to come up, and Ruth stays with her. First fracture from the garden.
5. **The Vow He Breaks** — *the [[inter-tier-travel|Data Buffer]] (T1).* To keep the implants alive Adam accepts
   a **permanent up-clock**, leaving real-time — breaking his own Purist vow, *"One life, kept whole"* (`Q-74`), as Naomi once broke hers for him.
   Naomi, on the village feed, at her own threshold: *"The Hurried don't have children. We do."* (plants S3E7's barrenness, `WR-B2`).
   *UI:* the down-clock's [[chromatic-expressionism|Despair field]] **inverted** — a *euphoric* golden field
   the audience reads as loss. **End Act I:** the surface — and mortal time — begins slipping away beneath him.

Only then, with a brain again, can he forget. Her cells were never rushed, so the flesh ages normally (`Q-57`); he ends a cyborg of mostly regrown flesh (`Q-98`). Ruth is at the bedside.
