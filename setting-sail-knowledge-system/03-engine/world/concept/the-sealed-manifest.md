> The High Clock knows the whole plan — the margins, the odds, the clause — and the base is told a kinder
> version "to keep them protected." The firewall is not a lock. It is the clock.

## Concept
The fleet is governed from the Apex by the **High Clock** (`FAC-05`): a council of [[the-ghost-entity|Ghosts]]
who stand the voyage's watches at Tier 3. They hold the **manifest** — the true [[the-stasis-economy]] ledger,
the fuel reserve, the ramp schedule, the real odds of landfall, and the terms Earth set for the mission.
The base, mostly Ghosts on watch with a few [[the-shadow-entity|Shadows]], is told what they need to keep working and hoping. The High
Clock is not malicious. It is **protective**. That is the problem the season is about.

## Rules / mechanics
- **Tier asymmetry is the firewall.** What the High Clock debates in an afternoon passes at the base in a
  blink ([[velocity-tiers]]). The lower tiers do not need to be *excluded* from governance; they literally
  **cannot attend**. Secrecy is a by-product of clock speed before it is a policy.
- **What is sealed:** the real margins; the real ramp count; the fuel curve; the deflect/fire record
  ([[the-firing-protocol]]) and its risk accounting; the mission's clause with Earth (what is owed back, and
  by whom); the **silence order** on the [[the-lure-beacon|beacon]] — given, never explained (S4E8); and, after
  Hull Four, its last log, whose hit sits under *the burst log that names who answered* (an order of events, not a
  cause, `Q-111`).
- **The clause behind the clause (`Q-110`).** One clause is sealed by a **tissue lock**, set before launch by
  someone with a body. A lock set in tissue answers only to tissue (`Q-67`, the rule behind S2E15's locked block and
  S3's un-run clip), so the High Clock, all Ghosts, can read that the clause exists and nothing more. It is filed
  against Hull One's standing-reserve embryo slot (`Q-112`, S4E4). Who set it, and whose body can open it, is S5's
  (a-ha #12; bible answer under Open questions).
- **What is told:** "the ramp is paused," "sensor fault," "all margins nominal."
- **The hypocrisy (the lever).** This world runs on **Full Disclosure** — the
  [[the-personal-blockchain|personal blockchain]] exposes every citizen's intent. The one institution exempt
  from disclosure is the one that holds everyone's lives. A Ghost who climbs high enough can *read* the
  manifest (S4E18): the firewall is a clock, and the only cap on a Ghost is the reactor's ration. Kai's is paid by
  Rafe (`Q-109`).
- **Cost:** trust, deferred. The base will one day arrive fast enough to read it — in a body, on a planet,
  with real resources. *(Part 2's revolt is the bill.)*
- **Limit:** the High Clock cannot seal what the array records. Logs are facts; only the *words* for them
  are chosen. And it cannot open what was sealed in tissue: the one secret kept *from* the council.

## In the world
The base experiences the High Clock as **weather from above** — decisions that arrive already made, in
polite, kind, finished sentences. A Shadow sent up a tier with a report meets a council that stopped
talking before they arrived (S4E5). Nobody at the base calls this secrecy. Nobody up top calls it a lie.

## Story role
The season's Doughnut held by the institution: **"we kept them safe" / "we never asked them."** Kai, once
protected by it, must hold both halves after reading it (S4E18) — and then **does the same thing** to the
base about the log (S4E19). The question the colony inherits: *is a protective lie the Grid's original sin,
or its only mercy?* Echoes [[the-distributed-president]] (law from status, never from consent) at
fleet scale.
