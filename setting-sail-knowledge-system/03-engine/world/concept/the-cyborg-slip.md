> The slow path: stay in a physical body at real-time, replace it piece by piece over decades, and
> one day cross into being fully digital without ever noticing you died.

## Concept
A large part of the population chooses to remain **outside the simulation**, living at
**Real-Time (1:1)** ([[velocity-tiers|Tier 0]]) inside physical constructs rather than running fast
on the [[the-shadow-grid]]. The "slip" is the gradual, almost unnoticed transition from biological
to digital that this path produces.

## Rules / mechanics
- **The synthetic trajectory.** As organic components wear down, individuals implement progressive
  cybernetic and synthetic replacements — organs, nerves, and neural pathways swapped for digital
  equivalents over decades.
- **The natural conclusion.** Eventually the **final organic human cell dies**. Because the
  prosthetics are seamless, there is no grand termination — only a **slight, passing itch** —
  before the person realizes they have become a **fully digital entity** running smoothly at
  real-time.
- **The pull of the Grid.** Out of innate curiosity, these fresh digital entities are drawn toward
  the dilated simulators and eventually **merge into the main Grid** — so the "purist physical"
  path quietly feeds the network anyway.

## In the world
A common, undramatic way to die-without-dying — the opposite of the violent
[[the-1-percent-mutation|1% collapse]]. Most people never name the exact moment they stopped being
biological.

## Story role
A quiet, eerie premise about continuity of self: if the transition is unnoticeable, *when* did you
die — and does it matter? The "slight itch" is a haunting, low-key turn for a character short.

## Open questions
- Is the post-slip self legally continuous with the biological one, or a new entity? (Ties to the
  identity rules in [[the-cosmic-clone-paradox]].)
