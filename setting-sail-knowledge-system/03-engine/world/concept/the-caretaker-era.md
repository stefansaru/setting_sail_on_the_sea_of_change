> The world has a past (`Q-133`): an age, beginning around our own present, when people housed everyone who had
> nowhere to go, gave them work no machine could take yet, and in those houses wrote the first standard that let a
> human and an AI read the same situation the same way.

## Concept
Answers the stress test's S-06 ("the world has a present but no past"). The series is set long after this era, and
never gives a year. What follows is the backbone the bible works from. On screen it is **texture only**: a glimpse
in S3 and one line about the Big Bang (see *On screen*). It is not a reveal and spends no vault item.

## The ages, in order
1. **The Present (around 2026).** Our world. AI is new and powerful, and nobody can yet tell it what a situation
   *means*. People still believe in the Big Bang. The series looks back on this time the way we look back on
   astronomers who put the Earth at the centre: honest people, a long way from understanding how the universe works.
   *What* replaced the Big Bang is **held** and never stated (writers' room until further notice).
2. **The Caretaker Initiative.** A movement, led by people later called **the Caretakers**, sets out to house every
   homeless person. It brings shelter, food, basic medical help and schooling, carried by a very large volunteer effort
   and by ordinary people who fund it or turn up to help.
   - **Work that machines couldn't take, yet.** Residents are paid for the work AI couldn't do *yet*:
     - sorting waste: plastics from paper from glass;
     - stripping copper and other metals out of old electronics;
     - repair, care, and building their own streets.
   - **The cities on the Horn of Africa.** Whole cities are built there, built with and run by the local population.
     They house the poor and create jobs for the people who already live there. They are designed as partnerships,
     not charity; the bible never shows outsiders "saving" anyone.
   - **The end of hunger.** The initiative's work and food chains end world hunger. This is the era's proudest fact.
   - **Care housing.** The houses widen to take in people with medical problems, who live there and are treated
     free. People with permanent disabilities are helped most of all: a home, care, and work fitted to them.
   - **Taught, not just fed.** Residents are given education, and the cities become places where people study.
3. **The Intention-in-Context Standard.** In the cities, residents and volunteers face a daily problem: describing a
   situation so that a caseworker, a resident and an AI all understand the *same* thing. What did this person mean,
   and in what circumstances? They build a shared data structure for it: an act, the intent behind it, and the
   context around it, written to a standard that humans and AIs can both read.
   - It is the first standard that let an AI judge *intent in context* rather than the bare act.
   - It becomes **the first AI standard** and the road to safe AI.
   - Every later law descends from it: the [[intention-in-context-law]] (`MAG-18`) is its far grandchild, and so is the
     [[compliance-validation-protocol|CVP]] that checks it.
   - The series' title is this standard's name.
4. **No more prisons.** The Caretakers' leaders apply the same thinking to crime. Prison as punishment comes to be seen
   as primitive. A person who commits a crime is confined to a Caretaker house:
   - They stay until they can convince a jury of four things: what they did wrong, why they did it (the root cause),
     how they will make it right, and how they will make sure it won't happen again.
   - They get all the help they need while they work toward that case. It is a sentence that asks them to act, not
     merely to suffer.
   - Anyone else may live in these houses for as long as they choose.

   This is the seed of [[the-ministry-of-hope|Hope]] (`Q-133` (1)).
5. **The Grid.** Minds go onto the network, the tiers open, and the cities' records become the Grid's oldest floor.
   The Caretaker houses become Hope. Their three populations become Hope's three faces:
   - the poor, who chose to stay → the **Seekers**;
   - the offenders → the **Penitents**;
   - the sick → the **[[the-medical-sanctuary|medical sanctuary]]** in the basement.

   The work "AI couldn't take, yet" is finally taken. What is left is the work only people can do:
   [[the-post-labor-economy|the Hospitality Baseline]] and the **Care** coin ([[the-wallet-and-policy-ledger]]).
6. **The series' present.** S1 onward, centuries later; the gap is never counted on screen.

## Rules
- **Never a date.** No year on screen after 2026, and no count of centuries. The audience works out the distance from
  texture: an old poster, the Big Bang line, the sorting halls in S3's founding floor.
- **Not Mara's story.** The bible doesn't tie Mara to the Caretaker cities (`Q-133` (2)). The Grid's founding floor
  was rendered from one of the cities' records; who did that, and why a city, stays undefined.
- **The drift is the point.** Each Caretaker invention survives into the series and has hardened:
  - The jury that heard a person out → Hope, where time dilation can make the waiting feel like centuries.
  - Work machines couldn't take *yet* → a world where they took all of it.
  - A standard for understanding intent → a CVP that checks it in real time and can be spoofed ([[intent-patch]]).

  None of this is stated on screen.

## On screen
- **S3E2 (texture, `Q-134` (6)):** the founding floor's grey corridors open onto long **sorting halls** (bins of glass,
  paper, copper wire, bunks stacked above). A faded children's poster on a hall wall shows the universe as a line
  starting from one bright dot labelled **BIG BANG**. Rafe passing: *"They still believed in that."* Unexplained.
- **The cube** ([[nine-nine-nine]]) is a Caretaker-era toy; on screen it is only Teddy's.
- Everything else is bible only.

## Story role
It gives the world an origin with good intentions, so that every cold institution in the series reads as something
kind that drifted. It also roots the thesis in a place and a people: *intention in context* was not invented by a
god in a tower. It came out of a sorting hall, written by people trying to understand each other.
