> The creed every Grid child is raised in. **Going second doesn't make you innocent:** if you are in a
> negative loop, you are at fault for not trying to break out of it. And nobody leads anyone without proof.

## Concept
"The Virtuous" names both the principles and the people raised in them. They are less a religion than common
knowledge, the way a society knows you need a licence to drive. A child hears them first from the
[[the-ai-guide|Teddy Bear]], in the nursery, as a response to a fight ([[s1e03a-he-started-it]]).

## Rules / mechanics
- **The loop rule.** A **negative loop** is a run of retaliation where each act is the reason for the next.
  Blame stops mattering. Both parties are at fault, not for starting it but for not trying to leave it.
- **The proof rule.** To be responsible for N people you must have passed a test with a survival rate of
  1 in N ([[the-one-in-n-rule]]). Without that proof, you can't claim to be virtuously responsible for them.
- **The alignment rule.** Children are raised with an **automated course-of-action setting** that pulls their
  acts into line with the principles ([[the-pattern-detector]]). It is sold, sincerely, as a kindness.
- **Argument over force, in principle.** Teddy offers to be disproved and offers evidence. The child can
  refuse the argument. The setting is not an argument.

## In the world
Taught in the nursery, recited by teens in E4 as *what to want and what to be*. A Grid adult no more
questions it than a fish questions water. The Purists (`FAC-02`) refuse it along with the Lens, and their
loops are fought out by hand, slowly, in real time.

## Story role
The Virtuous are the content of Raul's [[camel-phase|camel]] load: the good values he inherits unexamined.
That makes the descent harder, because the things he must clear out are *mostly right.* The unsettling
question the creed raises across the series: **if your good act was forced, was it your intention?**

**Held for S3 (do not reveal earlier):** the creed's author is Mara, who built it around a grief she could
never run. *The world's greatest negative loop wrote the rule against negative loops.*

## Open questions
- Does the loop rule ever excuse self-defence? [[generous-tit-for-tat]] says retaliation has a place; the
  Virtuous, as Teddy teaches them, leave little room for it. A deliberate gap for a dissecting audience.
