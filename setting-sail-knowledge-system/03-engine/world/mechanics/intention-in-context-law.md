> Statutory law is no longer archaic text — it is coded as **core principles plus explicit
> intention-in-context descriptions.** The legal system *is* the show's thesis ([[the-doughnut-principle]],
> [[intention-in-context]]): the system judges the **intent and the environment**, not just the act.

## Concept
The legal core queried by [[the-distributed-president]]. Each law is a **principle + its
intention-in-context**, evaluated dynamically rather than applied rigidly.

## Rules / mechanics
- **The Fluid Code.** A law is never rigid: the AI evaluates the **intent** and **environment** of an
  infraction, not just the physical act. An act committed under **systemic duress** re-calculates the
  legal response dynamically. (Same logic as Hope's [[the-absolution-protocol|Context Analysis]], at
  civic scale.)
- **The Meritocracy Upgrade.** Any human entity — biological, cybernetic, or fully digital
  [[the-ghost-entity|Ghost]] — can earn the right to **alter, optimize, or patch** the legal database.
  Strictly by proven utility, problem-solving capability, and meritocratic score, a citizen is granted a
  **cryptographic signature key.**
- **The Legislative Chain.** Every optimization or patch is **permanently signed and logged** to a
  global governance blockchain ([[the-personal-blockchain|ledger]] family) — absolute transparency,
  making **corruption mathematically impossible**, the claim Vance's forged batch breaks (S2E14, `Q-90`).

## In the world
Law is living code that good citizens *commit to*; the right to legislate is earned, not elected.

## Story role
The thesis as jurisprudence: a world that literally runs on *intention in context*. A rich source of
moral-grey episodes — an act that is "illegal" by letter but absolved by context, or a meritorious
citizen quietly patching a law with vast consequences.

## Open questions
- Can two high-merit citizens patch **contradictory** principles, and how does the President resolve a
  legislative conflict? (Sets whether the law can fracture.)
