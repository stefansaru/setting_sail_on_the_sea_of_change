> On the Grid, everything you look at is sharp, edge to edge. A human eye is sharp only in a small circle at the
> centre. Coming out of the tiers into your own body, you have to learn to see again.

## Concept
A human eye resolves fine detail only at the **fovea**, a spot covering about two degrees of the visual field, about
the size of a thumbnail held at arm's length. Everything else is soft. The brain hides this by moving the eyes several
times a second and stitching the result together. Grid sight has no fovea: the render is equally sharp everywhere, at
every tier. The higher the tier, the cleaner it is.

So when a mind used to the tiers looks out through organic eyes again, the world seems to have **narrowed**. Only
where they look is clear, the edges smear, and they keep turning their head to bring things into focus. They have to
learn what every infant learns: to see by looking around.

## Rules / mechanics
- **Who gets it.** Anyone who comes back to *organic* eyes after time on the Grid:
  - a Shadow on a licensed Sabbatical (`Q-72`);
  - a Shadow woken from fleet stasis;
  - a Woken colonist in a ramp body (S5).

  Ghosts in shells and heavy cyborgs **don't**: shell optics and implants see full-field ([[shells-with-ghosts-within]]).
  Raul (S1E18) and Adam (S2E19) step out with machine eyes, so no narrowing. That mercy of chrome is never remarked.
- **How long.** It scales with time away and with how little the eyes were used:
  - a Sabbatical after a few real weeks: **hours**;
  - after real years: **days**;
  - a capsule-born body that has barely opened its eyes ([[the-quickening]]): **weeks**, with headaches and nausea.
- **What it feels like.** Narrowness, not blindness. The edges are there but won't hold still. Reading is slow.
  Faces at the edge of a room are smudges. The eyes dart and tire. Grid citizens call it the **Narrow Eye**.
- **The cost.** It keeps Sabbaticals supervised: someone walks you to the table. It is also one more reason the tiers
  feel like home and the body feels like a smaller room.

## In the world / look (cinematography convention, `VIS-18`)
- **Grid POV:** deep focus, sharp corner to corner, no vignette.
- **Narrow-Eye POV:** a small sharp circle in the frame, the rest soft and slightly smeared. The circle *jumps*, eye
  to plate to window, in quick cuts. It is shot in camera (lens, iris, shallow focus) per `VIS-13`, never a filter.
  It widens over the reel as the eye relearns.
- **Never** used for machine optics, which keep the locked, full-field look of `VIS-04`.

## On screen
- **The First Supper (reel):** the Patron's first minutes at 1:1. The plate is sharp and the chef is a smudge. The
  attendant: *"It's your eyes. They only do the middle. Give it a day."* (`Q-134` (2))
- **S5 (bible):** the Woken on landing, in bodies whose eyes have never focused on anything real. The convention is
  logged for them.

## Story role
A small physical fact that carries the series' argument. The Grid gives you everything at once. A body gives you one
thing at a time, and the rest only if you turn toward it. Learning to see again means learning to choose where to look.
