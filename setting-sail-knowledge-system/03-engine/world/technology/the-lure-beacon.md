> A signal that looks like a supernova, decodes as a cry for help in every Earth language, and asks — in
> its last layer — for exactly one thing: where you are. Anyone kind enough to answer gives themselves away.

## Concept
The fleet's first contact with **the Unlisted** (`FAC-06`) is not a ship. It is an **artificial satellite
beaming in all directions** — a lure. Its broad-spectrum burst arrives from the direction of a star the
catalogue has long expected to go supernova, and at first reading it **is** that supernova: the base
gathers to watch the sky do something once in a lifetime. It is not the sky. It is a message, and the
message is built like a trap for the kind of mind a seed fleet keeps most of: hopeful, under-informed, and
slow.

## Rules / mechanics
- **The disguise.** An omnidirectional high-energy burst with the spectral shape of a nova, coming from the
  one star where a nova would be *expected*. The catalogue flags it *natural*; the base is mesmerized; only
  closer analysis shows the structure under the noise.
- **The nested layers** (each decoded only after the one before is believed):
  1. **"help"** — a bare distress signal.
  2. **"we're just like you, please help us"** — in **every Earth language** at once. Something dumb, easy
     to relate to, built for a vulnerable mind to act on.
  3. **"please send us your heading, speed and acceleration relative to the star — rest assured it did not
     go supernova."** The ask. The reassurance is the tell: it knows what the base thought it was watching.
  4. **"you can be our hero."** The layer only the one still decoding will find — the one who was told to
     stop.
- **Omnidirectional = a net, not a hail.** The beacon is not aimed. It is cast. Whoever answers reveals a
  vector; the beacon's owners come to *that*.
- **Cost of answering:** the fleet's position, heading, speed and acceleration — everything needed to
  find it, given time. The bill comes due in S5, not in the next day's window.
- **Limit:** the beacon cannot find a fleet that stays silent. The High Clock understands this at Apex
  speed and orders silence (S4E8). The order is sealed, not explained ([[the-sealed-manifest]]). A base
  worker who was never told *why* keeps decoding, finds layer 4, and sends a burst (S4E9).
- **Where it is (bible).** On the line of sight to the star the catalogue expects to die, **within about 0.27
  light-years** of the fleet. Its lure is written from Raul's beam, read in transit in the last months before it
  landed on Hull Four (T+3.9, a-ha #4), and its first light has to land between then and the strike (about T+4.2,
  [[the-seed-fleet]]): 0.3 years, less the time to write it. Nor can it be much nearer: the fleet moves about 17 AU
  a day, and a beacon closer than a few tenths of a light-year would slide off the star's line within the weeks the
  base watches it.
- **Light-lag sets what it can do.** Teo's burst (S4E9) takes about three months to reach the beacon, and nothing
  sent back can reach the fleet in less than about half a year from the burst. The hit came the next day, so it
  **can't be the beacon's answer**: it was a grain of natural rock (`Q-66`). What the burst did give away
  is the vector, and whatever comes for that comes in S5.
- **Consequence (established, S4E10, S4E19):** the strike comes through Hull Four's open window during a
  routine lock. The log says *sensor fault*. The last log, read at the Apex, shows only that the hit's timestamp
  sits under the burst (`Q-111`). Correlation, not cause, and on screen nobody says which.

## In the world
For three episodes the beacon is the most beautiful thing the base has ever seen: a star dying on
schedule, then a voice inside it speaking their own languages. The High Clock sees a lure the way a Tier-3
mind sees anything — whole, immediately, with an afternoon to decide. The base sees it the way the beacon
was designed to be seen.

## Story role
The world's **tier asymmetry weaponized by an outsider**: the Unlisted do not break the fleet's shield;
they break its *pyramid*. The slow, kind base is the attack surface, and the High Clock's secrecy is what
leaves it undefended. The Doughnut: *the message was a lie / the person who answered was good.* **Failure
mode:** a hero.
