# Production Guide — From Script to Finished Short

> The house **production workflow** for turning an audited script into a finished, distributed
> short/reel. Where [[fantasy-writing]] governs *story* and [[scifi-cinematography]] governs
> *image*, this guide governs *process* — the controlled path that turns an idea into a
> finished story while reducing uncertainty at every step. General framework, with **▶ notes**
> for the anthology's reality: 4–7 min sci-fi shorts, single-location, small-cast, interface
> overlay (not spectacle), micro-budget. The worked example is [[setting-sail-tier-descent]].

> **The pipeline:** Development → Pre-Production → Production → Post-Production → Distribution.
> Each phase **reduces uncertainty and increases execution clarity** — by the time the camera
> rolls, the decisions already exist.

---

## Phase 1 — Development (the idea stage)

### 1.1 Concept
Start from a clear core idea, explainable in **1–2 sentences**. Answer: *who is the main
character? what is their core problem/transformation? where does it take place? why does this
story matter now?*
> ▶ In this engine the concept is pre-loaded: each short = **one Brain concept + its failure
> mode** (the built-in turn). The logline already exists in the anthology outline.

### 1.2 Idea development
Expand the concept into a creative direction: **story arc, theme, tone, genre conventions** —
supported by mood boards, reference films, and visual inspiration.
> ▶ Theme/tone come from [[fantasy-writing]] §4/§30; the reference-film + mood-board work is
> the `VIS-*` block of [[canon-registry]] and the touchstones in [[scifi-cinematography]].

### 1.3 Screenwriting — the blueprint
The script defines dialogue, scenes, locations, actions, pacing. Core principles: **every line
serves story or character; write how people actually speak; keep structure tight; tell
*visually*, don't explain.** A film without a strong script is unstable from the start.
> ▶ Draft scripts with [`../prompts/draft-fantasy-fiction.md`](../prompts/draft-fantasy-fiction.md),
> then **gate them through the audit** ([`../prompts/audit-script.md`](../prompts/audit-script.md)
> against [[writing-requirements]]) *before* a dollar is spent. A short's "tight structure" is
> minutes, not 90–120 pages — one idea, one turn ([[fantasy-writing]] §19).

### 1.4 Budgeting — the reality check
Budget defines how creativity is executed, not whether. Core categories: **cast & crew,
equipment, locations, props & costumes, post.** Rough estimate:
> **Total ≈ (pages ÷ pages-per-day) × daily-cost.**
> ▶ For a 4–7 min short this is often a **1–3 day** shoot. The whole anthology is *designed*
> to be cheap — single location, small cast, the sci-fi delivered as an interface overlay or
> one device ([[scifi-cinematography]] §13, `CINE-15` VFX restraint). Budget posture is a
> first-class canon fact, not an afterthought.

## Phase 2 — Pre-Production (the engineering phase)
Where films are actually engineered. **Most success happens here, before filming starts.**

### 2.1 Casting & crew
Assemble: director of photography, actors, production designer, sound crew, assistants. Source
via auditions, showreels, networking, film schools / indie talent. Prioritize **reliability +
skill + collaboration ability** over raw flash.
> ▶ Small-cast by design — often one protagonist + the system voice (Mara, `CHR-01`).

### 2.2 Locations & production design
Define the physical world: scouting, **permits**, set design, lighting adaptation.
> **Choose locations that serve the script — not the reverse.**
> ▶ Single-location posture (a flooded channel/dock, a desk + overlay). Production design must
> match the `VIS-*` look (human↔machine contrast `VIS-03`, the through-line motif `VIS-12` — doughnut + arrow; the water+sail `VIS-07` is HELM-legacy)
> and the setting-aesthetics in [[setting-design]] (lived-in texture, material realism,
> beauty-vs-decay) — use its production-design checklist here.

### 2.3 Storyboards & shot lists — the visual translation
Translate the script into **camera angles, framing, movement, scene flow**. Storyboards = the
visual sequence; shot list = the technical breakdown. This pre-decides decisions so the set
isn't chaos.
> ▶ **This is the artifact the CINE audit checks.** Run the cinematography perspective of the
> audit on the storyboard/shot list to verify scale hierarchy, the palette canon (`VIS-09`/`VIS-15`; 60-30-10 `VIS-01` legacy),
> motivated light, and a planned wonder beat (`VIS-06`) *before* the shoot — see
> [[scifi-cinematography]] / layer **CINE** in [[writing-requirements]].

### 2.4 Scheduling — production logic
Convert creativity into logistics: shooting calendar, scene grouping, **call sheets**, daily
plans. Rules: **group scenes by location, avoid unnecessary moves, optimize time per setup.**
> A shoot is a logistical puzzle, not chronological storytelling — you film out of order.

## Phase 3 — Production (the execution phase)
Where the film is physically captured. **Production is execution, not invention** — and the
goal is to **capture options, not perfection.**

### 3.1 Shooting
Frame to the storyboard, control lighting intentionally, record clean sound, shoot multiple
takes. The three pillars:
- **Lighting** — defines mood, creates depth, supports tone *(execute the `VIS-02` motivated
  sources: panels, holograms, reactor glow — "the room lights itself").*
- **Camera** — composition guides emotion; movement reflects narrative energy *(execute the
  `VIS-04` motion grammar: machine-smooth for the system, breathing/handheld for the human).*
- **Sound** — **never rely on the camera mic;** record clean dialogue separately. Capture room
  tone and wild sound for post.

---

## Phase 4 — Post-Production (the shaping phase)
Where the film truly becomes a finished story. **Editing, sound, and color define the final
emotional impact.**

### 4.1 Editing — story construction
Editing is **rewriting the film in visual form.** Stages: **Assembly cut** (everything in
order) → **Rough cut** (structure forms) → **Fine cut** (rhythm, pacing, emotion) → **Final
cut** (locked). Re-test the locked cut against the story requirements ([[writing-requirements]]
SCENE/STORY) — the edit can break or fix arc and escalation.

### 4.2 Sound design — the emotional engine
Treat sound as a **narrative layer, not decoration**: ambient beds, diegetic/non-diegetic
effects, foley, atmospheric layers. Sound bridges scenes, builds tension, carries emotion.
> ▶ The throughline image has a sonic twin — e.g. the dock foghorn that is Mara's voice
> (`FCT-001`). Keep recurring audio motifs consistent across episodes.

### 4.3 Sound mixing — the balance system
Balance every element with EQ, compression, panning, reverb, gain staging. Goal: **everything
clearly audible and emotionally controlled.**

### 4.4 Color correction — the technical fix
*Realism, not style:* fix exposure, correct **natural skin tones**, normalize consistency,
**match shots together** so the sequence feels like one world.

### 4.5 Visual effects (VFX)
Use only when something can't be shot practically, or for correction/enhancement.
> **VFX should be invisible, not noticeable.** One convincing effect beats fifty mediocre ones
> ([[scifi-cinematography]] §13, `CINE-15`). The interface overlay is the main "effect" here.

### 4.6 Color grading — artistic styling
Where the film becomes *cinematic*: LUTs, palettes, contrast shaping set mood, time
perception, emotional atmosphere (blue = cold/isolation; orange = warmth/humanity).
> ▶ **Grade to the palette canon** — the `VIS-09` color-vectors + `VIS-15` chromatic-expressionism
> templates are the grade target (the 60-30-10 `VIS-01` is HELM-legacy), and the accent is
> where the eye goes ([[scifi-cinematography]] §6, `CINE-06`). Correction (4.4) makes it
> *real*; grading makes it *mean* something. Keep the grade consistent across the anthology
> (`ANTH-06`).

## Phase 5 — Distribution & Promotion (the final phase)
**A film that is not seen is incomplete** — distribution is part of production.

### 5.1 Distribution
Channels: cinemas, streaming, **YouTube/Vimeo, festivals**. Distributors handle release
strategy, localization (subtitles), marketing — but **independent creators can bypass them
entirely.**
> ▶ This anthology is built for **shorts/reels**: vertical/short-form platforms + festivals.
> Plan aspect-ratio and captioning from pre-production, not after.

### 5.2 Promotion
Create demand before and after release with **trailers (≈1.5–2.5 min), teasers, social
content, collaborations.** A good trailer: **don't spoil the story; show emotional stakes;
keep pacing tight.**
> ▶ Each short *is* its own promo for the anthology; the recurring Mara trace + water/sail
> motif is the throughline that turns a set of reels into a *binge*.

---

## Core principles (the through-line)
1. **Clarity over complexity** — a simple strong idea beats a complex weak one.
2. **Preparation reduces chaos** — most success happens before filming.
3. **Every department aligns to story** — camera, sound, light, edit all serve narrative.
4. **Production is execution, not invention** — decisions exist before the shoot.
5. **Post is where the film is truly made** — editing, sound, color set the emotional impact.
6. **Distribution is part of production** — an unseen film is unfinished.

These rhyme with the writing-side §36 priority hierarchy: *story first; everything else serves
the story.*

## Appendix — Per-short production checklist
Copy per episode; tick before advancing a phase.

```
DEV   □ logline (1–2 sentences)  □ theme + failure-mode named  □ script drafted
      □ script PASSES the gate audit  □ budget/day-count estimated
PRE   □ cast locked  □ location + permits  □ production design matches VIS-*
      □ storyboard + shot list  □ shot list PASSES the CINE audit
      □ schedule + call sheets (scenes grouped by location)
PROD  □ framing to storyboard  □ motivated lighting (VIS-02)  □ motion grammar (VIS-04)
      □ clean separate sound + room tone  □ multiple takes / coverage
POST  □ assembly → rough → fine → final cut  □ re-test STORY/SCENE reqs on locked cut
      □ sound design + mix  □ color correct (skin/consistency)  □ VFX (invisible)
      □ color grade to palette canon VIS-09/VIS-15 (+ accent)  □ ANTH-06 look-consistency vs other episodes
DIST  □ master + captions/subtitles  □ aspect ratio for target platform
      □ trailer/teaser (no spoilers, stakes, tight)  □ release + festival plan
```
