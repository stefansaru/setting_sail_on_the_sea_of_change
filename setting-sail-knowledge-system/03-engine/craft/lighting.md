# Lighting Deep-Dive — World-Building in the Shadows

> A companion to [[scifi-cinematography]] (it expands §7 *light as technology* and §9
> *atmosphere*) alongside [[negative-space]] and [[cinematography-techniques]]. In sci-fi you
> aren't lighting a set — you're **generating a reality, so you make the rules.** The light
> grammar sets the audience's psychological boundaries and implies a world continuing past the
> frame edge. Each pillar below serves a **CINE** requirement ([[writing-requirements]]) and
> its recurring choices belong in the `VIS-*` block of [[canon-registry]]. **▶ notes** map it
> to the aesthetic of the [[setting-sail-tier-descent|*Intention in Context* series]].

> **Rule of thumb:** never light a sci-fi set just so the audience can *see the actors* — light
> it so they understand the **political, psychological, and environmental rules** of the world
> they're looking into. (This is `CINE-02`: every visual choice argues the theme.)

---

## The 5 pillars

### 1. Practical lighting (on-set instrumentation) · *serves `CINE-10`, `VIS-02`*
Don't rely solely on off-camera studio lights — **build the sources into the world.** LED
strips, monitor screens, illuminated control panels, neon fixtures in the set walls and props,
emitting real exposure and lifting ambient levels.
- **Effect:** a tactile, sensory space; chosen color temperatures use color psychology to
  state a society's or ship's mood *before a line of dialogue.*
- **▶** The **HELM interface overlay *is* your practical source** — "the room lights itself"
  (`VIS-02`). The module's accent color (`VIS-01`, `Q-03`) is literally the light it casts.

### 2. Hard light & rich shadows · *serves `CINE-10`, `CINE-11`; pairs with [[negative-space]]*
Darkness is not empty — it holds narrative. Use **uncompromising hard light** for crisp,
defined shadow lines, and **negative fill** (big black flags/fabric that *absorb* light) to
strip bouncing reflections off walls and faces.
- **Effect:** pitch-black shadow injects mystery and dread; the audience fills the blanks of an
  untold backstory and feels the character's isolation/fracture.
- **▶** Dark voids double as composed negative space ([[negative-space]]); use hard light +
  negative fill for the **failure-mode** beats where the clean world turns oppressive.

### 3. Underlighting (the third-act shift) · *serves `CINE-02`, `CINE-09`*
Standard light mimics the sun or overhead interiors; breaking that **signals something is
wrong.** Place fixtures **low, on the floor, pointing up** into faces and bodies.
- **Effect:** distorts facial structure, builds tension, telegraphs that the world has turned —
  taken to its extreme, reduces characters to ominous, unreadable silhouettes. A potent **Act
  Three** tool.
- **▶** Reserve it for the **turn** — the exact moment a HELM module's failure mode bites
  (pairs beautifully with the dolly zoom, [[cinematography-techniques]] §1).

### 4. Strong backlight & atmospheric diffusion · *serves `CINE-12`, `VIS-06`*
To make a flat screen feel endless, separate subject from background with depth and texture.
Put a **powerful light directly behind the subject, toward the lens**, paired with **smoke,
fog, or persistent haze.**
- **Effect:** the backlight cuts the particles into **volumetric beams**, pulling the eye to the
  subject while their front falls to shadow — suspense, awe, cosmic wonder.
- **▶** The cheapest route to a **wonder beat** (`VIS-06`, [[scifi-cinematography]] §15) inside a
  single location: one hard backlight + haze turns a plain room into scale and mystery.

### 5. Make your own rules · *serves `CINE-02`, `CINE-16`*
You're building an original universe — you're unbound by earthly aesthetics. Hard or soft
light, heavy backlight, anamorphic flares, thick haze: each is an *active storytelling tool*,
not a default. But "make your own rules" is not "no rules" — whatever you choose, record it in
`VIS-*` and **hold it** (consistency `CINE-01`/`ANTH-06`), and make it serve emotional truth
(`CINE-16`), never spectacle alone.

## Sci-fi lighting setup reference
Align technical lighting with your sub-genre during pre-production:

| Sub-genre | Primary lighting | Atmosphere | Emotional target |
|-----------|------------------|------------|------------------|
| **Cyberpunk / Dystopian** | Heavy practicals (neon, UI screens) | Low-lying haze / smog | Sensory overload, corporate oppression |
| **Deep Space / Horror** | High-contrast hard light + negative fill | Clean, crisp voids | Isolation, fear of the unknown |
| **Post-Apocalyptic** | Harsh sun / hard side-light | Heavy dust / smoke | Desolation, survival grit |
| **High-Tech / Space Opera** | Balanced soft diffusion + clean backlights | Light, subtle mist | Awe, cosmic scale, wonder |

> ▶ **This anthology** blends two rows: a **High-Tech** clean baseline (soft diffusion, clean
> backlights — HELM's clinical order, the clean near-future of [[scifi-cinematography]] §14) that
> **degrades toward Deep-Space/Horror** (hard light, negative fill, crisp voids) as each
> module's failure mode takes hold. It is *not* cyberpunk-neon or post-apocalyptic — record this
> as the `VIS-02`/`VIS-05` rule so episodes stay consistent.

## How this connects to the engine
Lighting is the execution side of two cinematography principles, so it's audited under **CINE**:

| Requirement | Pillar |
|-------------|--------|
| `CINE-10` motivated light | Practicals (1), Hard light + negative fill (2) |
| `CINE-11` human↔machine contrast | Hard shadow (2) — cool machine vs warm human |
| `CINE-12` atmosphere / volume | Backlight + haze (4) |
| `CINE-02` theme-justified visuals | Underlighting (3), Make-your-own-rules (5) |
| `CINE-09` composition | Underlighting silhouettes (3) |
| `CINE-16` emotional truth | the rule-of-thumb; Make-your-own-rules (5) |

Recurring choices (light-source logic, contrast level, haze policy, the Act-3 underlight) live
in `VIS-02` (sources) and `VIS-05` (texture) of [[canon-registry]] and are drift-checked by
`ANTH-06`. **Executing** them on set is [[film-production]] Phase 3.1 (shooting).
