# Composition Deep-Dive — Shooting 9:16 Vertical (the I.I.C. format)

> The reframing of [[scifi-cinematography]] for **9:16 vertical** with the always-on diegetic
> [[the-perspective-lens-ui|Perspective Lens]] UI. The general guides teach widescreen (2.39:1,
> horizontal lead-room); *Intention in Context* is shot **tall**, for TikTok / Reels / Shorts, with
> **Context circles `[◯]` pinned top and Intention squares `[▢]` pinned bottom**. This doc adapts the
> principles; the deep-dives [[negative-space]], [[lighting]], and [[cinematography-techniques]] still
> apply, re-oriented as below. Audited under **CINE** ([[writing-requirements]]); look canon `VIS-08`.

> **The one inversion to internalize:** widescreen hoards *horizontal* space; 9:16 hoards *vertical*.
> Everything you knew about lead-room, two-shots, and scale rotates 90°.

---

## 1. The frame is bounded — compose for the UI-safe window
The UI is **not** an overlay you can ignore; it is a permanent **frame-within-a-frame**
([[cinematography-techniques]] §10). The top **Context bar** and bottom **Intention bar** eat the
top/bottom edges, leaving a **central cinematic window** for the scene.
- Keep critical action and eyelines in the **central safe zone**; treat the top/bottom ~10–15% as
  UI-occupied (heads and key detail clear of the bars).
- The UI's state *is* coverage: when it **stabilizes or fades to transparency**
  ([[the-perspective-lens-ui]]), the cinematic window **opens up** — a built-in reveal (see the Ep 19
  UI-fade climax). Plan shots for both "UI-on" (boxed) and "UI-off" (full-frame) states.

## 2. Verticality is the native language · *serves `CINE-08`*
9:16 is *built* for the show's spine — a **descent**. Use the tall frame to stack scale:
- **Top-to-bottom scale:** a towering Grid megastructure up top, a tiny figure at the bottom — the
  [[velocity-tiers]] descent rendered as literal vertical position.
- **Vertical "World → Society → Character"** ([[scifi-cinematography]] §4): stack the layers in one
  frame, or move the eye down the column, rather than the widescreen left-right sweep.
- The **down-tilt / down-crane is the signature move** — descending the tiers, sinking toward the
  real world.

## 3. Negative space goes vertical · *serves `CINE-09`, [[negative-space]]*
Lead-room and looking-room **rotate to the vertical axis**:
- **Headroom above** = awe, insignificance, the Grid/sky pressing down (a small figure under a vast
  top void). **Footroom/space below** = the abyss, the descent, what's coming.
- **Lead-room for motion** is now *vertical*: a **descending** figure gets space *below*; a rising one,
  space *above*. (Falling = removing the space beneath them.)
- **Looking-room is cramped horizontally** — a profile has little side space, so favor **frontal /
  near-frontal** framings or **vertical stacking** over wide side-profiles.

## 4. Faces, singles, and the death of the wide two-shot · *serves `CINE-09`*
Side-by-side two-shots (the widescreen staple) **fight the frame**. Instead:
- **Vertical stacking** — one figure above/behind another (foreground low, background high).
- **Over-the-shoulder** and **alternating singles** carry dialogue.
- For a true two-subject frame (e.g. the Ep 19 chrome hand over warm flesh), go **tight and stacked**,
  not wide — let the contrast fill the tall frame top-to-bottom.

## 5. Symmetry & center-punch hit harder · *serves `CINE-09`, [[cinematography-techniques]] §8*
A centered subject between the two UI bars reads as **boxed, clinical, trapped** — the Kubrick
register, amplified. Vertical **mirror symmetry** (a single center line) is the strongest, cleanest
composition in 9:16 — perfect for the Grid's controlling order, and for the final
[[the-macro-doughnut]] center-fuse. Break to **asymmetry / tilt** ([[cinematography-techniques]] §6
Dutch) when the order cracks (Phase B).

## 6. Movement, focus, and the adapted toolkit · *serves `CINE-13`, `CINE-14`*
- **Tilts and cranes (vertical) replace pans (horizontal)** as the default; push-ins and the **dolly
  zoom** ([[cinematography-techniques]] §1) read fine.
- **Rack focus** ([[cinematography-techniques]] §2) pulls **top↔bottom** (foreground-low to
  background-high) instead of left-right.
- **Split-diopter** ([[cinematography-techniques]] §3): stack the two sharp planes **top and bottom**.
- **Deep focus** ([[scifi-cinematography]] §11) makes the tall column inspectable end to end.

## 7. Lighting for the tall frame · *[[lighting]]*
Mostly aspect-agnostic, with vertical accents:
- A face fills a tall frame, so **top-light and under-light read strongly** — use under-light
  ([[lighting]] §3) for the Act-III/IV "wrongness" beats; **backlight + haze** ([[lighting]] §4) gives
  a vertical god-ray column.
- Keep **human↔machine contrast** (`CINE-11`) legible at phone size: the warm/cool split must survive
  a small screen.

## 8. Legibility for the medium (phone-small, 2.5-min, algorithmic)
Vertical shorts are watched small, fast, often muted:
- **Favor close-ups and clean silhouettes;** wide establishing detail is lost on a phone.
- **The 10% accent (`VIS-09`/`VIS-01`) is the eye-anchor** — spend it to point the glance instantly.
- **Hook in the first second** (the cold-open extreme close-up, e.g. Ep 19); high contrast; captions
  legible above the bottom Intention bar.
