# on learning to say no

*a letter for my daughter — teaches [[art-of-saying-no]]*

---

my love,

there will be a stretch of your life — maybe a long one — where you say yes to
almost everything. yes to the favor. yes to the plan you didn't want. yes to the
version of you other people find easiest. you'll do it to be kind, to fit in, to
avoid the small sting of disappointing someone. and from the outside it will look
like generosity.

i want you to know, early, what took me too long to learn: every yes is spent out of
the same small purse. your hours. your attention. the energy you have for the things
and people you actually love. when you say yes to what doesn't matter, you are
quietly saying no to what does.

so here is the thing nobody tells you — *no* is not rude. *no* is not cold. a good no
is one of the kindest, clearest gifts you can give, because it tells people the
truth. you don't owe anyone a ten-minute speech to justify it. the softest no is
usually the strongest one: *"thank you for thinking of me, but i'll pass."* that's a
whole sentence. you can stop there.

you won't always get it right. sometimes you'll say no when you should have leaned in,
and sometimes you'll say yes and regret it by tuesday. that's fine. the skill isn't
to become someone who refuses everything — the loudest rebel is just as trapped as
the tireless camel. the skill is to choose. to let your *yes* mean something because
your *no* is real.

and on the days it feels hard, remember: you are not being difficult. you are
protecting something. you owe people honesty. you owe yourself peace.

i love you. say no when you need to. i'll be proud of you either way.

— dad
