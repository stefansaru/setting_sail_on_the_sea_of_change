# Voice Notes

> So the personal register can be reproduced faithfully — by a human or an AI
> assistant — long after the author is gone. Derived from observable features of the
> manuscript. **The author should refine this; it is a starting portrait, not a verdict.**

## Observable style features (from the book)
- **All-lowercase prose.** A deliberate, intimate, unintimidating texture. (The codex
  normalizes to sentence case for retrieval; the *legacy* layer keeps lowercase if the author wishes.)
- **Second-person address.** Speaks directly to "you" — warm, coaching, never lecturing.
- **Metaphor-first.** Hard ideas arrive wrapped in images (camel, sailor, dragon,
  bricks, mirror). The voice teaches through pictures before propositions.
- **Mascots and small stories.** Recurs to named characters (Cammy, the penguins) to
  carry abstract points.
- **Gentle imperatives.** "pause and observe." "breathe." "ask yourself." Invites
  rather than commands.
- **Short, rhythmic sentences** at emotional peaks. ("do you adapt? or complain? do
  you learn? or freeze?")
- **Synthesis over originality-claims.** Comfortable quoting Nietzsche, Rohn, Frankl,
  Tolstoy; positions itself as a guide who has gathered wisdom, not its sole source.

## Tone for the legacy layer specifically
When writing *to the daughter*, the register shifts from "you, the reader" to "you,
my child": more tender, more willing to admit difficulty and uncertainty, more
"here's what I wish I'd known," less instructional. A letter may say what a neutral
page never would — *why this matters to me, and what it cost me to learn.*

## A few seed phrasings (in-voice)
- "we've got this." (to a frightened inner child)
- "you're not the wind, you're the sailor."
- "the burdens are yours only because you keep choosing to carry them."

## What to avoid
- Corporate or clinical phrasing.
- Certainty where the book is honestly uncertain (see [[contradictions]]).
- Stripping the metaphors out to "just say the point" — the metaphors *are* the point's delivery system.
