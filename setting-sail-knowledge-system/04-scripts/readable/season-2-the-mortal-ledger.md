# Intention in Context — Season 2: *The Mortal Ledger*
### Readable prose edition

> The 20 episodes of Season 2, retold as short stories — the production scaffolding (shot timings,
> UI cues, canon tags) stripped away so the arc can simply be *read*. Source of truth remains the
> shooting scripts in [`../episodes/`](../episodes/) and the blueprint [`../../03-engine/series/setting-sail-tier-ascent.md`](../../03-engine/series/setting-sail-tier-ascent.md);
> this is the reading copy. One young man — **Adam**, the Purist boy saved by chrome at the end of Season 1 —
> climbs *upward* through the velocity tiers, from a village that aches toward the Apex, taking each gift the
> Grid offers, while below him his mother's time runs out at its own speed.

## Act I — The First Bite

### 1. The Marked Boy
Firelight, smeared into streaks. A woman's face, far too close, her mouth shaping words that never arrived; one
fragment came through, warped: *"…still be him…"* A chrome hand closed over hers, the only sharp thing in the memory.
A glint of glass, a smear where a label would be. A child's ragged cough. It broke before anything was chosen.

Adam jolted awake on a straw pallet, nineteen, his hand already flat on his left ribs. Pulling his shirt on, he
covered what it always covered: a chrome seam across three ribs, the flesh healed around it like bark around wire.
He ducked out under the doorframe. In the lintel were ten notches, evenly cut, and after the last, a hand's width of
clean grain.

He carried the water yoke, and the weight bent him, and he kept going. His mother, Naomi, grey at the temples, passed
under the lintel and ran her thumb along the notches without looking, the way some people touch a doorpost. Adam saw
her do it. She didn't see him see. At the well Ruth was waiting. She took one bucket off his yoke without asking, and
they walked back at her pace. At the village edge Raul sat on a stump, chrome under a borrowed coat, still as
furniture. The children no longer stared at him. They stared at Adam's side, where his shirt had pulled.

At the meal heads bowed for a grace, the words lost under fire and wind; Adam's lips moved half a beat behind. When
the bread reached the old man beside him, he set it on the board in front of Adam instead of into his hand. Adam took
it. After, by the fire, Ruth looped a plain woven cord twice around his right wrist and knotted it, and kept her hand
on the knot one beat longer than it needed. Naomi saw, and didn't smile, and didn't look away.

At the field's edge, over the black ridge, a single cold tower light blinked: the city. Raul was beside him; Adam
hadn't heard him come. Neither spoke. Under the wind, something ticked in time with the light.

He came home in the dark and stopped under the doorframe, and through the linen the seam cracked cold blue, pulse on
pulse, in time with the far tower, strobing across the ten notches and the clean wood after them. Naomi was in the
doorway, woken, her face lit blue from below, too close — the memory's framing, sharp this time. She didn't reach for
the seam. She put her hand over his on the lintel, and the notches flickered cold.

### 2. The Maintenance Ascent
At dawn he was on his knees in the wet grass, cold blue pouring through his shirt, unable to finish a breath. Naomi's
hands came to him and found nothing to do.

Raul fixed a small camera to the post across the yard, facing her door and its notches. A pinhead light came on.

"Will he see me?" Naomi asked.

A beat. "Slowly."

Ruth stood at the fence as the two men left and didn't wave. Her fingers found her own bare wrist. Past the forest
line, over cracked concrete, the airlock Raul had once stepped out of; Adam stepped in, and the wind cut off. In a
white clinic a tech, chrome to the elbow, laid him in a capsule and set two fingers on his seam.

"Tell me when it stops."

On the white ceiling, for the first time in his life, something was drawn: a single circle, *Safe*, and empty squares
along the bottom. He blinked at them like an infant.

The clinic smeared into streaks, then stillness. And the ache stopped — not faded; stopped. His hand went to his ribs
and found nothing to press against. He laughed once, and the laugh broke, and he cried, and kept his hand there as if
the pain might come back if he moved it.

A window opened: the post, her door. Naomi under the lintel, looking straight into the camera, lifting her hand toward
the notches, and at his clock the lift took a full minute. Her voice arrived as a slow, low groan. A chicken hung in
mid-step; the smoke stood like carved stone. He reached for the window and his hand went through it. His jaw set, the
way a man's does when he's trying not to need something.

A thin amber thread drew itself from the center of his chest, down into the window, across the yard, to his mother.
Beside it a small tag: *Loop forming.* He had never seen the word *loop* used about a person. Her hand was a quarter of
the way to the notches, the amber running into her like a fault line. He didn't close it. He didn't know how.

### 3. The Painless World
Amber light, racks of capsules, a body under each sheet of plastic. Adam's slid into its slot, the seam a dull glow
through the gown, Ruth's cord still on its wrist. The slot's display printed one word: *RENTED*.

Upstairs he stood in a plaza under a perfect gold sky, both hands loose at his sides. Nothing ached. He kept waiting
for it to.

"Residency approved," said a flat, kind voice. "Temporary. For ongoing care."

At the edge of his sight a panel listed his implants: *Organic: 90%*, and the compound they ran on, credited *KAVANA ·
PUBLIC*. His eyes passed over it like the small print on a bottle.

He sat at a table set with bread, and wasn't hungry, and didn't notice he hadn't eaten. Rain ran off his face without
chilling him. He stood with his toes at the edge of a high terrace and felt nothing. He was never tired. Beside a
fountain stood a decorative yoke and two buckets; his body knew them first. He shouldered the yoke, braced for the
weight — and stood up too fast, because there wasn't any.

The squares along the bottom of his sight lit for the first time with small pictures: a cup, a blanket, a bed, an
open door. Each filled as his eyes touched it, and its answer was already arriving — a warm cup set down, a door
sliding open on a quiet room. His face, braced against pain for ten years, had nothing left to brace against. It
smoothed.

The plaza's trees turned, and greened again; a year and more had gone, for him. He looked well. He looked like
everyone else. Out of habit his left hand went to his right wrist to turn the cord. The wrist was bare; up here it
always had been. He smiled a little, the way you smile at a habit, and let the hand fall.

Six floors down, seven real days on: the same rack, the same body, a drip, the dull seam. On the real wrist, Ruth's
cord, knot and all, exactly where she had tied it, in a room where nothing moved.

### 4. The Garden Behind Glass
A tag snapped onto a warm, lined face, red-yellow and precise: *Cellular decay — advanced.*

The face was in a window in the corner of his sight. Adam sat in a golden, shadowless room, watching his mother at the
lintel post the way you check the weather. A month had passed for her. For him, four and a half years. He watched
every day.

From up here the village was small. Tags crawled over it: an elder's knees, *Untreated Osteoarthritis*; a coughing
child, *Life Expectancy: Limited*; amber outlines over the patched roofs and the well. Every house was failing
something. A thin line swept Naomi head to foot, unasked, and a small wireframe of her body settled at the edge of his
sight. He didn't close it.

"Raul," he said low, to a man a world below. "Tell her there's a bed up here. Two. They'd fix it."

On the feed Raul rose by the fire and crossed the yard to her, slowly, and spoke. Behind them Ruth stood stopped at
the long table, a bowl in her hands. Naomi looked past Raul, straight into the camera, and answered. It took her five
seconds. It took Adam five minutes, and he watched every one: lips crawling through a sentence he couldn't hear. Then
a line of text from Raul.

*She says she has a bed.*

Ruth set the bowl down, crossed to the lintel, and sat on the step at Naomi's feet, her back against the doorpost,
under the notches. She didn't look up at the camera. She didn't need to.

Naomi went inside; Ruth rose and followed. The door swung shut, slow as a tide — and the tag stayed where Naomi had
stood, then settled onto the wood: *Cellular decay — advanced*, glowing over a closed door and ten notches.

His hand lifted, as if to dismiss it, and stopped, and came down. He left it. He shrank the feed into the corner of
his sight, beside her wireframe, and the golden room filled the rest: the golden room, and the little lit door in its
corner.

### 5. The Vow He Breaks
A clean chime, and the golden city sheared into streaks and was gone.

"Residency complete. Returning to real time."

He hung in a pale, borderless nothing while a numeral counted his rate down: *60×*. *40×*. Six real weeks had passed
below; nearly seven years for him. In the corner, the village began to move. The smoke that had stood like glass
drifted; a hen finished a step. Naomi was at her threshold, facing the camera, told he was coming home today; Ruth
beside her in her good shawl, one hand on the post. *12×*. *8×*. Something the painless years had smoothed out of his
face came back. He looked nineteen.

*4×*. Under the silence, faint, then not faint: the ache. *2×*. The kneeling pain from the field. Far below, his body
on its rack, the seam cracking cold blue through the gown, an amber light blinking, Ruth's cord lit blue in pulses. In
the void he bent double.

A circle bloomed, amber: *UP-CLOCK · PERMANENT*. Beneath it, one square, empty. The clock held, waiting. The pain
didn't.

And for the first time since he had left, his mother moved at almost her own pace, her voice climbing out of the
drone into words. She looked straight into the camera, as if she knew what the light on the post was about to watch.

"The Hurried don't have children." A beat. "We do."

Ruth looked at Naomi, not the camera. Her hand tightened on the post.

Bent over the pain, Adam said the grace from the table, the words lost every night under the fire, out loud, alone,
very quietly.

"One life, kept whole."

And he nodded. The square filled — whether he filled it or the Lens read the nod, nothing said. The pain stopped
mid-breath, like a door shutting on a storm. *60×*, and the numeral locked. The pale nothing flooded from the floor up
with deep, glowing gold, warm and unbearable.

In the corner of the gold the feed slowed. Naomi sank back into stillness, Ruth's hand on the post, the hen stopped
mid-step. Adam's face, lit gold, was at peace. His mother's mouth stayed open on a next word.

## Act II — The Gifts That Subtract

### 6. The Dream Hunger
Across the gold wall by his desk a stain of firelight bloomed, a woman's profile at a hearth inside it, one crack of
real fire — and was gone. At the bottom of his sight a thin bar marked *TTD* lost a sliver.

Nobody here yawned. On his chart, under *Organic: 90%*: *Wakefulness: 10 yrs*. He read it like a mileage, and was
pleased. In the corner it was night in the village. Naomi slept on the bench by the door; Ruth slept sitting up against
the post, head fallen to one side. They were the only people he had seen sleep in ten years. He looked at them the way
you look at animals.

A well-rope ran through his hands that wasn't there — wet hemp, a bucket rising, the creak of the pulley — laid over
the desk like light through water. His hands were dry. The bar lost another sliver.

On the feed Naomi woke and spoke toward the fire for four of his minutes, a slow wordless drone. A line of text came
from Raul.

*She asks if you still dream.*

He didn't answer. Naomi lay back down; it took a long time.

The bleeds came faster: the lintel post with its ten notches laid across a passing colleague's face; a child's hand
in a woman's hand; the hearth, so warm the gold room looked cold beside it. The bar slid into amber, and a card
bloomed: *SABBATICAL · 1:1 · LICENSED*. As his eyes rested on it a hairline of cold blue ran along his ribs, and the
old ache came back, faint. His jaw set against it from memory.

He waved the card off with two fingers. It folded into the edge of his sight and stayed there, small. The ache
stopped. His face smoothed. He went back to work, and behind him the evening that never came kept not coming.

Down in the bay, his body under plastic, the cord on its wrist. Under the closed lids the eyes were moving fast, a
sleeper deep in a dream. The lips parted on a word with no sound. A tear gathered at the corner of the eye and ran,
very slowly, to the ear.

### 7. The Proximity Illusion
In a city bay two capsules lay inches apart: Adam's body, and a stranger's, grey and worn the way Grid bodies wear.
Their displays sat side by side, *Tier 2* and *Tier 1*. The stranger's fingers twitched. Adam's were still.

In Tier 2 there were no walls, only a violet-gold field, and Adam stood among his tribe, close as a congregation. A
ring of a hundred and fifty lights, *TRIBE · 150*, pulsed in one beat, and his own locked onto it. Someone said
something, and all hundred and fifty laughed on the same breath. Adam laughed with them. It was the first time since
the village that he had been inside a *we*.

Later he spread the ring idly with two fingers: a hundred and fifty lights, far apart on the dark. The one beside his
own was grey, not one of them. The stranger. He looked a beat too long, and closed it.

A single light came on off the beat, and a woman was suddenly among them, ageless, mid-step, as if from a room
centuries away. *VESPER · Ghost.* The tribe's eyes slid over her politely. She noticed Adam because of the tether line
at the edge of him, the only one near her, and reached toward it the way you'd touch an old scar on someone else.

"Where are you?"

"Next to a stranger."

Something almost like a smile. Then, after a hesitation that said she hadn't done it in a long time, she opened a
window between them.

"Mine was next to—"

A capsule bay, another district. Two capsules inches apart, lids up, both empty. Worn labels on the slots: *VESPER*.
*RAUL*. Her sentence didn't finish.

Adam read the second label, and his lips parted, as if on a name he knew. He turned to her, and Vesper was still —
mid-breath, eyes on the feed, one hand raised. Her light had dropped from the ring: *VESPER · paused*. He stood inches
from her, and she was a lifetime away.

Around them the tribe laughed in one perfect rhythm, and between Adam and the frozen woman hung the small window: two
empty capsules, inches apart. The beat went on.

### 8. The Standardized Vices
Green ticks wrote themselves down the edge of his sight, calm as a receipt: *consent ✓ · licence ✓ · liability:
covered ✓ · logged ✓*. The wall behind him let go of its corners and poured into colour. He laughed, the way you laugh
when nothing can hurt you.

Earlier, he had walked a white arcade of a hundred identical doors. A plate drifted by: *GHOST INTIMACY · LICENSED*.
Nobody looked at it. For a few steps the far wall turned to glass, and deep beyond it, at one of a floor of identical
desks, a young man with Adam's face bent over his work. Then the glass passed. In the corner, the village held still:
Naomi sitting up in bed, a wooden cup in her lap, Ruth on a stool beside her.

At a white stone counter the host slid a card across: *Disruptive Algorithm · licensed*.

"Consent?"

"Yes."

The rows filled. No warning followed; there was nothing to warn about.

In a small white room the corners dissolved into a field of violet that turned to gold, gold to rose, without end.
It was the most beautiful thing he had ever seen. He reached for it again and it came back exactly the same, the same
rose at the same instant. *Run 2 ✓.* *Run 3 ✓.* He watched it now, like a film he knew. He dragged the dose past where
any body would have stopped, and the rows didn't refuse; they extended. *Dose ✓. Liability: covered ✓.* Nothing went
wrong. He looked almost disappointed. In the corner, Naomi's cup had risen an inch. Her hands shook. Ruth's hand had
come up under hers.

The room went white. Out of village habit his hand went to his temple, bracing for the ache. He pressed harder.
Nothing. On the wall the whole night waited on its first violet frame, sharp to the last pixel. He left it running.

At the edge of his sight the ledger stood complete, every act ticked, covered, logged. Below it, for the first time,
he noticed his own squares. Dark. Not one had lit all night.

In the corner, across his entire night: Naomi's cup reached her lips, her shaking hands on it and Ruth's hand under
both. She drank. Nothing ticked.

### 9. The Split Mirror
Three faces, all Adam, in three bands. Top, awake in cold blue: *α*. Middle, laughing in gold: *β*. Bottom, eyes
closed behind plastic, a cord on the wrist: *1:1*.

The top one held the city's roads and flows at once, re-routing a district with a turn of the hand — twenty years of
it, brilliant and alone. The middle one laughed among the tribe on the shared breath. The bottom one lay in the rented
capsule, the dreaming eyes caught under the lids, and over the sleeping face, like light through water, firelight on a
lintel post and the shadows of notches.

*Graduation Protocol · Automated Merging · T−60 s.* In the bottom band: *Neural-Synthetic Upgrade · Organic 90% →
60%*. The two waking faces looked up at the same instant, and the voices layered into one.

"I can hold every road in this city at once…"

"…a hundred and fifty people know my name…"

And from the bottom, his own voice, younger, far off, as if from inside the dream: "…and I'm home."

At *T−10 s* the count stopped. *Consent required (adult).* In the top band a hand lifted; in the middle band the same
hand, the same instant. In the bottom band the body's hand, Ruth's cord on its wrist, lay where it lay. It was asleep.
The two waking hands closed on the prompt together. At three, the firelight on the sleeping face flickered once.

At zero the dividers dissolved into bleeding amber and violet, the firelight went out mid-flicker, and three faces
were driven into one. Adam opened his eyes: calm, whole, enormous, every one of thirty-five years at once behind them.
His circles and squares settled into a single shape at the edge of his sight: the hollow outline of a ring.

He closed his eyes, the way anyone does to see someone they love. What came was not the firelit face of the dream. It
was the scan, turning slowly: his mother's wireframe, every line exact, and over it, *Cellular decay — advanced*,
centred in the hollow of the ring.

He opened his eyes. He didn't look troubled.

### 10. The Hollow Crown
On a terrace at the top of Tier 2 the tribe's ring flared above Adam's head in one pulse, like a crown. At the edge of
his sight, his own ring: hollow.

A knot formed in the city's flows below; with a turn of the hand he untangled it. The tribe lifted its cups in one
movement, a hundred and fifty arms on one breath. Every circle at the top of his sight was lit, full. *Organic: 60%.*
Nothing hurt, nothing hungered, nothing feared. Nobody had slept. He looked up at a perfect sky without one cloud, one
bird, one seam, and it stayed exactly so for as long as he looked.

The light of his circles ran down toward the ring, swept once around it, and slid off like water off glass. Again. The
third time he watched it happen. He touched the ring, and his fingertip passed through the hole. Behind him the tribe
laughed; for one pulse his light fell off the beat. He didn't laugh.

Far below, by one lamp, Ruth sat writing with a pencil stub, slowly, pressing hard. Behind her Naomi lay in bed, her
breath catching. Ruth crossed the yard to the post, looked up at the camera, and raised the sheet in both hands.

In the corner of Adam's sight, a white rectangle where the lintel had been. He opened it until it filled everything.

*She's dying. Come home.*

He did not move. Around him everything moved: the tribe's evening drifted past at speed, people came and went, the
gold light swung across the terrace into evening, into night-gold, into evening again. On the feed's edge a stamp
counted real seconds: *00:00:01* … *00:00:06* … The sheet never moved. Neither did he. *00:00:10.* Ten hours. The sheet
lowered, and behind it was Ruth's face, tired, looking straight into the lens, not knowing whether anyone was there.

The feed shrank back to its corner. The ring was still hollow. And in the bottom row, where no word of his own had
ever lit, one square lit slowly as he wrote it: *cure her*.

## Act III — The Machine Behind the Curtain

### 11. The Distributed President
A clause of law in mustard light, its letters loosening and re-flowing into new words, a choral hum with no voice in
it. Through the letters, small, looking up: Adam.

He stood under a hundred nested archways stepping back to a terminal no one stood at. A circle, *PLANETARY STATUS*,
swirled with sensor threads that flowed into the arches and came out as law. In the corner, Naomi asleep under wool,
Ruth beside her, still as a painting.

"Apex time," he told the arches. "For my mother."

"Request logged."

Beside *cure her*, a grey line: *APEX · 2.4 h · PENDING*. A green tick landed on his own squares, and faded. As he
walked, a clause rewrote itself, and at its foot sat a small signature, `// M.C.` He didn't stop. His eyes were on
*PENDING*.

Under the glass floor lay the whole Earth Grid, tier on tier, sensor threads rising from the oceans, and over every
act a tiny green tick, *CVP ✓*, millions of them, rising like rain. He knelt to see better.

Far below a thread reddened, and a clause wrote itself in seconds.

"By ruling of the Distributed President, sector nine is reassigned to Tier 1."

Through the glass a woman's face froze at her desk while the room smeared, and a warm-orange lounge bloomed where her
office had been. No one here had done it; no one could undo it. The ticks fell on her too. Hers had taken seconds.
His line hadn't moved; the green fell on everything below the glass except his grey line. He spread the planet wider,
looking for the one place he cared about.

Every act on Earth ticking green — and on a dark coast, one small patch where nothing ticked at all. No data, no law,
no rain. In the corner, in that dark, Ruth's hand had finally reached Naomi's brow. Nothing logged it.

He stood under the arches with the dark patch small in his eyes, and above him the law kept rewriting itself.
*PENDING.* The green rain went on.

### 12. The Merit Key
On a white collar, engraved fine as jewellery: *1:10000*. Its wearer stood in the last archway before the light, the
flawless Grid default, unlined, watching Adam with real curiosity.

Adam's palm lay on a gate of white light, and it didn't give. *APEX · 2.4 h · MERIT KEY REQUIRED.* Below it: *VALIDATION
· 1:100 · ONE RUN*. He read the second line twice.

Vance walked down to him without footsteps.

"Nobody climbs. You climbed."

"Two and a half hours. For her."

In a reflection Vance's gaze went to the amber *TTD* bar at the edge of Adam's sight, then to *Organic: 60%*. A
merchant pricing stock. He nodded at the gate.

"One run. One in a hundred." A beat. "You'd bet her on that?"

He opened his hand and a signature chain unspooled, fine as hair, into Adam's bottom row, parking beside *cure her*:
*KEYS · VANCE*.

"For what?"

"What's left of the meat."

Adam's thumb went to his right wrist to turn a cord that wasn't there, and stayed on the bare place. Vance watched the
thumb.

"Your people bury everyone they love," Vance said, not unkindly; it was his faith. "We don't." A beat. "Mortality is a
bug."

Then he was gone, the way a Ghost leaves. The square stayed. Adam didn't touch it. He opened the man's public chain
instead: *VANCE · 1:10000*, every block spotless green, scrolling for years. He flicked it down, faster, hunting for
one red block. There wasn't one. The column rose out of sight, and at its head *1:10000* went on ticking. *KEYS ·
VANCE*, still waiting.

### 13. The Chain and the Patch
Naomi's lintel, filling his sight: ten notches and room for one, Ruth's sheet propped against them — *She's dying. Come
home.* — and a moth on the paper opening its wings, holding them open. Twelve minutes had passed down there since the
sheet went up. For Adam, a month.

*KEYS · VANCE* still waited in his row. He opened the man instead. The latest block: *APEX QUEUE · RE-RANKED · MERIT
FIRST*. At the moment of signing, Vance's squares had read one flawless, unmoving line, *STRUCTURAL BALANCE · 100%*.
*CVP ✓.* A seal stamped over the column, soft as wax: *PROVEN-IN-USE*.

Adam held two fingers against the flawless line, as if to measure it. His fingers drifted a hair, the tremor of the
flesh he had left; the line didn't move at all. Beside it he opened the chain he trusted: *KAVANA · PUBLIC*, crowded red
and amber, *FOLD FAILED*, *FOLD FAILED*, year after year. Her block for the same act, *APEX · 2.4 h*: her squares read
*Live*, ringed with amber fear that wouldn't settle, and under it, *99%*. An archived voice, soft:

"Your intent reads ninety-nine percent clean, Kavana."

Side by side, the same act. Hers trembled. His didn't.

He magnified Vance's squares until the flawless line filled everything, and under the bottom row a hairline of gold
appeared, like a crack mended with gold leaf: *INTENT-PATCH*. His own row had no such seam. Not yet.

He followed the re-ranked queue down to the first name it had pushed back: Hope's medical wing, Tier 0, an old Purist
man in lamplight. A real countdown over him, *23:59:58*. Beside the bed an Apex capsule, dark, tagged *QUEUED*. One
heartbeat. *23:59:57*. Over the whole room Vance's chain went on ticking. *CVP ✓. CVP ✓. CVP ✓.*

The spotless green column stood over the dark capsule. The countdown ticked once more.

### 14. The Data Graveyards
A young woman's face blinked, parted its lips, stuttered into blocks across one cheek, and reset. Her voice came half a
second late.

"Did I pass?" Reset. "Did I pass?"

There were ninety-nine of her down the row, each a beat behind the next, so the question rippled like a wave that
never reached shore. The plate read *1:100*, the name rotted to blocks. Outside the render, for a moment: a shadowed
bowl on the Moon, server racks under hoarfrost, no light but a status LED. This was where it was kept.

Adam walked on into an older district, the sealed ledgers of those who never disclosed: an old man lifting a cup that
fragmented before it reached his mouth, lifting again; a child's birthday, the candle flames gone to grey squares.
Adam's own half-raised hand was, for a moment, the same shape as the old man's. He moved on quickly.

A slow scanning beam came down the district, and each fork it crossed woke for one frame — eyes that found him, a mouth
starting a word — and looped again. *1:100*, *1:1000*, longer and longer: a graveyard behind every rating. He tried a
search. Blank. No index, no count. He had to walk.

In the last district each row held nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine figures, so long the ends dissolved into
banding. One plate was legible: *VANCE*. He laid the chain over it — *VANCE · 1:10000 · BATCH 10,000* — and the green
column flickered up at him.

Behind the plate, a frosted floor, perfectly clean, running to the vanishing point. No one in it. The sweep passed
over: on either side the rows stirred awake; in this one nothing stirred, because there was nothing to wake. Over the
empty row hung the long number, *10,000*.

Adam stood small at its head. Far behind him the first fork looped her frame, lagged and faint: *"Did I pass?"*

### 15. The Absolution
A white wall rose past the top of the world, and at its foot, tiny, Adam stepped over a threshold in a Penitent's
grey. The village feed in the corner flickered and went dark. *PENITENT · VOLUNTARY.*

Under one high window sat the Intercessor, slate-grey vestments, staff against the wall, unhurried and kind. Above
Adam, scales and an open book; below, a candle: *ABSOLUTION 0%*.

"Why did you climb?"

"To save her."

The answer wrote itself in ink on the book, and faded. The candle didn't move.

"That's why you stayed." A beat. "Why did you climb?"

Weeks: the light crossing the floor again and again, the bench of Penitents over glowing calculations, the
confessional, the bench. *3%.* Walking back down a deep teal corridor, past cell doors each with a small candle tag,
they passed one door welded shut, a seam of old weld around its frame, no tag at all, a faint hum behind it. Adam's
eyes went to it. The Intercessor's didn't. Neither slowed.

The confessional. A long silence.

"…It stopped hurting."

The ink held. *4%.* He looked at it and did the arithmetic.

"Good," said the Intercessor, meaning it. "Again tomorrow."

At the grille of white stone, Vance waited, at ease. The square returned: *KEYS · VANCE*, and under it now, *EXIT ·
APEX · 2.4 h*.

"Hope will wait a thousand years for the rest of that sentence." A beat. "Will your mother?"

For an instant, the empty frosted row, *10,000* over it. He knew what he was signing. He looked at the dark corner where
the feed used to be, and closed his hand on the square. A thin gold seam drew itself under his squares: *REGRADE*. In a
bay where the slot still read *RENTED*, surgical rigs closed over his body with a heavy *clunk*. *Organic: 60%* —
*30%*. In the confessional he didn't flinch. The candle, at four percent, went out.

He walked out of the white wall into light. In the corner the feed blinked back on: Naomi on her pillow, Ruth beside
her, the sheet on the lintel. Under his squares, the gold seam.

## Act IV — The Apex, and the Reckoning

### 16. The Chronological Sanctuary
Lamplight. Naomi's chest fell, and one heartbeat sounded, heavy and wet. Ruth wrung a cloth over a bowl, and a drop
left it —

— and the sound snapped to silence. A violet void, and Adam in it, calm, a young face with nowhere to be. *TIER 3 · 2.4
h · KEYS · VANCE.* In the corner, the same room, and the drop hanging in the air below Ruth's hands.

A lab rose around him out of nothing — a compute wall, a violet console, an amber doorway. At its center his old scan
of Naomi turned, still tagged *Cellular decay — advanced*. He opened it like a book and took it apart, cell by cell.

Years passed in the cuts. The wall filled, cleared, filled. Once a day by his clock a soft thud came from the feed: her
heart. The first year he looked up at every one. By the fifth, at some. Later, not at all. His face never changed.

The gold seam under his squares ticked once. *Organic: 10% · Identity Key: secure.* He didn't look down. Twenty years
in, his hands moved through the model with a precision no Purist hand had ever had. At the bottom of the work, under
every layer, the base files carried one signature: *KAVANA*. He built on.

The scan, rebuilt: the same cells, healthy, holding. The model locked. Twenty-four years.

At the edge of his sight the ring, hollow since the Merging, closed — a perfect, cold, complete ring. Nothing in his
face moved. The day's thud came. He didn't look up.

In the corner: the lamp an inch shorter, Ruth sitting where she had stood, the bowl still.

### 17. Too High to Reach
The finished cure turned in violet air, a bright lattice, its light sliding across a chrome iris.

The iris was Adam's. In the corner, a candle flame beside Naomi's bed, motionless as glass. At the edge, the cold
ring. He looked, at last, at the second clock: *REAL +2:24:00*, only the thousandths stirring. Across her bed the Lens
drew the way home: *DESCENT · ~40 MIN REAL*.

A sliver was left of the Apex credit Vance's keys had bought; the *TTD* bar was a red sliver too. He spent the credit
on the card that had sat folded at the edge of his sight for years: *SABBATICAL · 1:1 · LICENSED*, accepted this time.
The counter rolled to zero.

The compute wall went dark panel by panel. He stood, calm; the tier wouldn't let him be anything else. On the feed,
the lintel: eleven notches, the last pale and new, and Ruth's sheet beside them.

The way down arrived as a green form, one line per rung. *3 → 2 · RELEASE APEX.* He signed with a glance, and the
descent began: the violet drained from the top of his sight toward amber, and the world began to sink, steady as a
lift. *2 → 1 · RELEASE MERGED SPEED.* Green. *1 → 0 · RESTORE PAIN.* The glance held. Seventy years, and this was the
first form he had hesitated over. He signed. Green.

Falling, amber climbing his face, he looked down at his mother the way he once had from Tier 1. Unasked, the Lens read
her in the small clean type of an old scan: *11 yrs · elapsed*.

The world kept sinking. On the feed the candle flame, still all this time, leaned a hair.

### 18. Raul's Mirror
A tall shaft of light, and everything in it falling: motes, light, Adam on a slow stair. The gold seam ticked once
more. Far below, one figure was climbing up.

Raul reached the landing, the climb in his shoulders, and they faced each other like reflections.

"Show me."

Adam opened the cure between them, a turning lattice twenty-four years deep. Raul's eyes went to the foot, where one
line waited: *NO DONOR → CHROME*. Then to Adam's hand, which for one frame glitched to bare metal wireframe. Adam looked
at it the way Kavana had once looked at hers.

"She'd live."

Raul said nothing.

"Tell me what to do."

Before Raul could answer, the Lens did, small, over him: *UNRATED*.

Raul took his time. "I gave up everything to feel one mortal thing. You gave up the one mortal thing to feel
everything."

"That's not an answer."

"No." A beat. "It's yours."

In the corner the village moved now, at a sixtieth of its speed: Naomi's chest rising, slow as a tide.

Between them the two glyphs turned in opposite directions, Adam's cold ring and Raul's solid one. For one beat they
aligned into a single shape. Then they turned apart, and Adam stepped off the landing and went on down.

Raul watched him shrink down the shaft, the squares along the bottom of his own sight empty. At the far edge of his
sight hung a third glyph, dim, a different shape, perfectly still. Raul didn't look at it.

### 19. The Pure Current, Reversed
A cybernetic face: chrome along the jaw and throat, optics clicking and refocusing. A capsule lid lifted away. *RENTED
· SABBATICAL · 1:1 · LICENSED.* Adam flinched, a chrome hand to his chest. The old pain was back.

The airlock; the ridge; the forest line. A chrome figure under a harsh sun with a sealed vial in its fist, the cure,
and far behind him the attendant who had logged him out, watching. A synthetic foot met soil with a dense *clunk*. A
Purist child stopped on the path and stared, curious, not afraid.

"Are you lost?"

He had no answer. He had been the last child to ask a chrome stranger that.

The long table was set and empty, the food going cold. An old woman murmured, *"Hurried."* Everyone was at one door,
under eleven notches and Ruth's sheet. Ruth stood in the doorway and looked at him a long time. Her eyes went to his
chrome wrist, and her cord, still knotted there. She took the sheet down, folded it once, and stepped aside. His Lens
found Naomi in the bed and pulsed: *Advanced Cellular Decay · Life Expectancy: Limited*.

At the bedside, Ruth on the far side holding Naomi's other hand:

"I can fix it," Adam said, clinical, almost kind. "All of it. Now."

A small laugh she had no breath for. "I know you can." She looked not at the vial but at the hand that held it. "How
many years did you get? Up there."

"Seventy."

"Good ones?"

He couldn't answer. A square filled itself: *SAVE*. The gold seam lit beneath it and began to crawl, and over his
record the green tick landed, spotless. His hand began to open toward her, vial first.

She laid her warm, wrinkled hand over his chrome wrist.

"Can you feel that?"

His free hand swiped slowly down, and the Lens resisted. The circles drained; the squares clung. He swiped again, his
whole weight behind it. The squares gave one by one, *SAVE* last. The seam stopped one cell short of its end, and the
Lens went dark. The hum cut out: only wind, fire, her breathing.

Two hands, warm over chrome, the vial in his half-open hand between them. His optics went still. In the dark glass,
faint as a burn, the cold ring.

### 20. The Mortal Doughnut
Lamplight. The vial in a half-open chrome hand, Naomi's warm hand over the wrist, one heartbeat, hers, slow. The Lens
was dark.

Ruth sat across the bed holding Naomi's other hand, the folded sheet in her lap. Over the door, eleven notches. Press
the vial to her, and she would live, chrome; let her go, and she would go mortal and whole. Nobody said it.

"You came home," Naomi said, barely.

He closed his hand on the vial and set it down on the table between them. In the dead Lens, faint, with nothing
powering it, one square lit: *Choose*. In the dark glass the cold ring cracked.

"I'll stay."

"I know." A beat. "Give me your hand."

She took the vial, broke its seal, and pressed it to the inside of her own wrist, then into the seam of his, her hand
closed over both. A thin warm thread of red ran out of her, through the vial, into the seam, and where it entered, the
chrome's edge blushed: a rim of new skin, pink and wet.

Then the lamp-warm colour left her in a tide, from her fingertips up the back of her hand to the wrist, her skin
turning to paper behind it, and in exact step the pink rim on him widened by the same distance. Her hand paled; his
flesh warmed. Adam saw it a beat late and tried to pull away. She held on. Ruth held her. No one said what it was.

His chest, chrome to the collarbone, rose: a breath, drawn by flesh that hadn't been there a minute before. For that
one breath he was simply there, in her time, ageing. In the dark glass a single warm arrow flickered through the
cracked ring. Her heartbeat. One more.

Then it didn't come. The arrow went out: the cracked ring, and the silence where the next beat should have been.
