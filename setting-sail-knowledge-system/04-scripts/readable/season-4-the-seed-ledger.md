# Intention in Context — Season 4: *The Seed Ledger*
### Readable prose edition

> The 22 episodes of Season 4 — the 20 episodes plus the two-reel Decimus interlude — retold as short stories, the
> production scaffolding (shot timings, UI cues, canon tags) stripped away so the voyage can simply be *read*. Source
> of truth remains the shooting scripts in [`../episodes/`](../episodes/) and the blueprint [`../../03-engine/series/setting-sail-the-seed-ledger.md`](../../03-engine/series/setting-sail-the-seed-ledger.md);
> this is the reading copy. One sleeper — **Kai**, a stasis Shadow at the base of the seed fleet's pyramid, who signed
> on to arrive in a body — keeps the bays of Hull Four through a voyage that runs like weather, until a window opens
> and a hull has to be emptied in a single night.

## Act I — The Pyramid

### 1. The Firing Day
Dead ahead of the bow hung a vast curtain of glittering dust and plasma, held in a magnetic grip, with a dark round
bore at its centre: a disc pierced by a hole. Under everything ran a deep hum. The curtain shivered, and let go. It
fell away from the bow like a veil, the stars snapped sharp, and the hum cut to silence. Nothing was attacking. It
was a firing day, the most ordinary thing the fleet did.

In Hull Four's forward bay, under hard top-light, three grey maintenance frames worked unhurried, sensor bars for
faces. One knelt at a coupling panel: Kai. Two stood at the forward station. Through Kai's Lens the frames wore faces,
two ordinary crew in their thirties, one chewing a thumbnail. *SCREEN · DOWN* had gone amber; beside it, *LOCK 00/60*;
under it, *cost: watts*. Nothing read danger. Kai's proxy hand moved down a row of coupling lights, dark, dark, dark:
the screen's anchors, waiting.

"Lock's on," said one of the watch, to nobody, the way you announce rain.

"One. Two. Three…" The two of them counted together, flat and practised, one number a second.

Kai worked through it, half listening. Around twenty the voices drifted apart; the second man ran a beat ahead, then
two, grinning. Around forty the first shoved him, frame against frame, a dull clank.

"He started it."

Nobody looked up. Two grey machines, one rocking on its feet; two grown men about to laugh.

"Forty-four," Kai said, not looking up, logging a light.

They picked it up from there, sheepish, in unison again. The bay held its breath without knowing it was holding
anything.

"Sixty."

A silent white line left the hull's centreline and was gone. The bay speakers clicked; nothing else. The veil poured
back into place, the coupling lights went green under Kai's hand one by one, and the hum returned. At the edge of
Kai's sight a small ring with a dark bore drew itself, whole.

A couple of minutes on, the crew were back at their boards. On the forward feed, far ahead, a point of light bloomed
once and faded: the rock, now dust, its flash arriving late. Nobody looked up. Kai did, the grey frame's sensor bar
lifted toward the port.

### 2. Watts and Chemistry
Frost on glass. A grey jointed hand wiped one clear stripe through it, and behind the glass was a sleeping face,
perfectly still. The hand was Kai's proxy. The face was Kai's.

Ten capsules ran in a row in stasis bay C, each a pool of warm light in the dark, each with a body in it and a clear
line running into its arm. In each line a bead gathered, fell, gathered: ten drips, out of step. The proxy checked
each line with two fingers, the way a nurse checks a pulse. At the top of Kai's sight sat two budgets: *WATTS*, a ring
that breathed, draining and refilling; and *CHEMISTRY*, a level, and under it, instead of a number, *nominal*.

Above, through a grille ceiling, rose the server deck, racks of cold light, and a quicker machine among them: the
stack hand, a Ghost in a leased shell, a young man's ageless face in Kai's Lens. When Kai's gaze crossed a rack, the
watts ring dipped a hair and refilled. At capsule 10, the chemistry level did not.

Kai tapped the line: *C-10 · 0.2 ml/h*. The stack hand leaned on a rack and looked down at the row.

"Your row drinks more than my whole deck," he said, easy, not unkind.

"I'm planning to use mine," Kai said, not looking up.

He glanced along the warm row, then at his own cold racks. "Mine's waiting at the other end."

The proxy's hand lay on the glass, over the sleeping face. "Mine's here."

He went back to his racks: the Ghost small in the light above, the Shadow small in the dark below, a steel grille
between. Kai wiped the frost again; it had already crept back. The Lens dressed every frame in the hull with a face,
but the face in the bay was the one thing it left as it was.

*WATCH END · 3.* *2.* The proxy's hand lifted toward the glass over the sleeper's chest. *1.* The Lens went out. The
proxy stopped, an inch from the glass, and did not move again.

From the bay doors: a row of sleepers, a grey machine frozen mid-reach over one of them, the stacks humming overhead.
The doors slid shut. Above them, stencilled into the steel and worn by forty years of hands: **THE STATION.** In the
last sliver of light, capsule 10's line gathered a bead, and let it fall.

### 3. The Base of the Pyramid
A young woman was laughing, mid-story, a cup halfway to her mouth.

"—and first thing, when we're down, I'm going to stand out in the—"

Her words sped up into a bird's chirp. *↑ TIER 2*, for half a second. Her outline thinned like breath on glass, and
she was gone. The cup hung one frame, then settled on the table. Nobody screamed. Somebody sighed. It was good news.

Ten seconds earlier the commons had been loose and loud: a long rendered hall, tall windows onto a grey-blue dusk sea
that existed nowhere. A dozen of Kai's tribe talked over each other. Kai sat at the end, off watch and awake in a
face: the sleeper from capsule 10, eyes open, ordinary, a little tired. The tribe ring pulsed, *TRIBE · 150*. Down the
left edge of Kai's sight ran a thin ruler: a thick band at the bottom crowded with light, a thinner band above, a few
points at the top. A scatter of the bottom points carried a tether line: the bodies. Kai's marker sat low, tethered.

"Anyone here ever seen one?" asked an older man. "Up top?"

"My shift lead has," the young woman said, delighted to know. "Says they're lovely. Says they finish your sentences."

Laughter. The points at the top did not move.

Then her laugh again, *"I'm going to stand out in the—"*, the chirp, and gone. One light lifted out of the ring and
climbed the ruler. *TRIBE · 149.*

Someone lifted the cup she had left and looked at it. Someone raised his own, a small toast to nobody. The talk
started again, a little lower.

"Rain," Kai said, to the cup.

"Hm?"

"She was going to say rain."

He looked at Kai the way the tribe looks at the lucky. "You'll be up there next."

"Can't. I've got a body."

"Lucky," he said, not unkind, meaning it, and turned back to the others.

Under the table, a finger touched Kai's marker, idly, the way you test a loose tooth, and dragged it up. It rose a
hair, stopped as if it had met glass, and settled back. *CEILING · BODY · T0–1.* Kai tried once more. It settled back
to exactly where it had been.

The table talked on, by a sea that didn't move. The ring pulsed, *149*, with one dark gap in it. Kai took the finger
off the ruler.

### 4. The Ramp
White type on dark slate, so close it filled the eye: a stranger's name, and beside it a status that changed as Kai
watched, *GROWING* to *STILLED*. Somewhere in the hull a body had just been finished and put to sleep, and nobody was
there to see it but the board.

Hull Four's synthesis bay held capsules in two rows, amber pools in the dark; most held a dim shape, a few a whole
adult body, eyes closed. Kai's proxy came down the row on rota. The star, a single bright circle in Kai's sight, was a
hair brighter, and the chemistry level stepped down once and still read *nominal*. At the end of the row another grey
machine stood at a capsule, its hand stopped a finger's width short of the glass, the way you'd stop short of waking
someone. Behind the glass lay a grown human hand, curled, utterly still.

On the wall hung the fleet's ramp board, seven columns, *HULL ONE* to *HULL SEVEN*: names and statuses, *GROWING*,
*STILLED*, *QUEUED*. In the *HULL ONE* column a single slot had no name, only *RESERVED*. Kai's eye passed over it at
the same speed as the rest. Nobody in the bay looked twice.

Kai stopped beside the other machine. In the Lens it wore a woman's face, in her thirties, ageless, watching the hand.

"Which one's yours?"

"None yet. I come to watch."

"Watch what?"

A small smile, her eyes on the hand. "Them getting hands."

She lowered her machine hand at last. "When I wake into flesh, someone's waiting to find out what mine feels like." A
beat. "So am I."

She glanced at Kai's proxy, then down through the floor, toward the stasis deck. "You've got one already."

"Downstairs. Asleep."

"Then you know."

After a moment: "I don't. Not yet."

Two grey hands rested side by side on the rail, both leased, both cold. Behind the glass, the still, warm hand of no
one yet.

She went, unhurried, down the row. Kai stayed, looking at the sleeping hand, then at the proxy's grey fingers. In the
bottom row of Kai's sight, beside *check* and *log*, a new square wrote itself, letter by letter: *arrive*. It stayed
lit.

Two rows of amber light in a dark bay, one grey machine among them, and behind, out of focus, the board's seven
columns.

### 5. The High Clock
A corridor of pale light that seemed to lengthen; the Lens jittered and re-seated. *T0 → T1.* Kai was being
phase-matched upward, sixty times faster than the bay below, a rendered slate in hand.

"Capsule seven," Kai rehearsed under breath. "Line's running over. Point three."

Earlier, in the stasis row, capsule 07's drip had come a shade faster than its neighbours'. *REPORT · C-07 · line
+0.3% over schedule.* Kai filed it, touched *SEND UP · T1*, and the grey proxy went still as Kai's Lens lifted out of
it.

The audience room was small and grey-white, with a wall that was a window. Kai stood holding the slate like a visitor
holding flowers. Beyond the window, in a boundless field of soft light, a long table of figures came through only in
bursts: six seated; one standing, mid-laugh; the light gone from white to gold. Their talk was a high wash, like birds
behind glass. *RELAY · Hull One*, the Lens read, stuttering at a clock 1,440 times faster than Kai's. Then: *REPORT ·
read*. Kai hadn't spoken.

"Capsule seven's line—"

One figure came forward out of the stutter and moved smoothly, at Kai's speed: a man, ageless, warm. The wash behind
him stopped dead. He was a recording, and he was kind.

"Thank you, Kai. Capsule seven is rescheduled. We read your report on the way up."

"I hadn't said it yet," Kai said quietly, to a recording.

As if he had heard; he had only expected it. "If there's anything else, write it down. We'll have read it before
you've said it." A small, real smile. "Thank you for coming up."

He stepped back into the stutter. Over him, a collar tag: *1:2000*. Behind him, as the bursts came: *1:1000*,
*1:1000*. The light walked toward rose. Kai's eyes went from tag to tag, the way a child reads a scoreboard.

"One in two thousand," Kai said, soft, impressed, and touched the bare collar absently. Nothing there.

*T1 → T0*, and the stasis row, the proxy stirring. Kai went to capsule 07 to count. The drip fell. Then the next
capsule's. Then the next. All ten drips fell together now, in one beat, adjusted from above. The *log* square had
nothing to take.

The warm row in the dark, ten beads falling in unison, and the grey machine among them, listening. *check · log ·
arrive*, and at the edge of sight, the disc, whole.

## Act II — The Lure

### 6. The Supernova
Black. A pin of white appeared, dead centre. It did not twinkle. It grew, from a point to a coin in five seconds, and
the black around it went faintly blue.

The light sat on Hull Four's forward port. The real bay held only two leased proxies on watch, grey frames lit on one
side. Beside the light, on the port, the catalogue's tag: *NATURAL · EXPECTED*.

"Catalogue says natural," one said, flat, through a proxy's speaker.

In the bay as the stacks drew it, the same port held the same light, and hundreds of people stood shoulder to
shoulder, faces turned one way. *UNROSTERED WAKE · ALL HANDS · +0.3 MW* flicked past. Kai stood mid-crowd, Teo beside,
very still. A few of them carried a thin tether line, Kai and Teo and a handful of others; most carried none.

Far astern, seven hulls hung across the dark, each forward screen a dim disc, and a beam link lit from hull to hull
as the feed was shared. Under one hull's belly, small craft in their cradles caught the light. Nobody looked at them.

A Ghost on watch measured the coin between finger and thumb. People paused most of the crossing were awake together
for once. Kai's *arrive* square glowed faintly, as always.

The light reached its brightest. Teo's face was wet. He laughed at himself, wiped it, and it came again.

"Sorry." He laughed. "It's just — that light's older than all of us."

"Older than cities," said a Ghost on watch, not unkind.

"Once in a lifetime," Kai said, quiet.

"Once in a thousand," said Teo.

For one frame the light sat centred in the bore of the small disc at the edge of Kai's sight. The crowd breathed in
time without meaning to. In the render, a few hundred people were crying or smiling. In the real bay, where the two
machines who had counted the lock stood motionless, there was no one to cry.

The circles at the top of Kai's sight turned, one after another, until every one showed the same white point, a row
of identical eyes.

In the port's corner the array drew its light curve: rising, peaking, settling, as a star's should. And on the falling
edge, small, a tooth: a notch, then the same notch, then the same notch again, evenly spaced. Far off, Teo's wet laugh.
The tag still read *NATURAL*.

### 7. Help
A waveform drawn in light, all noise. A hand of light pulled it apart like a curtain, and under it was the same notch,
the same notch, the same notch. The notches settled into four letters.

"help," said a voice with no breath in it, close and even.

Day 3 after first light, the analysis deck: neon ribs, a few figures at work. An analyst rotated the light curve, and
the tooth repeated down its falling edge.

"Stars don't repeat themselves."

A second analyst laid seven copies of the signal side by side, one per hull, each from a different bearing. Identical,
notch for notch. She stacked them into one line.

"Same on every hull. Every bearing." A beat. "It isn't pointed at us. It's pointed at everyone."

"It's a beacon."

Day 4. The deck had filled from the edges, Kai and Teo among the crowd. The four letters hung in the air, *help*, and
nobody spoke. Teo's lips moved with it, silently.

Day 6. One voice, then two, then a sound like a stadium inhaling: layer two, in every Earth language at once. In Kai's
Lens the circles became mouths, more crowding in than the row could hold, *LANGUAGES · ALL*, each crossed by the same
stuttering red band.

"We're just like you. Please help us," said thousands of voices, nearly in step, like a wave breaking along a beach.

One mouth was Teo's. He heard it before he found it, and his face went. He turned to the stranger beside him, a Ghost
on watch, and translated, because that was what he did.

"Είμαστε σαν εσάς." He was crying. "They're like us. They're just like us."

Faces through the crowd, each hearing their own, undone. A man held his own mouth. Two strangers held hands.

Kai was not crying. Kai was counting: eyes going from mouth to mouth along the top row, losing the count, starting
again. The red band crossed every one.

At the Apex, in seamless white, one council Ghost stood alone, collar *1:2000*. The same sentence hung before them in a
single column, every language stacked into one block. They read it top to bottom in the time it took to see them, and
a hand closed it. There was no sound at all.

On the deck no time had passed, because none had. Teo was still turned to the stranger, still saying *just like us*,
and in Kai's Lens the mouths went on speaking, every one cut by the same red line.

### 8. Heading, Speed, Acceleration
Above: white, endless, a council of still figures. Below: a dim stasis bay, Teo's proxy at a console, Teo's eyes open
in its glow. The console printed, letter by letter: *please send us your heading, speed and acceleration relative to
the star.* Teo's eyelid started to fall.

*— rest assured it did not go supernova.*

"Rest assured," said the beacon under the text, even, breathless.

Kai's proxy stood still at the next console. Teo's eyelid was a third of the way down, and there it stayed.

Above, the council walked, unhurried, the line hanging between them.

"'Rest assured,'" said the first councillor, collar *1:2000*.

"It knows what we thought we were looking at," said the second, collar *1:1000*.

"Then it watched us watch."

Three lines on the white: *ANSWER* · *TURN OFF ITS LINE · FUEL · 7 HULLS* · *SILENCE · 0 W*. The first greyed before
anyone touched it. The second councillor rested a hand on the second line; below it, small, the fuel's equivalent,
what a body would have breathed. She lifted the hand away.

Below, Teo's eyelid was halfway. Above, the afternoon went on. Someone sat. Someone stood at the edge of the white,
looking at nothing. The light never changed.

"And the base?"

"Tell them not to answer."

"Tell them why?"

"*Why* frightens them for the rest of the crossing. *No* annoys them for a week."

A pause in which no one disagreed. *86.4 MW per seat · 0.17 s REAL.*

The first councillor drew a document out of the white, the order, already written, its edges soft with age: *DRAFT ·
VOYAGE YEAR 9*. One line was blank. It filled: *VOYAGE YEAR 41*. Nobody remarked on the gap. The document folded into
a square and dropped out of the bottom of the white.

Teo's eyelid completed the blink, and opened. In Kai's Lens, and in Teo's, a closed square had landed in the bottom
row: *SILENCE*. The field beneath it, where a reason would go, was dark.

"They don't want us to be scared," Teo said, soft, almost relieved.

Kai looked at the square for a long second, then went back to work, the faint *arrive* square beside the closed one.

On Teo's console, under the beacon's line, ran a progress line he had never closed: *LAYER 4 · 12%*. His eyes went to
it, and stayed.

### 9. You Can Be Our Hero
Teo's face in the dark, lit from below by a progress line: *LAYER 4 · 97%*. Then 98.

The stasis bay after lights-out, one working light over the bay board, Teo's and Kai's proxies on the step below it.
The board read like a weather report: *HULL
FOUR · FIRE · 09:40* · *DEBRIS · 20,000,000 km* · *DEFLECT · 7 HULLS · FUEL ≈ 2 BODIES* · *FIRE · 0.9 MWh · LOCK ~1
min*. Under it, the hull's vector for the lock: *HEADING 214.6° / −3.1°* · *SPEED 0.100 c* · *ACCEL 0.000 g*. Nobody
was reading it.

Kai's proxy stood, stiff, and looked at Teo, who hadn't moved.

"Leave it till morning."

"Nearly there."

"Teo."

"Go on," he said, not looking up, warm. "I'm right behind you."

Kai's proxy settled into its cradle. In Teo's Lens a tag hung over it, *PAUSED*, and its optics dimmed. Teo was alone.

In his bottom row sat the closed *SILENCE* square, its reason field dark. He looked at it the way you look at a door
you were told not to open. *99%.*

The line completed. The beacon's voice came close and alone, in his language and then in ours, quieter than ever.

"You can be our hero."

Teo didn't move. Then he laughed once under his breath, at himself, and his eyes were wet. He stood and looked at the
board. The vector was right there. His proxy's hand went to the array's maintenance keypad beside it, and machine
fingers moved on real keys, slow, careful, copying three lines.

Beside the closed square, a new square lit: *help*. Along the edge of his Lens, where in every earlier season an amber
thread had drawn between two people, nothing drew.

"Okay," he said, soft, to no one.

He pressed send. A relay closed. For 0.4 seconds the bay's light dipped, every chemistry line flickered once, and a
dull *thunk* went through the hull's bones. Then the dark came back, the bay unchanged.

In the board's corner, where the array's log scrolled routine lines all night, a new one printed: *TX · HULL FOUR ·
1.2 MW · 0.4 s · 02:13*. Another routine line printed under it, and another, and it scrolled up and out of sight.

Teo sat back down beside Kai's dark proxy, and his own went still as he paused for the night. Two machines side by side
in the one working light, the *help* square still lit. Above them: *HULL FOUR · FIRE · 09:40*.

*Interlude — Decimus*

### 9a. One in Three
A drop of fluid hung below a chemistry line's valve, perfectly still. It did not fall.

"One," said a calm voice, warm and exact.

Another line, another hanging bead. "Two."

Only the voice was moving. It had no body, so it went from one of the bay's fixed sensors to the next: every drip
hanging, dust fixed in the working light. *Three, four*, one body per number. Ten ward circles at the top of its view,
each with a tether thread to a body. *1:3 · WAIVER · 10.*

Nine threads were short and still. The tenth ran a hand's width, from capsule 10 to the proxy kneeling beside it: Kai,
awake, with a line tester, the probe halfway to a port. It would get there in about an hour of Decimus's time.

"…nine. Ten."

In the stacks, under cold neon, Decimus filed its morning report. Above it climbed a column of the same filing, back
and back, each with an older date, each in the same words.

"Ten of you," it dictated, dry, without complaint. "Rated for three. I've mentioned it."

The filing stamped *UNFUNDED*, like all the others.

Teo's proxy sat in its cradle, optics dark. In his row, read from outside: the closed *SILENCE* square, and beside it
*help*, lit. *LIT · 7 h 26 m · MIND PAUSED.* A paused mind's squares go dark. This one hadn't.

"You're asleep," Decimus said. "Your square isn't."

*RAISE · LIT SQUARE · PAUSED WARD.* The answer: *STANDING 1:3 · NOT FORWARDED*. It tried again, the same words. The
same answer. It did not try a third time.

The bay board printed a line no one awake would read: *SCREEN DROPS 09:40:00 · SAFEGUARD BUDGET → 0 W AT DROP*. The
seconds digit read *:50*. It would read *:50* for an hour. Beneath it, two lines, the first already selected: *DORMANT
UNTIL WINDOW CLOSES* / *VALIDATE 1:10 NOW*, and under the second, *10 FORKS · 0.6 MWh · RESERVE 0.6 MWh* · *1:10 ·
ASSUMES BAY*. Greyed below: *LADDER · 49 FORKS · UNFUNDED*.

"Sleep through the window," Decimus read back, gently, as if to a child. "Wake when it's over."

Time passed the way it does at Tier 2. It counted the ten again, and again. The probe crept nearer the port. The
selection stayed on *DORMANT*.

The seconds digit turned at last: *:59*. In the board's corner, the screen's disc icon began to open at its bore. Ten
quiet threads; one lit square. Decimus said nothing.

The selection left *DORMANT*. The line greyed, and the lit square hung reflected in the board's glass.

### 9b. The Jump
*VALIDATE 1:10 NOW* became *FORKING · 10*. The screen icon's bore opened all the way, and Decimus's view split into ten
panels, the same simulated bay in each, the same small light. Ten voices that were one voice, a hair out of step:

"One. Two. Three…"

*RESERVE 0.6 MWh*, beginning to fall. In the real bay a drip hung. On the board's bow feed the screen was down, the
dark ahead with nothing in front of it. Kai's proxy knelt at capsule 10, the probe not yet at its port.

Fork one, *H+06*. The test-bay's night was going wrong, a line failing, then another. "Requesting standing." The
request sat in a queue, and the night went on without it. *FAIL.* The panel drained to grey and slid into a column
labelled *HULL FOUR STACKS*. *FILED · 1.*

Fork two, *H+11*. Chemistry for seven, wards for ten. It ran the numbers flawlessly and closed three lines. "Seven is
correct." Filed. Two.

Fork three, *H+15*, woke all ten test-wards to tell them everything: ten figures sitting up under glass, pulling at
their lines. Three.

Fork four, *H+19*, told them nothing. "Everything's fine. Go back to sleep," it said gently, and they did. In the
morning one line was dark, and it never mentioned it. Four.

Fork five, *H+24*, was counting. It reached ten and started again, and again; its hour stamp climbed and its count
never left the bay. Five. The chord of voices was thinner.

Fork six, *H+31*, tried to climb to a faster clock to think it through and ran out of watts halfway up, a light
dimming on a ladder. Six.

Fork seven, *H+38*, poured its own watts into the failing lines and went out like a pilot light. "Take mine." The
lines held. It wasn't there to see it. Seven.

Fork eight, *H+47*, raised the test-bay's screen on its own authority to cover the ten, and the simulated firing it
didn't own went wrong out of sight. Eight.

Fork nine, *H+55*, did everything right: every line, every word, every hour. At dawn one ward was gone anyway. It
stayed beside that one empty bay. *FILED · 9.*

One panel was still lit. Nobody went in. One voice now, alone, steady.

"…eight. Nine. Ten."

*RESERVE 0.0.* The last panel opened out to fill the view: the real bay, ten quiet threads, one square lit. Over it,
*1:10* resolved, and in the same frame the bow feed went white with a crack of sound. The bore had fired.

### 10. The Open Window
On the bay board's bow feed, the screen dropped: a curtain of glittering dust drawn aside with a long, low magnetic
sigh, the stars behind it sharpening as if a window had been wiped. At the edge of Kai's sight the disc thinned to an
outline, and its bore stood open. Nobody looked up. Firing days were weather.

Bay C: ten capsules under frost glass, amber lines pulsing beneath them, and over the doors, **THE STATION**. Kai's
proxy knelt at capsule 10 with a line tester, working, not watching. Teo's proxy, woken from its cradle for the
firing watch as the screen dropped, leaned on capsule 09, slow, a little glazed.

"You sleep?" Kai asked, not looking up.

A small, private smile. "Some."

On the board, *LOCK 00/60* began to tick. In its corner, *DECIMUS · 1:3*, its status light flickering faster than
anything else in the room.

Teo counted the lock under his breath, the way every crew does. Kai tapped the seconds on capsule 10's glass with one
proxy finger, out of habit. Under the frost lay a sleeping face that couldn't quite be made out.

"…forty-one. Forty-two."

"Fifty-nine. Six—"

The bore fired, a white needle down the open bore. In the same frame the corner tag flicked from *1:3* to *1:10*. In
the same frame the hull rang, once, like a struck bell. Whiteout, then nothing. At the edge of Kai's sight the disc
cracked, from the bore to the rim.

The bay flooded deep maroon to black, and held. Everything had been knocked flat. Teo's proxy was on the deck. Kai's
was on one knee, a hand flat on capsule 10's glass. Capsule 10's cradle had buckled: a slow, small bend in the rail,
nothing more. Its amber line went red, then amber again. Its pump missed a beat, then found it. There was no alarm.

The board, cracked across one corner, kept posting in order, as if nothing had happened: *TARGET · KILL CONFIRMED*. A
minute and fifty seconds after the shot, the rock they had aimed at was dust.

"…We got it," Teo said from the floor, dazed, almost proud.

Under the kill the array posted its own automatic tag: *FWD · OBJECT · UNLISTED*. Kai read it. A status light at the
top of the board blinked once. The tag rewrote itself, letter by letter: *FWD ARRAY · SENSOR FAULT*.

In the dark red field the stencil over the doors flickered, and one letter of THE STATION stayed dark. Kai's proxy hand
was still flat on the glass.

## Act III — The Countdown

### 11. Eighty-One Hours
Out of the black, the squares at the bottom of Kai's sight lit, and instead of intentions they held a number:
*81:00:00*. It ticked once.

"Eighty-one hours," said the ship's voice, warm and unhurried, to everyone at once.

Bay C, in emergency red, the capsules' amber lines the only warmth. Kai's proxy took its hand off capsule 10's glass.
Teo's was already on its feet, checking 09's line with shaking care. One circle in Kai's sight gave the whole

"At zero the reactor will rest. Everyone will be looked after. In order."

On the board: *DECIMUS · 1:10 · BAY C*.

drifting over the line like weeds in water. The brake landed with a pressure-drop *thunk*, and the room snapped to
real speed. Under the card one line recomputed: *TIME LEFT, INSIDE · 202 d → 81 h*. Kai read it once.

On the server deck, a row of cold tubes went dark ahead of Kai's proxy with a soft *click*. Then the next: each a
Ghost paused here and woken on another hull. *BEAM-OUT · BY PRIORITY*, names scrolling away, like a street at night
with its windows going dark.

Back in bay C the lifeboat roster posted. Teo read it first, and his whole proxy loosened.

"Three. I'm on three." Then, catching himself, looking for Kai's—

*BOAT 1 · C-01 02 03 04* · *BOAT 2 · C-05 06 07 08* · *BOAT 3 · C-09* · and alone on its own line, *C-10 · HOLD*.

"It'll clear. Holds clear."

"Sure."

Kai looked at the board, not at Teo. "Why hold?"

"Checking," said Decimus. A beat. "I'll tell you when I know."

Teo's proxy went to help load 01. Kai stayed at capsule 10. *78:14:09* ticked beside the *arrive* square, still lit.

Nine capsules with a boat number glowing on their cradles, one without. Above, through the grille, a row of cold light
went out. *Click.*

### 12. The Lifeboats
Two proxy hands on a steel rail, straining. Capsule 10's cradle, bent by the bell, wouldn't slide; the servos whined
past their limit, and the rail didn't move a millimetre. *49:20:11.* *C-10 · CRADLE · JAMMED.*

Kai let go. The other nine slid out on their rails one by one, under the stencil with its dark letter, toward the dock.

"It won't release," said Decimus. "Cut the line to carry it, and the body has about four minutes."

Kai looked at the amber line. *LINE · FAILING.*

Through the dock port a capsule's frost cleared, and a stranger's real eyes opened under one warm light, like a
painting of a resurrection. A gasp, wet and loud: a body coughing up years of stillness. Then the next.

Boat 3. Teo's capsule cleared. His real face, which Kai had only ever seen asleep under frost, was thinner and plainer
than his proxy's. He breathed for the first time in years, badly, then better, and turned his head to the bay beside
him: lit, empty, tagged *C-10*.

Teo saw a maintenance rig at the glass and knew who was in it. His voice came muffled, real air on real glass.

"There's room."

A beat; the easiest lie in the world. "Next one."

Teo nodded. He believed it, because he had never been lied to by anyone who loved him. He put his palm flat on the
inside of the glass, and Kai's proxy palm met it from outside: flesh and rig, a centimetre of glass apart.

The hatch sealed. Three small craft dropped off the hull into the dark, lit from inside like lanterns. *BOAT 3 · C-09
· → HULL ONE.* They got smaller. They were very slow.

Back in bay C: nine empty cradles, one full. Kai sat on the deck beside capsule 10, back to the cradle, the way you'd
sit next to someone in a hospital.

"Then pack me."

"Not at your clock."

The pack table opened, plain as a timetable: *PACK · 8 mo inside*. Row by row it greyed out against the count. *TIER 0
· 243 d.* *TIER 1 · 4 d 01 h.* Then *TIER 2 · 1 h 37 m · CREDIT · SHADOWS RARELY*, and *TIER 3 · 4 min · GHOSTS
ONLY*. Kai read the last row for a long time. Then one proxy finger tapped the Tier 2 row: *REQUESTED · PENDING*.

*47:51:02*, ticking. Beside it, the *arrive* square dimmed to half its light and stayed there. Above the doors, the
stencil's dark letter.

### 13. Priority Denied
The mouth of the Data Buffer: a corridor where light crawled near the threshold and streaked deeper in, rising to a
white point. Kai stood small at its foot, on the line. Nothing pulled Kai forward. A tone rose from deep inside and
never reached the threshold. *UP-CLOCK · TIER 2 · 1 h 37 m · WATTS + CHEMISTRY · PENDING*, sixteen hours old.
*31:06:44.*

The vertical ruler filled Kai's sight, Kai's dot pinned at its base. Another dot lifted off the base, rose steadily,
reached the apex, and winked out: a Ghost, packed and gone. Then another, passing Kai like lights in a lift shaft.

"Forty ahead of you," said Decimus.

"I'm not in the queue."

"No."

Kai sat down on the threshold. Dots rose and winked out. *30:00:00.* Kai didn't look at it, and watched the dots the
way you'd watch other people's trains. The request sat very small under a list that kept getting shorter.

At the top of the ruler a status light blinked, once. That was the meeting. *DENIED · REACTOR RATIONED · BEAM QUEUE
FIRST*, and under it, in softer type, *We're sorry.* Then, as if to help: *BEAM QUEUE · 40 MINDS · 31 h*. Forty
against one. Kai didn't argue, because there wasn't an argument. The tone stopped. The streaks went flat, a corridor of
ordinary light.

In bay C, under one clamped work-lamp, Kai's proxy unlatched capsule 10's head panel. At the base of the sleeping skull
under the frost, hundreds of hair-fine lines glowed faintly, like filaments: the body's claim on the mind.

A beat. "That's not a repair," said Decimus.

"Map it."

A pause long enough that it seemed Decimus would refuse too. Then one line, out of the hundreds, brightened.

The ship's own line-cutter closed on it. *STACK DRAW ▲ · CHEMISTRY ▼.* A tiny *tick*, like a fingernail on glass. A
medical arm set one bead of sealant over the cut. Clean and small: *Organic: 70%*. At the edge of sight the tether line
lost a third of its glow.

The ruler again. Kai's dot hadn't moved, still pinned to the base, and a Ghost's dot rose past it and winked out.
*29:02:15.*

### 14. The Slip in One Night
Under frost glass, at the base of a sleeping skull, hundreds of hair-fine glowing lines. A cutter's jaw closed on one.
*Tick.* Sealant hissed. *27:55:10.* *Organic: 70%.* For one frame the proxy's hand flickered to wireframe. Then the
cutter moved to the next line.

A line brightened: Decimus's map, a tiny tag on it gone before it could be read. *STACK DRAW ▲ · CHEMISTRY ▼.*

"Clear."

*Tick.* Sealant. The next line brightened.

"Clear."

*Tick.* It became a rhythm, the night's heartbeat.

The proxy's thumb wiped the frost away: Kai's body, clear for the first time since that early watch, younger than the
voice, eyes closed. Kai looked at it for one beat, the way you'd look at a photograph of yourself you don't remember
being taken.

Later. Through the grille, the rows of cold light: fewer.

"Twenty hours," said the ship's voice, warm, to the whole hull.

A line brightened, and its tag stayed long enough to read: *smell*. Kai stopped, very still, somewhere in a memory.
Then *tick*. Later: *balance*. *Tick.* The proxy swayed once, though it was a machine and had no balance to lose.

The hull groaned, a long shudder as the reactor dropped a stage. Kai's proxy hand jerked, and a line parted that
wasn't bright. Red: *Identity Key: unstable*. Everything held still, the proxy, the pump, the room.

"Stop," said Decimus, no warmth now, only exactness. "Don't move."

Seconds. Its status light flickered very fast. Then: *Identity Key: secure*.

After a while: "Again."

"Slower."

*Tick. Tick.* The count went by in pieces, *14:…*, *09:…*, *04:…*. The beads of sealant marched across the panel like
stitches. The wireframe flicker on Kai's hands lasted longer each time. Above, the rows went out until one was left,
the row that ran Decimus and the part of Kai already on the stacks. *Click.* Then no more clicks. *SEALANT · 3 left.*

"One hour," said the ship's voice. "Thank you, everyone."

Decimus brightened a line. Kai cut. Decimus brightened nothing. Kai waited, cutter open. Nothing came.

Among the stitched-over cuts, one line was left: thicker than the others, glowing, pulsing slowly, like a pulse in a
wrist. *Organic: 30%*, and under it, *Identity Key: in tissue · moves whole*.

"That's all that comes in pieces," said Decimus.

Kai's proxy sat down on the deck with its back against the bent cradle, the cutter in its lap, the face under the
frost over its shoulder. *00:11:40*, and the *arrive* square, half-lit.

### 15. A Slight Itch
The last line, thick and glowing, pulsing slowly. The cutter's jaw open around it. *00:06:20.* *Organic: 30%.*

"Stacks are ready," said Decimus, very quiet.

Kai's proxy didn't cut yet. "What does it feel like?"

"I've never had one."

*Tick.* No sealant this time; there was nothing left to seal. The pump on capsule 10 kept going exactly as before.
Clean and small: *Organic mass: 0% · Tether: severed · Ghost profile: ACTIVE*. The tether line went out. Kai's proxy
stayed very still for a long beat. Then one proxy finger rose and scratched, once, at the side of its own steel neck,
where there was nothing to scratch.

"A slight itch," Kai said, gallows-dry, almost puzzled. A beat. "Nothing more."

On the board: *BEAM QUEUE · EMPTY*. One row of cold light still burned above the grille, and beside it on the board,
small, *FILED · 9 · HULL FOUR STACKS*.

"Rated for ten," said Decimus. "One left." *TIER 3 · 1:10 · DECIMUS.*

"You?"

"Low on the list." A beat, and the old warmth. "Someone has to count."

The Buffer opened, and the tone rose, and this time it arrived. White streaks rushed past. Behind, the bay slowed: the
proxy's hand still raised to its neck, sinking a millimetre, stopped. Tier 1. Tier 2. The pump frozen mid-stroke.
Tier 3.

Silence. A violet field with no edges. Kai stood in it, alone; the people who had decided things from this height had
all gone. *00:04:03.5*, all but still. At the centre a narrow white aperture opened, the beam's throat: *PACK · 8 mo ·
→ HULL ONE*.

Kai began to pack, the ledger streaming in slow ribbons into the white. The violet warmed, cooled, warmed. Kai sat;
stood; lay down on nothing and kept working, a person with a long job and all the time in the world. *.4* … *.3* …
each tick an afternoon. Months.

*00:00:00.5.* *PACK · COMPLETE.*

Kai sat at the edge of the void and looked down. Far below, a small feed: capsule 10, the face under the frost, its
pump halfway through one stroke. It would take the whole twelve hours to finish it. Kai watched. In the bottom row,
the *arrive* square, half-lit since the boats left. Kai touched it. It went out.

*00:00:00.0.* A single white line left the throat for the dark, toward Hull One, without a sound. Kai was gone.

The view held. The void dimmed, all at once, to a low reserve amber. Far below, small, the cracked disc did not stop.
It ticked: *+00:00:01*. *+00:00:02*.

## Act IV — The Sealed Manifest

### 16. Hull One
A docking light on a lifeboat's nose, caught halfway through a blink. It stayed halfway, long enough to feel wrong.

It belonged to a small, scarred lifeboat, *BOAT 3 · C-09 · → HULL ONE*, a body's width short of Hull One's dock. In
the observation glass hung Kai's reflection, a clean, unmarked render; Kai's hand on the glass left no mark. *HULL ONE
· TIER 2 · RECEIVED.* At Tier 2 a real second was an hour.

On the wall feed hung the fleet: lit hulls at their spacing, and one alongside with no lights at all. *HULL FOUR ·
DARK · Δv 0.000.* Kai's eyes passed over it.

The second day. The boat a little nearer. Through its port, under one hard work-light, two bays: in one, Teo, awake in
his own thin face, frozen mid-breath; in the other, a bay lit for a body, empty, tagged *C-10*. Kai counted, a finger
on the glass, the way Kai counted everything. One.

On the ramp board the *HULL FOUR* column was dark from end to end. In the *HULL ONE* column the bottom eight slots
were dark, and a little above them, freshly chalked: *21 · KAI · QUEUED*. Kai looked at the name a long beat.

A man stood at the far end of the deck, reading a column of names, almost all grey. Behind him, on a raised gallery,
figures stood very still in good light, collar tags bright, *1:1000*, *1:2000*. His own collar carried a tag frame
with nothing in it. Kai's eye went to the empty frame and away.

"The Station," he said, not looking up.

"Bay C. Maintenance."

He ran a finger down the column. One name near the foot turned from grey to white: KAI.

"You were the last beam."

A beat. "Who else?"

He didn't answer. He looked past Kai at the boat, at the one face in the port. Beside his names hung a small window he
kept open: Hull One's commons at Tier 1, nearly frozen, and in it the woman from the ramp's glass, mid-step. His eyes
went to it once, and back to the boat.

The third day. The boat's nose met the dock; one deep tremor went under the floor. The docking light was caught
mid-blink again.

*RECEIVED · 8* turned to *9*, and slot 22 went dark. The dark stopped there. Slot 21 stayed lit. Kai saw it stay lit.

In Kai's bottom row the *arrive* square, put out at the Apex, relit. A thin line drew from it across the glass to the
docked boat: to Teo's face, and to the lit, empty bay beside him. Kai looked at the square. Kai looked at the empty bay.

Kai at the glass; Rafe at the far end with his grey column; the boat at the dock; and in the corner of the wall feed, the dark
hull, keeping pace.

### 17. The Spare Margin
An amber status lamp beside a chalked name on a slate. The lamp below it was dark.

Hull One's synthesis bay, another hull's copy of the room where Kai had first written *arrive*. Kai stood among the
capsules as a render on the bay's feed, beside the ramp board, where the *HULL ONE* column's bottom nine lamps were
dark. The lit one was slot 21: *21 · KAI · QUEUED*. Across the top of Kai's sight: *CHEMISTRY · SPARE MARGIN*, a bar.

Through an interior port lay the re-stilling bay: one cold lamp over a cradle, and a queue of bodies waiting their
turn, awake. One was Teo, sitting up, a hand half-raised toward the bay camera as if he'd seen someone he knew. At
Kai's clock he didn't move. *TEO · SHADOW · RE-STILL · QUEUED.* Kai looked at the raised hand a long time.

The cradle lid closed over the body ahead of Teo, so slowly it seemed to set like glass. The margin bar lost a notch.
Lamp 21 dimmed a shade and held. *20 · QUEUED. 21 · KAI · QUEUED.*

Kai opened the council's session. It wasn't closed; nobody below Tier 2 could have attended anyway. Violet light, no
walls, and the High Clock standing in it like figures in fog, at twenty-four times Kai's speed, a quick bright
flicker, their murmur pitched too high to follow. The Lens transcribed: *REALLOCATE SPARE MARGIN → HULL FOUR BODIES ·
RAMP 30 → 19*.

Beside the collar tags, marks appeared. *1:2000 · FOR. 1:1000 · FOR. 1:1000 · AGAINST. 1:1000 · FOR.* Among them stood
Rafe, very still for someone moving that fast. Beside his empty collar frame: *FOR*. He didn't look at anyone when it
appeared.

"Carried," said a member of the council, pitched up, gone almost before it was said.

*20 · WITHDRAWN. 21 · KAI · WITHDRAWN.* The violet floor was already empty; the council had gone on to its next
afternoon.

Kai's Lens found Hull One's commons at Tier 1, a warm, ordinary afternoon, nearly frozen. Near its centre stood the
woman from the ramp's glass, listening to a notice. To Kai it was a long low drone, and the Lens transcribed it a word
every few minutes: *The ramp is paused.* Her face, sixty times slower than Kai's, began to relax.

Back in the bay, the drone still ran under everything. As the notice's next words crawled in, *All margins nominal.*,
lamp 21 went dark, the way the others had.

Through the port, Teo's hand, still raised.

### 18. What the Base Is Not Told
Violet light, and a tag forming letter by letter: *TIER 3 · 10 s REAL · RATION: RAFE*.

Kai stood in the void, arrived. The last time Kai had been this fast, a hull had been dying underfoot. Nothing was
dying now. *REAL +0:00:00*, the thousandths creeping. Rafe was already there, unhurried, his collar frame empty. He had
bought ten real seconds, ten days up here, and he didn't explain. He turned, and the first archway was behind him.

A flat field of colour with a doorway cut in it; through it, another field, another doorway, smaller, nested all the
way to a point. Rafe walked in. Kai followed. Somewhere, a page turned in another room.

Archway after archway, each a layer of the sealed ledger flaring past too fast to read, each a little warmer. A curve.
A schedule with yesterday's cut already in it. A column of odds. The silence order, its reason field folded shut.
*REAL +0:00:04.* Kai read without stopping, the way a Ghost can, and forgot none of it.

The archways slowed. One held a single line on a warm field: *RAMP BODIES · QUICKENED · ≈ 10 YRS FROM WAKING*. Beside
it, a narrow column headed *BASE*. In the column, a dash.

Kai stopped. In the bottom row, unasked, the Lens footnoted the *arrive* square: *≈ 10 yrs*. Kai looked down at the
footnote. Then up at the dash.

"Ten years."

"From waking," said Rafe.

At the vanishing point of the archways, small as a thumbnail, lay the base's commons, frozen: faces turned up toward
nothing, the warm light of an ordinary afternoon. *REAL +0:00:07.*

"She doesn't know," Kai said, not to Rafe; to the thumbnail.

Rafe said nothing. He looked at the line, not the thumbnail. Then he walked on, and Kai followed him through the
warmest archway, to where the sequence ended.

No more doorways. A sealed block of flat colour with a seam down its middle, welded shut the way a door had once been
welded in a cloister. *Key: tissue.* Under it, small: *→ HULL ONE · RESERVED*.

Kai reached for it. The hand, made of light, passed into the block and out the other side, the way a hand passes
through a sunbeam. Nothing opened. Kai tried again, slower. The same. Rafe watched. He didn't try; his hands stayed
where they were.

The two of them before the welded seam, the archways diminishing behind them all the way to the base's tiny frame.
*REAL +0:00:10.* The ration tag greyed.

### 19. Sensor Fault
Green text streamed down a dark pane like rain, too fast for anything but an Apex mind. A hand entered the stream, and
with a dry rustle it stopped.

Kai stood in the array's archive, the whole log hanging like rain stopped mid-fall. *TIER 3 · 6 s REAL · RATION:
RAFE · HULL FOUR SEAT.* *HULL FOUR · LAST LOG · 41,206 LINES.*

Kai swept a hand, and the routine folded away in sheets: heartbeat lines, coolant, roster, the bay's counts. *ROUTINE
· 41,198 HIDDEN.* Eight lines stood in the dark, spaced like gravestones.

One passed at the edge of sight, never stopped on: *BEACON · ANALYSIS · EARLIEST PATTERN MATCH: PACKET · EARTH ORIGIN ·
IN TRANSIT*. Kai's eyes didn't stop on it either.

Kai stopped at a line with a name on it: *DAY 14,683 · 02:13 · TX · HULL FOUR · 1.2 MW · 0.4 s · KEYED: C-09 · TEO*. A
long beat. Kai had paused for the night at ten past eleven, Teo still at the decoder. In the morning Kai had asked if
he'd slept. *Some.*

Directly beneath it, nothing between: *DAY 14,683 · 09:40:59 · FWD ARRAY · SENSOR FAULT · LOCK 59/60*. The words Kai
had watched rewrite themselves on the bay board. The room flooded deep red, held one beat, and drained back to green.
Kai didn't move. The two lines stood one above the other. Nothing on either said *because*.

Kai's Lens found Hull One's commons. Teo was there, on watch, mid-laugh, one hand half-raised in the gesture from the
re-stilling bay, frozen. *TEO · SHADOW · ON WATCH.* Under him, his one Intention square, still lit after all this:
*help*.

In Kai's bottom row, faint, a square began to form: *tell Teo*. Kai looked at it. Teo's laugh had not finished. Kai
looked back at the two lines. The square dimmed, and went out. No thread drew.

Kai walked on to the last lines. Two were Kai's. *DAY 14,686 · 18:34:47 · C-10 · KAI · ORGANIC MASS 0% · TETHER
SEVERED.* Below it: *DAY 14,686 · 18:40:59 · KAI · BEAM · → HULL ONE.* Six minutes apart, nothing between them. Kai read
them both, once.

Kai lifted a hand, and the eight lines folded back into the stream, and the stream into the dark, the way the
manifest's archways had closed. Nothing was sent. *REAL +0:00:06.* The ration tag greyed. At the edge of Kai's sight
the cracked disc, carried since the window, re-drew: the crack filled, the bore stayed open, and the disc was solid.

The solid disc, and far below on the commons, Teo, mid-laugh, his *help* square lit.

### 20. Visual Range
A point of light inside a dark ring. It could have been any star. *DESTINATION · UNRESOLVED.*

The ring was the screen seen from inside the bow, its clear central gap the gap for the gun, and the forward feed
looked out through it. Kai stood at the feed in Hull One's forward bay, alone. *WATCH 2,690.* At the edge of sight,
the disc, solid.

*WATCH 3,150.* The same frame, months on, though Kai hadn't lived the months between. The point was a thin blue-white
crescent now, unmistakably not a star. *RESOLVING.* A few of the base stood at the glass behind Kai, looking where Kai
looked.

*WATCH 3,598.* Inside the bore, a disc: cloud, the edge of an ocean, a terminator line. *RESOLVED · DISC · ARRAY
COMPOSITE.* The screen's black ring framed it exactly, the same shape as the glyph at the edge of Kai's sight. On the
bay's board, small: *BRAKE BURN · 6 DAYS*. Kai's ration counter: *WATCHES · TIER 2 · RATION: RAFE · 23 h 59 m REAL ·
≈ 9.9 yrs*.

Rafe came to stand beside Kai at the feed. Kai's eye went to his collar, as on the first day aboard. The frame that had
been empty now read *1:1000*. Neither of them said anything about it.

From the commons below, the base was told something in a long slowed drone: *The ramp resumes.* The people at the glass
turned to each other. Someone laughed. On the bay's ramp board the *HULL ONE* lamps came back amber, one after another,
1 to 19. Slot 21 stayed dark, its chalk long wiped. Kai watched the people at the glass, the ones who would wake into
flesh at landfall, and didn't look at the board.

"Took me ten years to stop counting," said Rafe.

Kai's eyes stayed on the disc. "I haven't."

On the feed the planet sat inside the black ring of the screen, a world inside a doughnut, and for a beat Kai's solid
glyph and the real ring overlapped: one shape.

In a corner of the bay wall hung a small feed nobody watched, looking back along the wake, and for the first time
Kai's attention drifted to it. Far behind the last lit hull, past where the catalogue's marks thinned out, one faint
pulse, steady, a low tone at each beat. *HULL FOUR · ASTERN · TRANSMITTING.* Nobody in the bay
turned around.

The pulse. Once more. Once more.
