## Setup & hook
The child's clock first. A clay bowl in pieces on the hearth stones. A five-year-old kneels among them and fits the
shards back together, quickly, precisely, again and again, and one piece won't go, and she presses harder. She isn't
crying. She is working, the way an engineer works a fault that is her fault.

## UI behavior (the Perspective Lens)
Two Lenses this reel, both Ghosts' (`WR-L2`; no UI in the village except Raul's at the end). Per-character Lenses render the
same file differently (`VIS-08`, `VIS-17`).
- **Top circle (Context), both Lenses:** the parents' card from E11, `Lived: 192 yrs`. Read-only, as the Sea is.
- **Bottom square (Intention), the Helm's one editable field:** in **Rafe's** Lens it fills with a meter, `Charge`, and
  drains smoothly to `Charge: 0`. In **Raul's** it opens, and he switches it off: the square goes dark and reads nothing
  at all.
- **The split:** once it opens, the frame does not recombine for the rest of the stratum. Two Lenses walking.
- **The glimpse (≤ 2 s):** passing over the next layer down, unreadable tags blink over drowned faces. Not named, not
  focused, not explained.
- **The village, final shots:** Raul's Lens, surfaced into his shell. Nothing fills; the bottom square is still dark.

## Philosophical climax
Two men with one past and one set of parents look at the same file. One of them has spent decades building better
systems, and his system works: the grief drains out of the record cleanly, and his face eases, and something has gone out
of it. The other turns the tool off and keeps the ache, because the ache is what he has of them. Neither is wrong out loud;
they just stop walking in the same frame. Meanwhile a five-year-old who blames herself for a broken bowl is not fixed by
anyone. She is held, and the bowl stays broken, and she hums a song from a nursery she has never seen.

## Script (draft v1)
> 9:16 vertical · ~2.5 min · cinematic photoreal · *Black Mirror / Silo* register. **RAUL** (`CHR-02`), a Ghost in a shell,
> his Golden Baseline avatar in the stratum. **RAFE** (`Q-22`), Ghost, the rival-engineer. **MARA** (`CHR-01`), called
> Naomi in the village, about five. **RUTH** (`CHR-07`), about 25. One spoken line.

*Castes (`WR-B1`): Raul is a Ghost in a shell, Lens on, alignment off (`Q-119`); Rafe is a Ghost, in a leased shell in the
village, and reads the strata as an avatar; Mara and Ruth are Purists, no Lens (`WR-L2`). The fossil helmsmen are archive
renders, not minds. Timing (`WR-T4`, `WR-T1`): T ≈ +26, about 3.5 real years after E11. Everyone on screen runs Tier 0, 1×,
so real and subjective time are the same; the strata play only at 1:1, which is why the dive takes years. The card's 192
years are Tier 3 (86,400×) against Raul's eight at Tier 2 (3,600×), as paid in E11. No one runs Tier 1 (60×). Rafe has been
awake about 5.5 years since E2, after about 12 paused (T ≈ +8.2 → +20.5).*

**1 · THE BOWL · HOOK (0:00–0:05).** Dusk, a hut's hearth. A clay bowl in pieces on the stones. MARA, about five, kneels in
them, fitting shard to shard, fast, exact, over and over. One won't seat. She presses it harder. Her lip shakes; her hands
don't. [SOUND: clay clicking on stone.]

**2 · THE READ-ONLY SEA · CONTEXT (0:05–1:15, shots 2–4).** The Grid, the Helm stratum. A grey sea to the edge of the frame;
on the horizon, a storm that has been about to arrive for as long as this layer has existed. In rows on a stone quay,
**fossil helmsmen** stand at old ship's wheels. Each wheel spins freely and steers nothing. Below each, one small white
panel, the only thing that can be changed, and each helmsman is bent over his, editing, editing. One man's face is grey
with it. [SOUND: wheels ticking free on their spindles.]

**3 · THE CONSOLE.** At the end of the quay RAFE has rebuilt one of the old consoles: the fossil casing open, his own clean
work laid inside it, new light along old seams. RAUL beside him. Rafe sets the parents' card from E11 into it. [UI, Raul's
Lens: top circle, the card, `Lived: 192 yrs`.]

**4 · SAME FILE.** Rafe looks at Raul. Not a challenge; an offer.

> **RAFE:** Same file.

Raul looks at the card for a long beat. Then nods.

**5 · THE SPLIT · FRICTION (1:15–2:15, shots 5–6).** The frame divides down the middle (`VIS-10`): **left, Rafe's Lens;
right, Raul's.** The same card in each top circle. In each bottom square, the one editable field opens.
*Left:* the square fills with a meter, `Charge`, full. Rafe touches it. It drains, smooth, machine-even, all the way
down. [UI: `Charge: 0`.] Rafe's face eases. It is calm, and for a moment there is nothing behind the calm.
*Right:* the same square opens for Raul, the same meter waiting. He looks at it. With one finger he switches it off. The
square goes dark and reads nothing at all. His face keeps everything it had.
Neither speaks. Neither looks across the line.

**6 · THEY WALK ON, SPLIT.** The split holds as they leave the quay, each in his own half of the frame, walking the same
way, a stride apart. Underfoot the quay gives onto shallower water, and under it, for an instant, the next layer down slides
past: drowned faces, tags blinking over them, too small and too soft to read. *(≤ 2 s; unnamed.)* Gone.

**7 · RUTH · TURN (2:15–2:30, shots 7–8).** The hut, night. Ruth comes in behind the child and doesn't take the shards away.
She sits down on the floor and pulls Mara into her lap, shards and all. Nothing measures it. The bowl stays broken on the
stones. Against Ruth's shoulder Mara's breathing slows, and she begins to **hum**: a small, sweet, simple tune. At the open
door, Raul's shell, just surfaced, stops. It is the nursery song: the one Teddy sang in S1E2, still playing when the boys
came back in S1E3b.

**8 · FINAL IMAGE.** Raul's Lens, in the doorway: top circles idle; the bottom square still dark from the stratum, reading
nothing. He doesn't open it. The hum goes on. Cut to black on the tune, mid-phrase.

*(Theme in subtext, never stated: a better tool can empty a grief out of a record, and the man who uses it looks lighter and
less. The man who refuses keeps the ache, because it is what remains of the people. Neither says so, and the frame simply
stops being one frame. In the village no tool is offered: a woman holds a child who blames herself, and the bowl stays
broken. The tune is the undertow: a serial viewer knows where it was sung, and that no Purist could have taught it.)*
