> Your fast mind is a confident, systematic liar. Clear thinking means knowing your map isn't the territory, when to slow down to System 2, and which biases are bending your judgment.

## The mode of thinking
We run on fast, pattern-matching heuristics (System 1) that are efficient and often right —
but predictably wrong in known ways. Thinking well is not raw intelligence; it's *epistemic
humility plus a checklist*: distrust certainty, seek disconfirming evidence, and route
high-stakes judgments through slow, deliberate reasoning.

## Contradictions / open tensions
- Tension with "trust your [[inner-voice]]": resolved by domain — trust resonance for *values*,
  route *factual/probabilistic* claims through System 2 ([[gaps|G4]]).
- **Failure mode:** [[blindspot-bias]] — knowing the list doesn't debias you; structure and feedback do, not insight alone.

## How to apply it
1. On big/novel decisions, deliberately switch to System 2 — slow down, write it out.
2. Ask: *what would change my mind, and have I looked for it?* (counter [[confirmation-bias]]).
3. Check base rates and the simplest explanation before the dramatic story.
