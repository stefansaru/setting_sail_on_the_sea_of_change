> Move from living a life you were handed to authoring one you choose: examine inherited values, keep what's truly yours, and create your own hierarchy to live by.

## The mode of thinking
Most of what we believe and want was absorbed before we could question it. Self-authorship is
the developmental move from *inheriting* values (conformity) through *rejecting* them
(rebellion) to *creating* your own (authorship). It's not invented from nothing — you
discover what resonates, then author the *ranking* you commit to.

- **Score: 1** (`setting-sail`). Camel/lion/child is Nietzsche-specific, but the *pattern* (inherit→reject→create) is broadly shared.

## Contradictions / open tensions
- Self-authored ≠ solipsistic — values must account for others ([[contradictions|C4]], resolved via [[generous-tit-for-tat]]).
- "Discovered vs. created" resolved at [[values-discovered-and-created]] (signals discovered, commitments created).
- **Failure mode:** mistaking permanent rebellion (lion) for authorship; never reaching creation.

## How to apply it
1. Audit a strong belief: *is this mine, or did I absorb it before I could choose?*
2. List what genuinely resonates (signals); then rank them into a hierarchy you'd stand by.
3. Write values in present tense as a code of conduct; act from it ([[intention-in-context]]).
