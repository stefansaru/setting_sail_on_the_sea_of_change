> Your worth as a human is inherent and can't be lost; your self-respect is earned and moves with your conduct. Keep them separate — and change becomes growth, not self-rejection.

## The mode of thinking
A two-layer model of self-regard. **Worth** is given, unconditional, stable. **Self-respect**
is built by keeping promises and doing hard things. Confusing them turns a failure (a dip in
self-respect) into a felt loss of worth — the shame spiral. Separated, you can fully accept
yourself *and* strive to grow, fueled by worthiness rather than its lack.

## Contradictions / open tensions
- This *is* the resolution of C8; the only error is collapsing the two layers.
- **Failure mode:** trying to *earn* worth (impossible) — or expecting inherent worth to deliver the satisfaction only *earned* self-respect gives.

## How to apply it
1. After a failure, say: *my self-respect took a hit; my worth is unchanged.* Then rebuild respect by your response.
2. Pursue growth *from* worthiness, not *toward* it (links to [[acceptance-and-change]]).
3. Treat yourself as you would a friend ([[self-compassion-best-friend-test]]).
