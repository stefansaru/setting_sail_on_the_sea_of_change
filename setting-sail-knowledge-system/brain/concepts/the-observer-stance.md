> You are not your thoughts; you are the one who watches them. The moment you notice you're watching, you can choose your response instead of being run by it.

## The mode of thinking
Metacognition is the master skill beneath all the others: the capacity to step back and
observe your own thoughts, emotions, and impulses as *objects* rather than being fused with
them. Without it, you cannot exercise agency, catch a bias, or defuse an emotion — you'd just
react. With it, a gap opens between stimulus and response.

## Contradictions / open tensions
- **Failure mode:** over-observation → detachment/dissociation, sapping spontaneity and
  presence. The aim is to *direct, then re-engage* — observe, choose, act — not to spectate forever.

## How to apply it
1. Name it to tame it: *"I'm having the thought that…"* instead of *"It's true that…"*.
2. Picture thoughts as boats passing a dock; let the unhelpful ones drift by.
3. Before reacting, take one breath — that breath *is* the observer arriving.
