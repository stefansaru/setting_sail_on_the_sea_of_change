> Rewrite each wish that depends on things outside your control into a present-tense objective built on what you *can* control.

## Definition
A conversion exercise: take wishes (especially ones aimed at outcomes you can't control) and
restate them as controllable, present-tense objectives. "Don't just wish things were easier…
wish you were better, so that you can properly deal with all these things."

## Why it works
Wishes aimed at the uncontrollable ([[change-what-you-can-accept-what-you-cant]]) breed
frustration; objectives aimed at your own actions create progress. Present tense frames them
"as ongoing actions and commitments."

## Steps
1. List your wishes (from [[the-wishlist]], Ch.1).
2. Flag any that depend on others/luck/circumstance ("I wish my boss appreciated my work"; "I wish I'd win the lottery").
3. Rewrite each as a **present-tense objective** on what you control:
   - "I wish I were healthier" → *"I exercise 30 minutes daily and choose healthier foods."*
   - "I wish I'd get a promotion" → *"I enhance my skills and take on bigger responsibilities."*
   - "I wish my partner were more supportive" → *"I communicate my needs clearly and offer support in return."*
4. Feed the strongest into [[setting-smart-goals]].

## When to use it
- Reviewing goals/resolutions; whenever a wish creates stress without a path to act.

## Common mistakes
- Keeping goals phrased as outcomes outside your control.
- Future tense ("I will…") instead of committing present tense ("I do…").
