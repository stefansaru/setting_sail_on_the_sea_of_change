> Build redundancy into every important area of life so that no single failure can derail you.

## Definition
The practical engine of [[outcome-independence]]: spread your dependence across
multiple sources so that a setback in one doesn't become a catastrophe. Applies to
income, investments, and more — "when you diversify your opportunities, no single
failure has the power to derail you."

## Why it works
Concentration creates fragility: "if you put all your eggs in one basket... if the
basket were to suddenly drop, then your eggs would crack." Redundancy means a shock is
absorbed locally instead of toppling everything — converting brittle dependence into
resilient independence.

## Steps
1. **Audit single points of failure** — ask: *if I lose my job, how long can I last? If my business fails, do I have other revenue? If my investments drop, do I have alternatives?*
2. **Add income redundancy** — multiple streams: side businesses, freelancing, rental income.
3. **Diversify investments** — across stocks, bonds, real estate, etc. (see [[dont-put-all-eggs-in-one-basket]]).
4. **Keep a liquid buffer** — a [[backup-fund]] separate from investments.
5. **Extend beyond money** — diversify sources of meaning, support, and identity too.

## When to use it
- When your livelihood, finances, or wellbeing rests on one fragile source.
- When building toward [[outcome-independence]].

## Common mistakes
- Over-diversifying into things you don't understand (spreading thin ≠ informed risk; see [[why-investing-matters]]).
- Diversifying money but staking all emotional identity on one job/person.
- Confusing busyness across many things with genuine, resilient redundancy.
