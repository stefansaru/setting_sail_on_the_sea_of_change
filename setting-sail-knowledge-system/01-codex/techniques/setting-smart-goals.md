> Turn vague ambitions into action: Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. "Goals that aren't S.M.A.R.T. are just wishful thinking."

## Definition
A framework for writing goals that produce progress rather than guesswork. Example: not "I
want to get in shape," but "I will lose 10 pounds in 3 months by exercising 4 times a week
and following a balanced diet."

## Why it works
Vagueness kills follow-through. Each criterion removes a failure mode — ambiguity,
unmeasurable progress, unrealistic scope, irrelevance, no deadline — converting daydreams
into trackable action and small wins (which beat [[procrastination-as-protection|procrastination]]).

## Steps — each letter
- **S — Specific:** what exactly, who, what steps? ("I increase daily output via time-blocking and fewer distractions.")
- **M — Measurable:** a number/milestone to track. ("I write 500 words daily.")
- **A — Achievable:** realistic given time, resources, skills — challenging but not setting you up to fail.
- **R — Relevant:** connected to the bigger picture / your [[define-values-before-setting-course|values]] ("because it helps my career").
- **T — Time-bound:** a deadline ("within 6 months, with market research in month 1…").

## When to use it
- Converting any wish or resolution into an actionable goal (pairs with [[wishlist-to-objectives]]).
- Whenever a goal feels vague or keeps slipping.

## Common mistakes
- Setting unmeasurable or open-ended goals.
- Over-ambitious targets (ignoring [[planning-fallacy]] and "Achievable").
- Goals unaligned with values (achievable but empty — see [[start-with-the-end-in-mind]]).
