> Log each activity with two ratings — how it felt *during* and *after* — to reveal which parts of your life actually drain or nourish you.

## Definition
A simple journaling practice prescribed at the [[camel-phase-crisis]]: record
yesterday's activities and rate each on a "bad-to-good" scale both *while doing it*
and *afterward*. The gap between the two numbers exposes the difference between
momentary pleasure and lasting fulfillment.

## Why it works
It externalizes the [[inner-voice]] into data. Many activities feel good in the
moment but cost you afterward (and vice versa), and that gap is invisible until you
write it down. "The act of noticing is already the beginning of change." It is the
[[theme/observer-stance|observer stance]] applied to daily life.

## Steps
1. Take a notebook (or table with: date/time · activity · rate during · rate after).
2. List the activities you did yesterday.
3. Next to each, note how you felt **during** it (e.g. 1–5 or 1–10 — the exact scale doesn't matter).
4. Note how you felt **afterward**.
5. Do **not** judge what you see — just observe.
6. Repeat for a few days and look for patterns (which activities rise after, which crash).

## When to use it
- At the onset of restlessness or the [[camel-phase-crisis]] ("is this really mine?").
- When clarifying [[defining-your-own-values|values]] or auditing how you spend time.

## Common mistakes
- Judging or editing the entries instead of honestly observing.
- Rating only "during" (the pleasure) and missing the "after" (the cost/benefit).
- Quitting after one day — patterns need several days to surface.
