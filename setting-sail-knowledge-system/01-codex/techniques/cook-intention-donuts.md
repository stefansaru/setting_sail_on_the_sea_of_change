> Model any situation as a donut — the hole is your intention (how you want to act), the ring is the context — and make as many as you need to navigate life clearly.

## Definition
The book's capstone practice for applying [[intention-in-context]]. "Imagine a donut.
The hole in the middle is your intention — what you're attempting to do in that
situation. The ring around it is the context — the current state, the people, the mood,
the tools available, the risks, the constraints." Make a separate donut for any recurring
situation.

## Why it works
It externalizes a situation so you can *observe* it before reacting — turning you from
the overwhelmed participant into "the observer, and then the designer." Naming the
context's ingredients (emotions that might interfere, values to keep central) makes
aligned action far more likely than autopilot. "You're not aiming to find perfect
answers. You're just practicing awareness."

## Steps
1. Pick a recurring situation (morning routine, tough conversations, creative work, stress).
2. Draw/imagine a donut. In the **hole**, write your **intention** — how you want to act.
3. In the **ring**, list the **context** ingredients:
   - what the situation is
   - how you want to act in it
   - what emotions might interfere
   - what values you want to keep in focus
   - what mindset helps you stay aligned
4. If words fall short, switch media — see [[model-emotions-nonverbally]] (shape, color, song, gesture).
5. Keep the donut for navigation, not presentation: "what works for you, works."
6. Make as many donuts as you need; revisit and revise them.

## When to use it
- Before recurring high-stakes situations; when you feel "blurry" or reactive.
- As the everyday application of everything else in the book.

## Common mistakes
- Chasing "perfect answers" instead of practicing awareness.
- Building donuts to justify yourself to others (they're "for navigation," not presentation).
- Defining intention without honestly mapping the context.
