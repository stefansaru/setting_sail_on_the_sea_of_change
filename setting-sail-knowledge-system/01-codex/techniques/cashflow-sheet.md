> Put every income and expense into a spreadsheet across daily/monthly/yearly columns — because seeing the numbers side by side gives you real control.

## Definition
A tabular budget built in any spreadsheet tool (Excel, Google Sheets, LibreOffice
Calc) that lists all income and expenses across **three time scales** — daily, monthly,
yearly — grouped into budget categories, with a net result at the bottom.

## Why it works
It converts vague money anxiety into visible numbers. The three time scales reveal the
true cost of small habits ("coffee every day for 10 coins... adds up to 3600 coins per
year") and force yearly one-offs (insurance, property tax) into the monthly view so
they never ambush you. "Don't guess — write it down."

## Steps
1. Open a spreadsheet; make columns: **category · item · daily · monthly · yearly**.
2. List **income** (salary, side hustle) and total it across all three scales.
3. List **expenses**, grouped into budgets: *fixed* (rent, utilities, insurance) and *variable* (groceries, coffee, entertainment, subscriptions).
4. Convert each item across scales (e.g. 10/day ≈ 300/month ≈ 3600/year).
5. For yearly one-offs, divide by 12 and save that amount monthly (1200/year → 100/month set aside).
6. Compute **net result = income − expenses** at the bottom.
7. Read the result: **green** (surplus → savings/debt/investing) or **red** (deficit → cut spending or increase income).

## When to use it
- Before any other financial step (it underpins [[positive-cashflow]], [[backup-fund]], investing).
- Whenever you don't know where your money actually goes.

## Common mistakes
- Estimating instead of recording real numbers.
- Ignoring yearly one-offs until they hit.
- Building it once and never updating it.
