> When words can't capture a feeling, use another medium — a shape, a color, a song, a gesture — to model it, take back power, and become its designer rather than its victim.

## Definition
A practice for handling emotions that resist language: "stop thinking only in words...
your mind can do more than that. You can think in images, in sounds, in colors, in
feelings, in rhythms, in silence." Model the emotion in whatever medium fits.

## Why it works
"Your brain is wired for patterns. Use that. When you model emotions this way, you take
the power back. You stop being overwhelmed. You become the observer — and then, the
designer." Externalizing a feeling into a non-verbal form creates distance (the
[[theme/observer-stance|observer stance]]) and makes a vague emotion concrete enough to
work with — a complement to [[cook-intention-donuts]].

## Steps
1. Notice an emotion that words don't capture well.
2. Ask one or more non-verbal questions:
   - *What shape does this emotion have?*
   - *What color would you paint it?*
   - *What song matches this mood?*
   - *What physical gesture expresses it?*
3. Render it (sketch, hum, gesture) — "it doesn't have to be logical to others, just useful to you."
4. Notice the shift from being *in* the emotion to *observing* it — then choose how to respond.

## When to use it
- When an emotion feels too big or tangled to name.
- Inside the "emotions that might interfere" ingredient of an intention donut.

## Common mistakes
- Insisting on verbal/logical analysis of feelings that aren't verbal.
- Trying to make the model "correct" or presentable rather than useful.
