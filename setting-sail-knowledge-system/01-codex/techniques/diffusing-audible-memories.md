> Some memories haunt as sounds — a cruel phrase on repeat. Treat them as an audio file: lower the volume, shift the pitch, add cartoon effects, move the sound away, then re-title it.

## Definition
The auditory counterpart to [[diffusing-visual-memories]]. Not all memories are visual;
some "linger in the form of sounds, voices, or phrases that replay like an unwanted track."
The same editing logic applies to sound.

## Why it works
As with images, the *qualities* of a remembered sound (volume, pitch, closeness, direction)
carry much of its emotional weight. Altering them — especially with humor — breaks the
sound's grip while keeping you the observer ("editing, not reliving").

## Steps
1. **Enter the recording studio** of System 2; find the unwanted audio clip. Don't press play.
2. **Keep it paused** — observe it as a static waveform you can modify.
3. **Diagnose** what makes it unsettling: volume (too loud/sharp), pitch (too high/deep), closeness (feels like now?).
4. **Lower the volume**, like a distant radio; **adjust pitch** (raise a booming voice to silly, lower a screech to calm); add **white noise** to neutralize.
5. **Add a twist** — cartoon effects (turn an insult into a chipmunk voice), slow it like a sluggish tape; **move the sound away** (left/right/distant) to detach from its weight.
6. **Save & exit with a new title** so the mind meets the label before the distressing content.

## When to use it
- Replaying hurtful words, an argument, or a distressing noise.
- For auditory-leaning people, or memories that are primarily sound.

## Common mistakes
- Pressing play and re-triggering the emotion.
- Forgetting that the method is "a tool" — adapt it; if one tweak doesn't help, try another.
