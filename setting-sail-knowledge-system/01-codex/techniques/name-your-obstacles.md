> List what blocks each goal — honestly and specifically — then write one way through each, however small or silly. You're not making a plan; you're teaching your mind to search for exits.

## Definition
The companion to [[the-wishlist]]: after writing what you want, "look at what blocks it."
Write the obstacles between you and your goals, "be honest, be specific," and "keep going
until the same obstacles start to repeat." Then, for each, "write one way through. Make it
simple. Or make it outrageous. Both work... You're encouraging your mind to search for exits."

## Why it works
Naming obstacles converts vague dread into specific, addressable items (clarity, not
judgment). Generating *any* path through — even a silly one — primes the mind's
problem-solving and breaks helplessness. The "respected person" move borrows an outside,
calmer perspective.

## Steps
1. List the obstacles between you and each goal — honest and specific; continue until they repeat.
2. For each, write **one way through** (simple, outrageous, even wrong — it counts).
3. If stuck, ask smaller questions: *What's one tiny step? Who could help? What am I avoiding?*
4. Widen the view: study people who faced similar obstacles — "take their lessons," don't copy their steps.
5. Pick one calm, capable person you respect; imagine them in your exact situation: *what would they do next?*

## When to use it
- Right after [[the-wishlist]]; whenever a goal feels blocked.

## Common mistakes
- Vague obstacles ("I'm just busy") instead of specific ones.
- Judging yourself instead of mapping for clarity.
- Demanding a perfect plan rather than just possible exits.
