> Attach a new habit to one you already do — the existing habit becomes the cue for the new one.

## Definition
"The easiest way to adopt a habit is by attaching it to an existing one." The
established habit serves as a reliable [[the-habit-loop|cue]], so the new behavior "fits
seamlessly into your routine."

## Why it works
New habits fail mostly from missing or unreliable cues. An existing daily habit is an
already-automatic trigger; bolting the new behavior onto it borrows that reliability
instead of building a cue from scratch.

## Steps
1. Identify a habit you already do every day without fail (morning coffee, brushing teeth).
2. Choose a small new habit to attach.
3. State it as: *"After I [existing habit], I will [new habit]."*
   - Book example: already drink coffee each morning → pair it with a five-minute stretch.
4. Keep the new habit small at first (combine with the [[two-minute-rule]]).
5. Repeat until the pairing itself becomes automatic.

## When to use it
- Whenever you want a new habit to "stick" without relying on memory or motivation.
- Building a morning/evening routine out of several stacked micro-habits.

## Common mistakes
- Anchoring to an existing habit that isn't actually consistent.
- Stacking too many or too-large new habits onto one anchor.
