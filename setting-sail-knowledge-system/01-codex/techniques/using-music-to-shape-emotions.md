> Music is "one of the strongest instruments you have for your mind." Build playlists in advance so that when you're low, one click — not endless scrolling — changes your state.

## Definition
A practical use of [[anchoring]] through sound: prepare playlists ahead of time for
specific states, so shifting your mood is "one click" rather than a search you won't make
when you're already down.

## Why it works
"A single song can pull you back into a memory, shift your mood, or give you energy." Songs
are powerful pre-built emotional [[anchoring|anchors]]; having them ready removes the
friction (and the doom-scrolling) at exactly the moment your willpower is lowest.

## Steps
1. **Make an "uplifting" playlist** — songs that reliably "give you a lift," to move "from stuck to moving" when you feel down. (Book examples: *Walking on Sunshine*, *Dancing in the Dark*, *Turn Around*, etc.)
2. **Make a "feel-good" playlist** — to *reinforce* a good mood when you're already up. (Book examples: *Walk of Life*, *Night Fever*, *Everywhere*, etc.)
3. **Curate continuously** — "when you find a track that lifts you up, write it down and add it" to the right playlist.
4. **Use one click** — when your state needs changing, play the relevant list immediately; don't scroll.

## When to use it
- Feeling low and needing momentum (uplifting list).
- Wanting to sustain/amplify a good state (feel-good list).
- Pairs with [[set-an-anchor]] — a song can anchor a deliberate state.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting until you're already low to *search* for music (build the lists in advance).
- Using only sad music when low (which deepens the loop) instead of an uplifting list to shift state.
