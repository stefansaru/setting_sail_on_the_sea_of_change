> Ready-made phrases that let you decline clearly and kindly — no long justification, no burned bridges.

## Definition
A small set of pre-written refusals that turn the [[art-of-saying-no]] into something
you can deploy on the spot, without over-explaining or being harsh.

## Why it works
Most people say yes because they lack a graceful exit in the moment. Having the words
ready removes the friction; the softest no — "clear, kind, and firm" — is the most
effective. You don't owe a 10-minute explanation.

## Steps / scripts
- *"I'm not the right fit for this."*
- *"Thanks for thinking of me, but I'll pass."*
- *"I can't take this on right now."*
- Keep it short. Don't justify at length. Don't be rude — firm ≠ harsh.
- Remember the exit valve: *"the lion doesn't need to roar at everything — sometimes the lion can just calmly walk away."*

## When to use it
- Requests that drain or distract you from what matters.
- Any time the reflexive "yes" of the [[camel-phase]] is about to fire.

## Common mistakes
- Padding the no with excessive apology or explanation (invites negotiation).
- Going harsh when calm firmness would do.
- Believing you owe people your time simply because they asked ("you owe yourself honesty… peace").
