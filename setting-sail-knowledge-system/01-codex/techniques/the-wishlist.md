> Write down every unspoken wish — big or small, like a letter to Santa — until the wishes start repeating and your mind feels empty. That repetition reveals what truly matters.

## Definition
The book's opening exercise: empty out your unspoken desires onto paper. "Anything goes —
big or small, near or far. Just be honest." Write "as much as you need" — even "an entire
notebook." Completion is signaled by *repetition*: "once the repetition stops and your mind
feels empty, your list is complete."

## Why it works
Externalizing wishes "lightens the load" and surfaces patterns: "as you write, you might
notice some wishes repeat — that's a good sign; you're starting to see what really matters."
It's the raw input for [[bridge-the-gap]], [[wishlist-to-objectives]], and
[[setting-smart-goals]], and a window into your [[defining-your-own-values|values]].

## Steps
1. Find private, secure space (it gets personal) and something to write with — **pencil**, so you can revise.
2. Write every wish, unfiltered — "imagine you're writing to Santa Claus."
3. Don't stop at one page; keep going until wishes **repeat** and your mind feels empty.
4. Note repeated wishes — they point to what matters most.
5. Date it / keep a journal of when you set these wishes.
6. **Revisit regularly** — every few months, then once or twice a year; "a life worth living is worth tracking."

## When to use it
- At the start of any growth effort; whenever direction feels unclear.
- Periodically, to see how you've evolved.

## Common mistakes
- Filtering or judging wishes as you write (kills honesty).
- Writing in pen / treating it as fixed (it should evolve).
- Stopping before the repetition reveals the core.
