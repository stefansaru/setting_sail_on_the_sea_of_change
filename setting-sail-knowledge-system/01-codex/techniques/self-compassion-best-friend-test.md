> When you mess up, ask: "Would I say this to a best friend who made the same mistake?" If not, don't say it to yourself.

## Definition
A one-question intervention that swaps [[the-inner-critic|the inner critic]] for the
voice of a good friend. When you fail and the critic "takes the stage, ready to replay
its greatest hits of self-doubt," you hit pause and run the test.

## Why it works
We are routinely kinder and wiser toward friends than toward ourselves. Borrowing the
friend's perspective instantly reveals how disproportionately harsh self-talk is — and
supplies a better script: understanding, forgiving, and focused on learning rather than
punishment. It operationalizes [[self-compassion]].

## Steps
1. Notice the inner critic activating after a mistake.
2. **Pause.** Don't act on the harsh voice.
3. Ask: *"If my best friend made this exact mistake, what would I say to them?"*
4. Say *that* to yourself — understanding, kind, forgiving.
5. Keep the lesson, drop the punishment: *what would I learn from this and try next time?*

## When to use it
- Immediately after a mistake, failure, or embarrassment.
- Whenever you catch globally harsh self-judgment ("I'm such an idiot").

## Common mistakes
- Using it to dodge accountability — keep the learning, only drop the cruelty.
- Stopping at "be nice to yourself" without extracting the lesson.
- Forgetting to pause first (the critic speaks fast).
