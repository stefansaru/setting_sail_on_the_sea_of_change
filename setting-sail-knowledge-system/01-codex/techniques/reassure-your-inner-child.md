> When fear strikes, ask whether it's *you* or your inner child who's afraid — then soothe that part with a quiet "we've got this."

## Definition
A fast emotional move that operationalizes [[courage-is-acting-despite-fear]] via the
[[inner-child]] concept. Instead of being fused with fear, you externalize it as a
frightened internal part and speak to it kindly.

## Why it works
Externalizing fear ("who is really frightened — me or my inner child?") creates
psychological distance, converting "I am afraid" (fused, paralyzing) into "a scared
part of me is afraid" (observed, manageable). Self-compassion calms the threat
response better than self-criticism.

## Steps
1. Notice the fear without fighting it.
2. Ask: *"Who is really frightened? Is it me, or my inner child?"*
3. Picture that frightened younger part of yourself.
4. Speak to it softly and kindly — as you would to a scared child needing comfort: *"We've got this."*
5. Take the next small step while still afraid (the fear doesn't have to leave first).

## When to use it
- Before a daunting action (hard conversation, public speaking, a big "no").
- Any moment fear is about to make the decision for you.

## Common mistakes
- Suppressing or shaming the fear instead of comforting it.
- Waiting for the fear to vanish before acting (it usually won't).
- Treating it as a one-time trick rather than an ongoing, kind relationship with that part of yourself.
