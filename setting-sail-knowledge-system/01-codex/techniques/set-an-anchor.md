> Re-enter a peak emotional state, then "stack" its triggers — what you saw, heard, felt, did — onto a deliberate cue, so you can fire that state on demand later.

## Definition
The deliberate procedure for building an [[anchoring|anchor]] by recalling a strong state
and layering ("stacking") its components onto a chosen trigger.

## Why it works
Recreating the *components* of a past peak state (image, sound, self-talk, body posture,
breath) re-activates the state itself; pairing it repeatedly with a trigger trains the
nervous system to associate the two. "You already know how to feel that way — you've done
it before. Your brain remembers. Your body remembers."

## Steps
1. **Recall a peak state** — "a time when you felt completely motivated... fully alive, focused, and ready." Go back into it.
2. **Notice the first trigger** — what did you see first? hear? feel in your body? say to yourself?
3. **Stack the next** — what pushed you *deeper* into the state? a sound, a thought, a gesture?
4. **Keep stacking** — a breath, standing taller, clenching a fist, a quieting/focusing of the mind.
5. **Anchor it to a cue** — pair the full stacked state with a deliberate trigger (e.g. fist-clench) at peak intensity.
6. **Fire it when needed** — reproduce the cue to "carry its power into this moment." Reinforce often.

## When to use it
- Before high-stakes moments (a talk, a game, a hard conversation) to summon confidence/focus/calm.
- Any time you want to shift state quickly rather than wait to "feel" ready.

## Common mistakes
- Anchoring at low emotional intensity (weak, unreliable anchor).
- Setting it once and never reinforcing it.
- Picking a cue you'll trigger accidentally in unrelated contexts.
