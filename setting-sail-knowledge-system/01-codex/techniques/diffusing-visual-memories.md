> Treat a painful memory as a paused video and edit its "settings" — dim the brightness, drain the color, blur it, flip it, then save it under a wiser title — to remove its emotional charge.

## Definition
A step-by-step procedure (from [[memory-as-editable-film]]) for reducing the emotional
intensity of a *visual* memory by adjusting how it appears in the mind's eye.

## Why it works
Per the book's NLP lineage, the felt impact of a memory is carried by its *representation*
(vividness, color, size, orientation), not only its content. Change the representation and
the feeling changes — without erasing the event. Pausing it first keeps you the
[[becoming-the-observer|observer]], not a participant reliving it.

## Steps
1. **Sit in the chair, locate the "video file"** — enter [[the-operations-room|System 2's control room]] and find the memory.
2. **Pause — don't hit play.** Hold it as a single frozen frame; observe it "like scrolling through old photos," not reliving it.
3. **Open "video settings."** Lower **brightness** (soften harsh vividness); reduce **contrast** (blend the extremes); drain **saturation** (remove the emotional charge); shift **hue** toward a gentler tone.
4. **Blur / reduce quality.** Soften sharpness, lower resolution (grainy old-film look), apply a sepia or grayscale **filter**, let insignificant details fade.
5. **Flip horizontally.** Mirror the image to "break the automatic connection between memory and emotion" — it stays recognizable but feels detached.
6. **Save & exit with a new title.** Ask "what did I learn?" and rename it — e.g. "embarrassing mistake at work" → "a lesson in growth." File it in your [[hall-of-fame-for-memories|hall of fame]].

## When to use it
- A specific painful visual memory that still triggers you.
- Defusing a memory-based fear ([[most-fears-are-memories]]) — e.g. the elevator-phobia case (§6.4).

## Common mistakes
- Hitting "play" and getting pulled back into reliving it (always keep it paused).
- Trying to delete the memory instead of softening its impact.
- Skipping the re-titling step — the new conclusion is what stops the replay (see [[mindful-memory-loops]]).
