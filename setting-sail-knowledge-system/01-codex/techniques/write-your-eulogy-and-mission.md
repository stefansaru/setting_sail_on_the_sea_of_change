> Imagine what you'd want said about you at the end of your life — that's your compass north. Then distill it into a short, value-driven mission statement you review yearly.

## Definition
A pair of Covey exercises ([[start-with-the-end-in-mind]]) that make your "end in mind"
concrete: writing your own eulogy to find your true direction, and drafting a personal
mission statement as a lifelong compass.

## Why it works
The eulogy forces a long-horizon view that cuts through daily noise — it reveals what
you actually want to have *been*, not just done. The mission statement then keeps that
clarity available: "a compass, not a map," it holds even "when the path changes."
Together they make [[define-values-before-setting-course]] vivid and personal.

## Steps
1. **Write your eulogy today.** Imagine what you'd want people to say about you at the end of your life. That's "your real compass' north."
2. **Draft your mission statement.** Keep it short, personal, value-driven. It answers: *Who do I want to be? What do I stand for? What legacy do I want to leave?*
3. **Pause before decisions:** ask *"Does this move me closer to the life I want?"*
4. **Say no to distraction:** every "yes" to something meaningless takes time from what matters ([[art-of-saying-no]]).
5. **Check direction weekly;** review the mission statement **yearly** and recalibrate.

## When to use it
- Once, to draft; then yearly to review, and weekly for direction checks.
- At major crossroads (career, relationships, relocation).

## Common mistakes
- Writing a mission statement full of others' expectations rather than your values.
- Drafting it once and never reviewing (it should evolve).
- Treating it as a rigid map instead of a compass.
