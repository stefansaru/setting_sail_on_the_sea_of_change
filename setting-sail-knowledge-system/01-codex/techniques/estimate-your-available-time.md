> Shade the [[the-ruler-of-life|ruler of life]] from birth to your current age, and from the far end back to life expectancy. The blank stretch in the middle is your available time — your playground.

## Definition
A confronting, motivating visualization using [[the-ruler-of-life]]: make your finite
remaining time *visible*.

## Why it works
Abstract "time is limited" becomes concrete and urgent when you literally see how much ruler
is already shaded and how much blank space remains. It turns [[memento-mori]] into a planning
tool: "that blank stretch… it's your playground… yours to fill."

## Steps
1. Draw the ruler of life (0 → a high number like 122 for perspective).
2. **Shade from the left up to your current age** — acknowledging the journey already made.
3. Note average life expectancy (the book cites ~74.2 yrs women / ~69.8 yrs men, 2019; intersex → use the lower as a cautious estimate).
4. **Shade from the right back to that expectancy.**
5. **What's left in the middle = your estimated available time.** Sit with it.
6. Channel the urgency into action — feed it into [[setting-smart-goals]].

## When to use it
- Periodic life planning; when you're drifting or deferring ("someday").
- Alongside the [[memento-mori]] reflection.

## Common mistakes
- Treating it as morbid rather than mobilizing.
- Letting the exercise create anxiety instead of focused action.
