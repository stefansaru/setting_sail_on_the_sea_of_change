> A simple breath-and-gratitude reset: breathe deeply ~20 times, release stress on each exhale, and thank your heart for its steady beat. Pair with quiet music and a calm space.

## Definition
A short practice to "calm and present" the mind before deep work or reading — plus the
book's reading ritual (quiet music, fewer distractions, a warm tea).

## Why it works
Slow breathing settles the nervous system and creates "a quiet moment in your busy
schedule," priming the [[becoming-the-observer|observer]] state. The gratitude beat ("thank
your heart") and a deliberately prepared space turn reading/reflection into a ritual you
give yourself — raising the odds the work actually lands.

## Steps — the breathing exercise
1. Take a deep breath, filling your lungs completely.
2. Hold for a moment — notice the quiet pause you just created.
3. Exhale gently, "like you're whispering a secret."
4. On each exhale, "release a balloon of stress into the atmosphere."
5. Repeat ~**20 times**; close your eyes if you like; optionally stand barefoot on solid ground.
6. Place a hand over your heart, feel the beat ("thump-thump"), and offer a silent "thank you."
7. Carry the calm and gratitude with you.

## Set the reading ritual
- **Music:** quiet, lyric-free — ambient or classical (the book suggests Moby's *Ambient 23*, Satie, Chopin), or nature sounds (rain, fireplace).
- **Space:** reduce distractions, pause notifications, make a warm tea. "Sit well. Breathe. Get comfy."

## When to use it
- Before reading/reflecting; any time stress spikes ("a quick pause can refresh your mind anytime").

## Common mistakes
- Rushing the breaths or skipping the exhale-release imagery.
- Choosing music with lyrics that pull attention.
