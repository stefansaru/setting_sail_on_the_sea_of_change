> Catch limiting words as you use them and swap in constructive alternatives — especially in your inner dialogue.

## Definition
The practice of identifying [[limiting-words]] in your speech and self-talk and
replacing them with phrasing that preserves the meaning without the toxic charge.

## Why it works
Language shapes mindset; repeatedly chosen words harden into beliefs. Swapping the word
swaps the belief it reinforces — over time "removing them completely from our
vocabularies... allowing us to develop a more constructive and empowering way of
thinking."

## Steps / swap table
1. **Notice** the word as you say or think it.
2. **Replace** with a constructive alternative:
   - "I hate ___" → "I prefer ___ over ___."
   - "I'm bored" → "I'd like to find something interesting to focus on."
   - "I'll try" → "I will ___" (commit).
   - "I feel guilty for ___" → "I prioritize ___ because it helps me be at my best."
   - "That's ugly" → "That's not my style."
   - "It's impossible" → "It's challenging, but with consistent effort I can make progress."
   - "You always / never ___" → "I'd appreciate it if you ___ more often."
3. **Watch the inner dialogue most** — that's where it matters most.
4. Be patient; this changes "with time and patience," not overnight.

## When to use it
- In daily speech, and especially in self-talk and conflict.
- Whenever an absolute ("always/never") or a defeatist word ("impossible") appears.

## Common mistakes
- Reframing the word while leaving the underlying feeling unprocessed (euphemism, not growth).
- Policing others' words instead of your own.
- Expecting instant change rather than gradual habit shift.
