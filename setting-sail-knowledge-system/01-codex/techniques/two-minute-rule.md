> If a habit feels overwhelming, shrink it to a version that takes two minutes — one push-up, one paragraph. Starting small removes the resistance.

## Definition
"Starting small removes resistance. If a habit feels overwhelming, begin with a version
that takes just two minutes." Instead of a full workout, do one push-up; instead of a
chapter, read a paragraph. "These small actions make habits easier to start, often
leading to longer engagement naturally."

## Why it works
The hardest part of a habit is starting; a two-minute version lowers the activation
energy below the threshold of resistance. Once begun, momentum often carries you
further — but even if it doesn't, you've kept the habit alive and reinforced the
identity ("I'm someone who shows up").

## Steps
1. Take the habit you're avoiding because it feels too big.
2. Scale it down to a version doable in ≤ 2 minutes.
3. Commit only to the two-minute version.
4. Start. Let momentum extend it *if it comes* — but don't require it.
5. Repeat daily; grow the habit gradually once showing up is automatic.

## When to use it
- Any habit you keep postponing because it feels too demanding.
- Re-starting a habit after a lapse (make the comeback trivially small).

## Common mistakes
- Secretly making "two minutes" a trick to force the full task (then dreading it again).
- Scaling up too fast before the showing-up is automatic.
