> Plot ideas on a payoff-vs-ease grid: Possible, Implement, Challenge, Kill. Do the easy things that matter and the hard things worth it.

## Definition
A 2×2 grid for choosing among many ideas/projects. Horizontal axis = **payoff** (benefit);
vertical axis = **ease of implementation** (cost/effort). Each idea lands in one quadrant.

## Why it works
Not all ideas deserve equal energy. The grid makes the trade-off visible so you spend
limited energy where it returns most — complementing [[pareto-principle|80/20]] and
[[eisenhower-method]].

## Steps / the four quadrants
1. **Implement** (easy + high payoff): do these *first* — simple actions with lasting benefit (prep clothes the night before; water first thing; phone away before bed).
2. **Challenge** (hard + high payoff): worth doing, but schedule carefully — start small, prove value, expand (regular exercise; a budget; waking early).
3. **Possible** (easy + low payoff): small wins for momentum (tidy phone screen; a "good morning" text). Do when you need a lift.
4. **Kill** (hard + low payoff): drop without regret (over-explaining to please everyone; fixing people who don't want to change; keeping things "just in case").

**Process:** list ideas → rate each on ease & payoff → place on the grid → do Implement first, Challenge next, Possible if time allows, Kill without regret.

## When to use it
- When you have more ideas/projects than energy.
- Quarterly planning or clearing a backlog.

## Common mistakes
- Pouring energy into "Kill" tasks that look impressive but give little back.
- Doing only easy "Possible" wins and avoiding high-payoff "Challenge" work.
