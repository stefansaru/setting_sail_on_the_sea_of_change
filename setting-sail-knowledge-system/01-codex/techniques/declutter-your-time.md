> To add something new to your life, swap out something old — your time is a full bookshelf, so make room deliberately rather than cramming.

## Definition
The action form of [[decluttering]], via [[the-bookshelf-of-life]]: "want to add something
new? Something has to go." Each addition is paired with a deliberate removal.

## Why it works
Time and attention are finite shelf space; "you can't cram an extra book into an already
overflowing shelf." Pairing a new habit with a specific *swap* makes the new commitment
realistic and prevents overload. (And per "the sum of vices is constant," removing without
replacing invites a new bad habit — so swap, don't just delete.)

## Steps
1. Name what you want to add (a language, fitness, stronger friendships, creativity, calm).
2. Identify what it will **replace** — a low-value time sink:
   - learn a language → swap 30 min of scrolling for a daily lesson.
   - get fit → swap late-night bingeing for earlier sleep + a morning workout.
   - more creativity → trade an hour of passive content for a hands-on project.
   - less stress → cut doom-scrolling/overcommitting; recharge with a walk.
3. Make the swap explicit and scheduled (pairs with [[functional-equivalence]] — keep the function, change the form).
4. Review periodically; keep what adds meaning, release what no longer serves.

## When to use it
- Adding any new habit/commitment to an already-full life.
- When you "don't have time" — the real issue is usually allocation, not availability.

## Common mistakes
- Adding without subtracting (overflow, then collapse).
- Removing a habit without a replacement (it returns).
