> Date deliberately: short low-investment first meetings, keep options open, and filter for value alignment before emotions take over.

## Definition
A practical dating method for choosing a long-term partner well, given that the
[[relationship-phases-dating-vs-longterm|dating phase]] runs on emotion more than
logic. The aim is to filter for [[values-alignment-in-relationships|value alignment]]
*before* over-investing.

## Why it works
Initial attraction "may overlook" core-value mismatches that "become burdens over
time." Low-investment screening and keeping options open reduce desperation and
neediness — which is "naturally unattractive" — so you choose from clarity, not
scarcity. "Desperation leads to poor choices; patience allows you to wait for a
connection that truly feels right."

## Steps
1. **Know your filters first** — identify the values that matter most to you (education level, financial mindset, spiritual beliefs, lifestyle).
2. **Short initial meetings** — meet in a public place for no more than ~an hour, to avoid unnecessary investment.
3. **Keep options open** — line up multiple first dates so you don't overinvest in one person too soon (reduces neediness).
4. **Expand your environment** — bigger/varied social settings increase the odds of meeting compatible people.
5. **Be patient and selective** — let alignment, not loneliness, drive the decision.

## When to use it
- The dating/searching phase, especially after a pattern of poor matches.

## Common mistakes
- Overinvesting emotionally before checking value alignment.
- Letting loneliness or desperation pick the partner.
- Treating filtering as coldness — it's clarity about long-term compatibility, not a lack of warmth.
