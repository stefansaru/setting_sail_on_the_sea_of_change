> Separate urgency from importance. Sort tasks into four quadrants and act accordingly: Do, Plan, Delegate, Eliminate.

## Definition
A 2×2 prioritization grid distinguishing **urgent** (demands attention now) from
**important** (moves your life forward). "Knowing the difference keeps you from mistaking
noise for direction."

## Why it works
Most people drown in the urgent-but-unimportant and neglect the important-but-not-urgent
(where real growth lives). Sorting by both axes redirects energy from noise to progress —
the [[pareto-principle|80/20]] applied to tasks.

## Steps / the four quadrants
1. **Quadrant I — Do** (urgent & important): crises, deadlines (a system outage, a report due today). Handle first — but don't *live* here.
2. **Quadrant II — Plan** (not urgent & important): long-term growth and prevention (exercise, building a skill). **Schedule time here before it becomes urgent** — the highest-leverage quadrant.
3. **Quadrant III — Delegate** (urgent & not important): seems pressing but doesn't need *you* (non-critical emails, optional meetings). Delegate or automate.
4. **Quadrant IV — Eliminate** (not urgent & not important): low-value distractions (mindless scrolling, boredom-bingeing). Remove to reclaim focus.

## When to use it
- Triaging a task list or calendar; deciding what to drop.
- When you feel busy but not productive (likely stuck in Q I & III).

## Common mistakes
- Treating urgent as important (Q III masquerading as Q I).
- Neglecting Q II until it becomes a Q I crisis.
- Never eliminating Q IV.
