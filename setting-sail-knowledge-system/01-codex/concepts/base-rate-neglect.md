> Ignoring the general statistics (the base rate) and fixating on specific, vivid details — even when the stats matter more.

## What it is
Underweighting prior probabilities. "Base rate neglect makes rare things look common and
common things look rare."

## How it misleads
- **Medicine:** a 95%-accurate test for a 1-in-10,000 disease still means a positive is *probably* a false alarm — because the base rate dominates.
- **Law:** dramatic evidence outweighing crime statistics in jurors' minds.
- **Investing:** a few startup success stories obscuring the high base rate of failure.
- **Hiring:** two stars from one school → "all their grads are top talent."

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"What do the base rates say?"*
- Combine general statistics with specific details.
- Don't let one striking case overshadow the bigger picture.
