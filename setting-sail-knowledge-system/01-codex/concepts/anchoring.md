> Link an emotional state to a trigger — a gesture, a sound, a word — until the trigger can summon the feeling on command. A song that floods you with nostalgia is a natural anchor; you can also build them on purpose.

## Definition
"Anchoring means linking a specific emotional state to a trigger — something you see, hear,
or feel. Once the link is strong enough, the trigger can bring back that emotion
instantly." It happens naturally (a song → nostalgia, a smell → a place), but can be built
deliberately: clench your fist at a peak emotional moment, repeat, and eventually "just
clenching your fist can bring back that same power." The keys are **repetition and
emotional intensity**. Athletes and speakers use anchors "to feel more confident, focused,
calm, or motivated" on demand.

## Why it matters
It's a direct lever on state: instead of waiting to *feel* ready, you can *trigger*
readiness — "training your nervous system to respond on command." This complements the
memory work: where [[memory-as-editable-film]] reduces unwanted states, anchoring summons
wanted ones. The [[the-himba-song]] story extends it to identity — anchoring a person to
"who they are, not what they did wrong."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Anchors fade and can be unreliable** — without ongoing reinforcement, or under
  extreme stress, a fist-clench may not deliver. The book presents it more reliably than it often performs.
- **Evidence base is contested** (NLP techniques broadly); anecdotal/athletic use is
  strong, controlled support weaker.
- Negative anchors form just as easily (a trigger linked to panic) — anchoring is neutral, not automatically benevolent.

## Practical examples
- From the book: clenching a fist at peak power until the gesture restores the feeling.
- Beyond the book: a pre-performance ritual that reliably shifts a musician into "flow."

## Common mistakes
- Setting an anchor at low emotional intensity (weak link).
- Failing to reinforce it, then expecting it to work months later.
- Ignoring the negative anchors you've already built (and re-triggering them).
