> Extreme results tend to be followed by more average ones — highs and lows drift back toward the middle, with no special cause.

## What it is
The statistical pull of extremes back toward the average. "One extreme doesn't rewrite the
whole story."

## How it misleads
- **Sports:** a record-breaking game followed by a normal one — fans expect repeats; it just balanced out.
- **Business:** a lucky profit surge mistaken for a new trend.
- **Education:** an unusually high (or low) exam score, then a return to the student's true average.
- **Law:** treating a rare extreme case as a consistent pattern.

## How to counter it
- Don't overreact to extremes — look at long-term averages.
- Ask whether a big change is a real trend or a temporary spike.
- Remember unusual highs/lows usually self-correct.
