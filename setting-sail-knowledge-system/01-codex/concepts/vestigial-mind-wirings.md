> Vestigial traits aren't just physical — our minds run ancient tribal software too: sensitivity to criticism, status obsession, sugar cravings, fear of the dark, all once adaptive, now often misfiring.

## Definition
"Vestigial traits aren't just physical — they exist in our minds too. Our brains evolved
in small hunter-gatherer groups, and those ancient social patterns still shape how we
interact." The book catalogues these misfiring instincts:
- **Sensitivity to criticism / rejection** — group acceptance once meant survival, so a negative comment or a failed date triggers disproportionate pain.
- **Social-status obsession** — status meant resources and mates; now it fuels social-media comparison and materialism.
- **Hoarding & territorial instincts** — once survival; now clutter, overbuying, road rage, property disputes.
- **Xenophobia** — wariness of strangers (once dangerous) now feeds bias and resistance to the new.
- **Food wiring** — sugar cravings, high-calorie preference, bitter aversion: adaptive in scarcity, harmful amid abundance.
- **Fears** — of darkness, snakes, spiders; **blushing** and the **startle reflex** — protective once, often just awkward now.

## Why it matters
It's the mental half of [[evolutionary-mismatch]] and the deep root of much of the book:
the [[fight-or-flight|stress response]], [[success-failure-societal-script|social
scripts]], and many [[cognitive-bias|cognitive biases]] (Ch.7) are ancient wirings.
Naming them lets you [[becoming-the-observer|observe]] an instinct firing and decline to
obey it.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Adaptationist over-reach.** Labeling every modern discomfort a "tribal relic" is
  often speculative; culture and learning explain much of this too.
- **Some wirings still serve us** — criticism sensitivity supports cooperation; fear of
  snakes is sometimes accurate. The point is *calibration*, not deletion.

## Practical examples
- From the book: a single negative social-media comment feeling "disproportionately painful."
- Beyond the book: anxiety after one rejection despite "plenty of other opportunities."

## Common mistakes
- Treating these reactions as character flaws rather than ancient software.
- Obeying the instinct uncritically (e.g. letting status-comparison drive life choices).
