> Retreat is not escape but a return to yourself — solitude quiets the world's demands so your own voice becomes audible again.

## Definition
Stepping back from noise, people, and distraction so the "clutter fades" and you can
hear what you actually think, feel, and value. The book frames it as the method for
uncovering values: "not by chasing more, but by pausing. not by asking others, but
by asking yourself." It requires no monastery — a silent phone and a closed door
suffice. Nature amplifies the effect.

## Why it matters
Values and authentic preferences are drowned out by external demands. Solitude is the
*precondition* for [[defining-your-own-values]] and for hearing the [[inner-voice]].
It is how the [[lion-phase]] ends well — through reflective pause rather than endless
battle.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Solitude can become avoidance.** Withdrawal can be rumination or escape from
  relationships/responsibilities dressed as "self-work."
- **Humans are social.** Over-prized solitude conflicts with the book's own emphasis
  on connection and love ([[relationship-phases-dating-vs-longterm]]) — balance, not retreat as a lifestyle.

## Practical examples
- From the book: turn off the phone, close the door, breathe, ask "what do I care about? what drains me? what gives me energy?"
- Beyond the book: a solo walk in nature that resolves a decision a week of meetings couldn't.

## Common mistakes
- Filling "alone time" with screens — solitude requires actual disconnection.
- Mistaking loneliness (painful isolation) for chosen solitude (restorative).
- Never returning — retreat is meant to send you back with an insight.
