> The first financial goal: income greater than expenses. You don't need to get rich overnight — you just need to stop losing money each month.

## Definition
Positive cashflow means your income exceeds your expenses, leaving a surplus. The book
names it "your first financial goal" and gives exactly two levers to achieve it:
1. **Decrease spending** — cut habits without long-term value (daily coffee, unused subscriptions, impulse buys).
2. **Increase income** — better-paid jobs, a raise, a side hustle.

It is "the foundation for everything else — saving, investing, or getting out of debt."

## Why it matters
Without a surplus, none of the later steps (backup fund, investing, financial
independence) are possible — you're filling a leaking bucket. It reframes the goal
from the intimidating "get rich" to the achievable "stay in the green, even by a
little." Modest and reliable beats dramatic.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Income floor problem.** "Cut spending or earn more" assumes there's slack to cut or
  realistic ways to earn more; for the genuinely underpaid, neither lever moves easily.
- **Frugality has limits.** You cannot cut your way to wealth past a point; income
  growth (via [[money-follows-value]]) eventually matters more.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the two-lever choice — decrease spending or increase income.
- Beyond the book: canceling three unused subscriptions and redirecting the amount to a backup fund.

## Common mistakes
- Trying to invest or save while still running a monthly deficit.
- Focusing only on cutting (and feeling deprived) instead of also growing income.
