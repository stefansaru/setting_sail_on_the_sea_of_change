> Underestimating how long things take, how much they cost, and how hard they'll be — optimism clouds judgment, and reality shows up late.

## What it is
Systematic over-optimism in estimates. "The planning fallacy feeds on hope."

## How it misleads
- **Business:** the Sydney Opera House — planned for 4 years / $7M, took 14 years / $100M+.
- **Students:** "I'll finish this essay in one evening."
- **Personal:** "just fix one thing" turns into hours and mounting costs.
- **Law:** "this case will wrap up in months" → years of appeals and delays.

## How to counter it
- If your gut says double the estimate, "you might be closer to the truth if you double it."
- Look at how long *similar* projects actually took (the outside view), not best hopes.
- Plan for obstacles instead of assuming smooth progress.
