> People fear losing more than they value gaining — so framing something as a *loss* motivates harder than the same thing framed as a gain.

## What it is
Losses loom larger than equivalent gains. In [[the-makeup-artist]] (Ch.4 §4.19): the pitch
frames it as a *loss* — "you'll miss the chance to outshine your ex if you don't pay" —
which pushes harder than promising a gain.

## How it misleads
- **Marketing:** "don't miss out!" / "only 2 left!" — fear of loss driving the sale.
- **Investing:** holding losers too long to avoid "locking in" a loss (cf. [[sunk-cost-fallacy]]).
- **Everyday:** clinging to possessions to avoid the felt loss of letting go ([[endowment-effect]]).

## How to counter it
- Reframe in neutral terms: *what do I actually gain or lose in absolute terms?*
- Notice "loss" framing engineered to pressure you.
- Separate the *fear* of losing from the *facts* of the decision.

## Related concepts
- [[sunk-cost-fallacy]] — loss aversion keeps us throwing good money after bad.
- [[negativity-bias]] — bad/loss weighs heavier than good/gain.
