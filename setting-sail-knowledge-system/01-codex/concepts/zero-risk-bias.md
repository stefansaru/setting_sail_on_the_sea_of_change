> Preferring to eliminate a small risk completely over making a bigger dent in a large one — it feels safer, but doesn't make you safer.

## What it is
Choosing total removal of a minor risk over a larger reduction of overall danger.

## How it misleads
- **Health:** obsessing over a rare additive while ignoring diet, smoking, or inactivity.
- **Environment:** fully cleaning a minor pollutant while bigger threats stay underfunded.
- **Finance:** all money in "risk-free" savings, accepting the larger risk of inflation ([[money-illusion]]).
- **Law:** regulations that "guarantee safety" in one narrow area while broader risks remain.

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"What reduces the most **total** risk?"* — not what makes one risk vanish.
- Weigh risks by size and impact, not by eliminability.
- Accept that "zero risk is rare in life."
