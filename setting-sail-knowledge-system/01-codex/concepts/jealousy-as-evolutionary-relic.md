> Jealousy is ancient mate-guarding software — adaptive when a tribe held ~50 people, misfiring now that the dating pool is in the millions. The challenge isn't finding someone; it's understanding yourself.

## Definition
Jealousy — Shakespeare's [[the-green-eyed-monster|"green-eyed monster"]] — is "a built-in
survival feature, a relic from a time when securing a mate meant the difference between
passing on your genes or fading into evolutionary oblivion." In a small tribe, jealousy
was "your brain's built-in security system," ensuring "no one steals what you worked hard
to secure." But today the dating pool isn't 50 people — it's hundreds of thousands. "So
why does jealousy still exist when the pool is larger than ever? Because evolution
doesn't update as fast as technology." It's "ancient survival software, built for a world
of scarcity — not abundance," now expressing as "attachment, insecurity, and old
instincts misfiring."

## Why it matters
It's a worked case study in [[evolutionary-mismatch]] applied to relationships, and it
reframes a painful emotion: jealousy isn't a moral failing but outdated scarcity
software. The practical pivot: "the real challenge isn't finding someone — it's
understanding yourself," echoing [[fulfilling-life-beyond-the-relationship]] (Ch.3).

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-green-eyed-monster]] — the Shakespearean metaphor.
- [[evolutionary-mismatch]] — scarcity instinct in an abundant world.
- "You have more choices — and control — than your ancestors ever did."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Reductive on jealousy's causes.** Jealousy also stems from attachment history,
  insecurity, and real betrayal — not only "mate-guarding software." The evolutionary
  story is one lens, not the whole.
- **"Plenty of options" can dismiss real relational pain** or rationalize low commitment;
  understanding the instinct shouldn't excuse hurtful behavior.
- The "stone-age alpha male / dating math" framing is a stylized just-so story.

## Practical examples
- From the book: "next time jealousy whispers... you're not fighting over the last available mate in the tribe."
- Beyond the book: feeling threatened by a partner's friendship despite no actual risk.

## Common mistakes
- Treating jealousy as proof of love or as objective threat-detection.
- Acting on the instinct (controlling behavior) instead of observing it.
