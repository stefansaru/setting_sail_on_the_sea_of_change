> Your time and energy are a bookshelf, and you're the librarian. To add something new, something must go — clear what no longer serves you to make room for what does.

## Definition
Built on [[the-bookshelf-of-life]]: "every book represents a part of your life... want to
add something new? Something has to go." Decluttering applies to *time and energy*, not just
objects: swap 30 minutes of scrolling for a language lesson; trade passive consumption for a
neglected hobby. "Decluttering your time is about choosing what stays because it adds
meaning, and what goes because it no longer serves you." The book lists many **benefits of
decluttering** (§8.21: focus, calm, energy, time, sleep) and satirizes the "benefits" of
clutter (§8.22: procrastination paradise, living in the past, social isolation).

## Why it matters
It treats attention and time as finite shelf space — making "yes to this" always a "no to
that." It's the action arm of the [[pareto-principle]] (keep the vital few) and a defense
against [[comfortable-traps]] and the [[the-great-possession-obsession|possession obsession]].
The reframe — *you are the librarian* — restores agency over a crowded life.

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-bookshelf-of-life]] — the central metaphor.
- [[declutter-your-time]] — the swap technique.
- "The sum of all vices is constant" — drop one habit and another sneaks in; so *replace*, don't just remove.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Decluttering can become its own obsession** (or a way to avoid deeper issues by tidying).
- "Let it go" applied to relationships/commitments can rationalize avoiding hard repair work
  (cf. the persist-vs-leave tension, [[contradictions]] C2).

## Practical examples
- From the book: trading an hour of passive content for hands-on creative time.
- Beyond the book: cancelling commitments that drain you to protect time for what energizes.

## Common mistakes
- Removing a habit without replacing its function (it returns — see "sum of vices").
- Decluttering objects while ignoring cluttered time and attention.
