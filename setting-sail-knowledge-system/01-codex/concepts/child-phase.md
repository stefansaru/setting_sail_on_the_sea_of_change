> The stage of creation and play — saying "yes" to life and authoring your own values with the fresh eyes of a child, not a regression but spiritual maturity.

## Definition
The third and final metamorphosis. Having rejected inherited values (lion), the
individual now *creates* new ones aligned with their authentic self. The child sees
the world not as a battlefield but as a playground; the "thou-shalt" dragon becomes
something to explore rather than fight. Crucially, this is **not** a return to
childish naïveté but an *advance* to a state of spiritual maturity "driven by
unchained creativity."

## Why it matters
It is the book's answer to "freedom for what?" The lion frees you *from*; the child
shows what to do *with* that freedom — build a life on self-affirmed values, with
play, curiosity, forgiveness, and joyful affirmation. It resolves the dead-end of
permanent rebellion ([[negation-is-not-creation]]).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Why is the final phase called a beginning?"** The book itself flags the paradox:
  the child symbolizes a fresh start, yet it is the culmination. This is a feature
  (rebirth), but it strains the linear-stage framing.
- **Play can drift into avoidance.** "Seeing the world as a playground" risks dodging
  duty and consequence if untethered from values.
- Sustained joyful affirmation is an ideal; the book presents it as a destination but
  gives less guidance on relapses back to lion or camel.

## Practical examples
- From the book: Cammy reborn as "cammy the kid," crafting "new worlds with the pure joy of imagination"; the dragon now "a magnificent playground."
- Beyond the book: a founder who, past disillusionment, builds an organization expressing values they chose — energized rather than burdened by the work.

## Common mistakes
- Reading "child" as immaturity or naïveté rather than creative maturity.
- Trying to skip to the child phase without having done the lion's letting-go.
- Treating the phase as a permanent arrival rather than a practice ([[theme/journey-over-destination]]).
