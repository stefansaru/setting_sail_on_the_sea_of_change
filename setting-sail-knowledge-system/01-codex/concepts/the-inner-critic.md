> The internalized voice that polices your flaws around the clock — and, crucially, was originally someone else's voice before it became yours.

## Definition
The inner critic is "the voice in your head that seems to think its only job is to
keep pointing out your flaws... whispering doubts, replaying mistakes, and setting
unreasonably high standards." The book's key insight, via Alain de Botton, is that it
is *not native*: "an inner voice is an outer voice that got internalized." The critic
is the residue of past criticism — parents, teachers, culture — now running on
autopilot inside you.

## Why it matters
Recognizing the critic as an *internalized external voice* is liberating: it means the
voice is not "the truth about you," and not even originally *yours*. That distance is
the first step to dialing it down. Left unexamined, the critic drives
[[perfectionism-as-a-mirage|perfectionism]], erodes [[self-esteem]], and poisons your
self-talk with [[limiting-words]].

## Supporting ideas
- Alain de Botton: "an inner voice is an outer voice that got internalized."
- [[self-compassion]] — the antidote: replace the critic's voice with a friend's.
- Contrast with the [[inner-voice]] (Ch.3): the *inner voice* is your authentic truth; the *inner critic* is a borrowed accuser. They must not be confused.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Some self-criticism is useful.** A calibrated internal editor catches real errors;
  the goal is to demote the critic, not silence all self-evaluation.
- **"It's not really yours" can deflect responsibility.** Externalizing the critic
  shouldn't become an excuse to ignore genuine feedback (see [[self-esteem]]: healthy
  esteem stays *open* to criticism).

## Practical examples
- From the book: the critic that "moves the goalpost" so "no matter what you achieve, it can't be satisfied."
- Beyond the book: hearing a parent's exact dismissive phrase in your own head decades later.

## Common mistakes
- Treating the critic's verdicts as objective fact.
- Confusing the harsh inner *critic* with the quiet inner *voice* of authentic values.
- Trying to defeat the critic with more criticism ("you shouldn't be so self-critical!").
