> Three concrete routes to meaning: what you create, what you experience, and — most powerfully — the attitude you take toward unavoidable suffering.

## Definition
Frankl's "special offers" in [[the-existential-supermarket]] — three ways meaning is
actually realized:
- **Creative values** — "meaning through creation, whether it's art, innovation, or personal growth" (what you *give to* the world).
- **Experiential values** — "meaning through experiencing beauty, whether in nature, music, or human connection" (what you *receive from* the world).
- **Attitudinal values** — "meaning through how we respond to suffering, transforming hardship into strength" (the stance you take toward what you *cannot change*).

## Why it matters
It makes [[the-will-to-meaning]] actionable by naming *where* to find meaning. The
third path is the most profound: even when creation and experience are stripped away
(Frankl's insight from the camps), meaning remains available in *how you respond* — the
one freedom that can never be taken. This is the deepest link between the book's
meaning stratum and its [[stoicism]].

## Supporting ideas
- Attitudinal values ↔ [[stoicism]] / [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]]: meaning in your response to the uncontrollable.
- Creative values ↔ [[doing-what-you-love-is-loving-the-mission]] and the [[child-phase]]'s creation.
- Experiential values ↔ presence and savoring (the "enjoyment" of [[pure-model]]).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Meaning in suffering" can be misused** to valorize or impose suffering (cf.
  [[the-three-meaning-traps|the suffering trap]]). Frankl's point is meaning in
  *unavoidable* suffering — not that suffering should be sought.
- The three paths can feel like a list rather than a method; the book doesn't say how to
  cultivate each.

## Practical examples
- From the book: transforming hardship into strength (attitudinal); beauty in nature/music (experiential); art and growth (creative).
- Beyond the book: a person facing illness who finds meaning not in the disease but in the courage and love with which they meet it.

## Common mistakes
- Reading "attitudinal values" as "suffering is good" rather than "you can find meaning in how you bear what you can't avoid."
- Pursuing only creative/experiential values and feeling defeated when life removes them.
