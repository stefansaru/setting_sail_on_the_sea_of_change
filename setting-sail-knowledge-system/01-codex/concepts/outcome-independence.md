> Care about the goal and work hard for it — but detach emotionally from the result, so no single outcome can derail you.

## Definition
Disappointment comes from "the gap between what we hoped would happen and what actually
unfolded" — and "the more specific the expectation, the greater the risk of
frustration." Outcome independence is the discipline of detaching *emotionally* from a
result while staying fully committed to the *process*: "you prepare for both success
and setbacks, knowing that either way, you'll be okay." It is **not** apathy — "it
doesn't mean you stop caring or putting in effort."

A key implementation is **building redundancy**: diversify across areas of life so "no
single failure has the power to derail you" — multiple income streams, diversified
investments, backups (see [[diversify-for-resilience]], [[dont-put-all-eggs-in-one-basket]]).

## Why it matters
Tying your emotional state to one specific outcome manufactures unnecessary stress and
makes you brittle. Outcome independence is the practical, everyday form of
[[stoicism]] and of [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]]: control the effort and the
response, release the result. It is also a structural strategy (redundancy), not just
a mindset.

## Supporting ideas
- [[stoicism]] — focus only on what you can control; the result often isn't it.
- [[diversify-for-resilience]] — the technique that engineers outcome independence into your life.
- [[why-investing-matters]] — "don't put all your eggs in one basket" applied to money.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Detachment can dampen drive.** For some, emotional stakes *fuel* performance;
  over-detaching risks half-effort. The book's caveat ("don't stop caring") acknowledges
  this but the balance is delicate.
- **Some outcomes genuinely matter and warrant grief** if missed; "you'll be okay
  either way" can ring hollow for high-stakes, irreplaceable goals.

## Practical examples
- From the book: financial redundancy — "if you lose your job, how long can you sustain yourself? ... spread your risk."
- Beyond the book: applying to many jobs/opportunities so one rejection isn't catastrophic.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing outcome independence with not caring or not trying.
- Staking your whole identity/finances/happiness on a single result (one job, one deal, one person).
- Detaching from the *process* (effort) instead of just the *result*.
