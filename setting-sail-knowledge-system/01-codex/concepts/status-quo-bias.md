> Preferring things to stay as they are — the comfort of "what I've always done" over exploring something better.

## What it is
A default toward the current state. In [[the-makeup-artist]] (Ch.4 §4.19): the artist
"stays fixed on her pricing method, preferring the comfort of 'what I've always done' over
exploring new strategies."

## How it misleads
- **Work:** keeping an inefficient process because changing it feels risky.
- **Finance:** never switching a worse plan/provider out of inertia.
- **Life:** staying in familiar-but-unfulfilling situations (a [[comfortable-traps|comfort trap]]).

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"If I were choosing fresh today, would I pick this?"* (the same test that defeats [[sunk-cost-fallacy]]).
- Treat "no change" as an active choice that must also be justified.
- Run small experiments to lower the perceived risk of change.
