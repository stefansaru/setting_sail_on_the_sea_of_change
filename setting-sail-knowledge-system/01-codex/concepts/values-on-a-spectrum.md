> Don't write your values in black and white — most live on a scale, with room for compromises, dealbreakers, and context, as long as they stay true to your core.

## Definition
A refinement of [[defining-your-own-values]]. A *binary* value is a fixed yes/no
answer to a question. But "very few things are so clearly defined." An effective
value should exist on a *spectrum* that allows nuance — including exceptions and
context — while remaining anchored to a core belief. A few values may be absolute
(crystal-clear dealbreakers); most are grayscale. Values are best written in the
*present tense* as a personal code of conduct.

## Why it matters
Rigidly binary values shatter on contact with real life and lead to hypocrisy or
paralysis. Putting values on a scale makes them robust and *livable* — they bend
without breaking. It also encodes the child-phase capacity to hold nuance rather
than the lion's all-or-nothing stance.

## Supporting ideas
- The present-tense framing: "your values, written in the present tense, form your personal code of conduct."
- A few **absolute** values are legitimate; the point is not to make *everything* gray.
- [[tolerating-conflicting-beliefs]] — the maturity to hold nuance (see [[sparks-of-creation]]).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Too much gray = no spine.** Endlessly "it depends" values can rationalize
  anything; some lines should be bright. The skill is knowing which.
- **Spectrum thinking can erode commitment** if every value comes with an escape clause.

## Practical examples
- From the book (binary): "i value hard work and success that is earned. i do not value success that comes from inheritance… or sheer luck."
- From the book (grayscale): valuing financial literacy, "but i don't take financial advice from someone with less money than me… i prefer insights from those in a similar situation."

## Common mistakes
- Stating every value as an absolute, then constantly violating it.
- The opposite: hedging every value until it commits to nothing.
- Forgetting to mark the few genuine dealbreakers.
