> Not all trauma is a catastrophe. Big-T trauma is severe and life-altering; little-t trauma is the smaller hurts — rejection, failure — that accumulate over time and quietly shape who you become.

## Definition
From Gabor Maté ([[gabor-mate]]): when an obstacle "has shaped you so deeply that it alters
how you see the world, it becomes trauma," of two types:
- **Little-t trauma** — "smaller, yet still painful experiences — rejection, failure, or
  disappointment." Individually minor, but "repeated over time, they accumulate and shape
  self-perception."
- **Big-T trauma** — "severe, life-altering events — loss, abuse, or major crises... deep
  scars that affect thoughts, emotions, and even physical health."

"Whether big or small, unresolved obstacles continue to influence daily life."

## Why it matters
It validates the cumulative weight of "small" hurts that people often dismiss ("it wasn't
that bad"), and it frames healing as *processing*, not erasing: "acknowledge their impact,
learn from them, and take small steps toward healing... free yourself from its hold, so it
no longer dictates your future." Connects [[emotional-baggage]] and [[most-fears-are-memories]]
to the editing tools of Ch.6.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Self-processing has limits.** Big-T trauma typically needs professional care; "small
  steps toward healing" understates that (see [[gaps]]).
- Broadening "trauma" to include everyday disappointment is clinically debated — useful for
  compassion, but risks diluting the term.

## Practical examples
- From the book: repeated rejections accumulating into a fragile self-perception (little-t).
- Beyond the book: a single major loss reshaping someone's entire worldview (big-T).

## Common mistakes
- Dismissing little-t trauma because no single event was "serious."
- Trying to self-process big-T trauma without support.
