> The quiet whisper of what you genuinely value — barely audible at first, but unmistakable when an answer resonates as your own truth.

## Definition
The internal faculty that answers the question "how do I stand?" when external noise
is stripped away. The book describes it as "a quiet whisper from within, barely
audible at first" — and the moment an answer "resonates deeply, one that you wholly
agree with," that is your value, your truth. In the [[camel-phase]] this voice is
"silenced by the loud demands of external obligations."

## Why it matters
It is the source of authentic values. Self-authorship is impossible without learning
to hear and trust this voice over inherited "shoulds." It is dramatized in the book
as [[the-magical-mirror]] that speaks to Cammy.

## Supporting ideas
- [[solitude-for-self-discovery]] — the condition that makes the whisper audible.
- [[defining-your-own-values]] — what the inner voice produces.
- Resonance as the test: truth "feels" agreed-with, not merely reasoned.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **The inner voice can mislead.** Intuition is shaped by bias, trauma, and
  conditioning; "what resonates" isn't automatically wise (cf. the cognitive-bias
  chapter, Ch.7). The book under-weights this risk.
- **Resonance vs. rationalization.** A comfortable answer can feel like "truth" while
  just being the path of least resistance.

## Practical examples
- From the book: asking "how do I stand?" about a value and listening for the answer that resonates.
- Beyond the book: a decision that "looks right on paper" but produces persistent inner unease — the voice dissenting.

## Common mistakes
- Drowning the voice in busyness and screens.
- Outsourcing the answer to others' opinions.
- Trusting every gut feeling as the inner voice without checking it against reason and values.
