> Stop competing on price; compete on value. Understand what people deeply need — often an emotion — and meet it, and you become irreplaceable.

## Definition
The practical companion to [[money-follows-value]]. To earn more, create more value —
and value is frequently emotional ("security, confidence, peace of mind"), not just
the literal product. The book's five levers:
1. **Understand needs** — what clients *really* want, "not just on the surface."
2. **Communicate clearly** — people buy "with logic but also with emotion."
3. **Improve your skills** — better skill makes you "more unique and irreplaceable."
4. **Be flexible** — don't shut down budget conversations; show why more costs more.
5. **Build relationships** — "people pay more when they feel seen and understood."

Illustrated by [[the-makeup-artist]]: clients "don't just buy makeup. they buy
confidence, pride, and the feeling of being unforgettable."

## Why it matters
It shifts the earning game from a race to the bottom (cheapest wins) to differentiation
(most valuable wins). Understanding the deeper, often emotional need is what lets you
"stand out because of the value you bring" rather than competing on price alone.

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-makeup-artist]] — the worked example of reframing price as value/emotion.
- [[money-follows-value]] — the principle this operationalizes.
- The book's own caution: persuasion built only on emotional hooks "can cross into manipulation."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Value-selling vs. manipulation.** The same techniques (anchoring to emotion, loss
  aversion) can mislead; the book explicitly dissects the makeup-artist pitch for its
  fallacies and biases. Ethical value-creation requires honesty, not just persuasion.
- **Not everything should be monetized** by maximizing perceived emotional value;
  some relationships and goods are corroded by transactional framing.

## Practical examples
- From the book: reframing "800 rand" as "is it worth 380 to feel incredible?"
- Beyond the book: a consultant selling "peace of mind / risk removed" rather than "hours of work."

## Common mistakes
- Listing features instead of the outcome/feeling the client actually buys.
- Competing only on being cheapest.
- Crossing from honest value-framing into manipulation (false dilemmas, fear-based pressure).
