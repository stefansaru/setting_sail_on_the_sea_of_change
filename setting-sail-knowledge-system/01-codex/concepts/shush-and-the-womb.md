> "Shhh" calms babies across every culture because it mimics the constant whoosh of the womb — the first soundscape we ever knew.

## Definition
The sound "shush"/"shhh" "is recognized across cultures and languages, carrying the same
message — calm down, be still." Its power "comes from the womb, where the fetus is
surrounded by a steady whoosh — the sound of amniotic fluid moving, blood flowing, and the
rhythmic pulse of the mother's heartbeat." This "constant white noise is the first
soundscape a baby knows." When a parent whispers "shush," they "unknowingly mimic the
whooshing sounds of the womb, triggering a familiar sense of security."

## Why it matters
A small but striking example that some of our deepest responses are *prenatal* and
universal — evidence of shared, inherited wiring beneath cultural difference. It also
hints at a constructive use of our wiring (sound to regulate emotion), pointing toward
[[using-music-to-shape-emotions]] (Ch.6).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Mechanism is plausible but not fully settled** — the womb-mimicry explanation is the
  popular account; white noise's calming effect may have several causes.

## Practical examples
- From the book: parents instinctively shushing a crying newborn.
- Beyond the book: white-noise machines and "womb sound" recordings used to settle babies.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming it's purely a learned cultural convention rather than a primal response.
