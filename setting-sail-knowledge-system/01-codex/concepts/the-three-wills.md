> Three drives compete to fill the void — the will to pleasure, the will to power, and the will to meaning — but only the last one actually nourishes.

## Definition
Framed as three aisles in [[the-existential-supermarket]]:
- **The will to pleasure** (Aisle 1) — "instant gratification... entertainment, indulgence, the endless scroll." Not bad, but "the fast food of meaning — tasty but not sustaining"; pursued alone, you "leave with a cart full of empty calories."
- **The will to power** (Aisle 2) — "titles, influence, status... things that make us feel in control." Ambition "drives progress, but when power becomes an end in itself, it's like stacking blocks on a foundation of sand — impressive, but fragile."
- **The will to meaning** (Aisle 3) — "purposeful work, deep relationships, and the courage to face challenges." Unlike the others, "what we find here doesn't just fill our carts — it nourishes us."

The three correspond to the historic psychologies of Freud (pleasure), Adler (power),
and [[viktor-frankl|Frankl]] (meaning).

## Why it matters
It diagnoses *why* people stay hungry despite "filling their carts": they shop the
wrong aisles. Pleasure and power aren't evil — they're just nutritionally incomplete.
The model gives a quick test for any pursuit: does this *fill* me (pleasure/power) or
*nourish* me (meaning)?

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Pleasure and power can be meaningful**, not merely instrumental — the model's
  hierarchy (meaning > power > pleasure) is a value claim, not a fact; a life of joyful
  pleasure or principled power can be deeply meaningful.
- **Tidy taxonomy, messy reality.** Real motivation blends all three; sorting pursuits
  into one aisle oversimplifies.

## Practical examples
- From the book: doom-scrolling (pleasure) and title-chasing (power) leaving you hungry; contribution and deep relationships (meaning) satisfying.
- Beyond the book: a promotion (power) that feels hollow vs. mentoring someone (meaning) that doesn't.

## Common mistakes
- Treating pleasure or power as the *destination* rather than seasoning/means.
- Moralizing pleasure away entirely (it "adds sweetness to life").
