> Relationships move from a biologically-driven dating rush to a chosen long-term bond — and what was spontaneous effort must become intentional.

## Definition
The book models romantic relationships as two phases. The **dating phase** is
"excitement, discovery, and intense emotions," where decisions feel "more emotional
than logical" — a biological response prioritizing reproduction. This rush "doesn't
last forever." The **long-term phase** is when "daily routines replace intentional
effort," and "what was once a deliberate choice… becomes an expectation, a habit, a
shared life." The key insight: effort that was automatic in dating must become
*intentional* later.

## Why it matters
Many relationships fail not from incompatibility but from expecting the dating-phase
rush to sustain a long-term bond. Naming the transition lets couples deliberately
supply (through intention) what biology used to supply for free.

## Supporting ideas
- [[values-alignment-in-relationships]] — what actually sustains the long-term phase.
- [[relationship-as-brickwork]] — the metaphor for how long-term bonds are built or eroded.
- The book's long-term principles: learn each other's love language, plan quality time, communicate openly, argue constructively, heal emotional wounds.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Reductive on attraction.** Framing dating purely as a "biological response… 
  prioritizing reproduction" under-describes intimacy, companionship, and non-reproductive bonds.
- **"Chasing the thrill" judged harshly.** Some people's values genuinely favor novelty;
  the book treats the long-term bond as the mature default.

## Practical examples
- From the book: spontaneity in dating must become *scheduled* quality time in long-term relationships.
- Beyond the book: a couple instituting a weekly "date night" precisely because the spontaneous version stopped happening.

## Common mistakes
- Mistaking the fading of the dating rush for the death of love.
- Expecting a partner to "mind-read" needs that were obvious during the heightened dating phase.
- Treating arguments as combat rather than problem-solving ("partners are not emotional punching bags").
