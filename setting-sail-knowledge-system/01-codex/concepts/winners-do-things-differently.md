> Success has two sides — external (outcomes) and internal (mindset). The real win is internal: a clear, focused mind that sees opportunities and acts. "Winners don't do different things; winners do things differently."

## Definition
Success "has both an external and internal side." **External** success often looks
effortless but hides years of work or timing; clarity to spot and act on opportunities
"comes from a calm, focused mind — not from being stressed and burned out." **Internal**
success is mindset: "overcoming fear, clearing doubt, getting past the noise that clouds
judgment." The deeper battle is "the self-talk that says you're not good enough... the
limiting beliefs... the memories that still drain you." The chapter imports Tony Robbins'
([[tony-robbins]]) **7 beliefs of high performers**: (1) everything serves us, (2) there's no
failure, only results, (3) take responsibility, (4) you needn't understand everything to use
it, (5) people are your greatest resource, (6) work is play, (7) lasting success requires commitment.

## Why it matters
It relocates success from circumstance to *mind* — clarity, focus, and belief — which is
where the book's tools operate ([[becoming-the-observer]], memory work, debiasing). It frames
the inner game as the lever for the outer results, and previews much of the book.

## Supporting ideas
- [[tony-robbins]] — the 7 convictions (from *Unlimited Power*).
- A clear mind "sees what others don't"; overwhelm makes you miss things.
- [[taking-responsibility]] / [[self-esteem]] — pillars of the internal game.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Under-weights luck and structure.** "Internal success determines external" can shade into
  the bootstrap myth (cf. [[gaps|G6/G10]]) — timing, privilege, and luck matter too (the book even nods to "good timing").
- The 7 beliefs are motivational heuristics, not validated laws (e.g. "there is no failure" can deny real, costly failures).

## Practical examples
- From the book: "a millionaire buying a yacht isn't surprising. But someone who had nothing... and built a life — is different." The win was internal.
- Beyond the book: two people facing the same setback — one sees feedback, one sees proof of inadequacy.

## Common mistakes
- Chasing external markers while neglecting the inner game.
- Reading "no failure, only results" as license to ignore real consequences.
