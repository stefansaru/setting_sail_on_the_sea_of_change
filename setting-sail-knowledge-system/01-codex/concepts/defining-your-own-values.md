> A value is what something means to you and you alone — found by stripping away external expectation and listening for what resonates deeply.

## Definition
The creative act of the [[child-phase]]: rather than accepting (camel) or rejecting
(lion) values, you author your own. The method is subtractive — strip away
"external expectations, societal norms, and family pressures" — and then receptive:
listen for the quiet inner answer that you "wholly agree with." There is "no
universal template," and a value "doesn't need to make sense to anyone else but you."
The book sketches domains to examine: financial, social, health/lifestyle,
knowledge/learning, freedom/security.

## Why it matters
Values are the heading the [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind|sailor]] steers toward;
[[define-values-before-setting-course|defined first]], they make goals coherent and
obstacles surmountable. Self-defined values are the difference between an authentic
life and a borrowed one.

## Supporting ideas
- [[inner-voice]] — the faculty that supplies the answer ("a quiet whisper from within").
- [[solitude-for-self-discovery]] — the condition that makes the inner voice audible.
- [[values-on-a-spectrum]] — most values are grayscale, not binary.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Doesn't need to make sense to anyone else" risks solipsism.** Purely private
  values can rationalize harm; the book's later [[intention-in-context]] (Ch.10) and
  game-theoretic chapters push back toward values that account for others.
- **Discovery vs. decision.** The chapter frames values as *listened for* (found),
  but elsewhere as *created* (child phase) — found vs. authored is an unresolved
  tension. See [[contradictions]].

## Practical examples
- From the book's domains: risk vs. stability (financial), altruism vs. self-interest (social), longevity vs. enjoyment (health), routine vs. spontaneity (freedom).
- Beyond the book: writing a one-page personal code you'd stand by even if no one applauded.

## Common mistakes
- Listing values you think you *should* have (re-importing the camel's "shoulds").
- Copying a popular framework wholesale instead of listening.
- Skipping solitude, so the loudest external voice wins.
