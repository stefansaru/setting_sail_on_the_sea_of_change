> Judging a decision only by how it turned out, not by how sound it was when made — letting luck masquerade as skill.

## What it is
Evaluating choices by results rather than by the quality of reasoning at the time. "Outcome
bias makes hindsight look like foresight."

## How it misleads
- **Business:** a lucky risky bet praised as genius; a sound choice that fails (bad luck) blamed.
- **Medicine:** the right treatment that doesn't work judged a mistake; a reckless gamble that succeeds judged skill.
- **Sports:** a statistically good call that fails draws "bad decision!"
- **Law/debate:** "my client was hurt, so someone's to blame" — results over evidence.

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"Was this the best choice based on what was known at the time?"*
- Separate **decision quality** from **outcome luck**.
- Judge by process, not just results.
