> **Synthesis page — not in the book.** A lens for reading the book's contradictions: most are not errors but *polarity pairs* — two truths that each become correct under different conditions. Add the missing dimension and the contradiction dissolves.

## The idea
Most self-help tries to *eliminate* contradictions. Real life contains them. The book's
[[contradictions|logged tensions]] are, in fact, among its most valuable content — because
nearly every one dissolves when you introduce a **missing dimension**:
- **stage of development** — true at one stage, false at another (e.g. discover *then* create);
- **time horizon** — true short-term, false long-term (or vice versa);
- **context** — true in one situation, false in another;
- **level of abstraction** — the two claims refer to *different things* under one word.

What looks like "A vs. B" is usually "A *and* B, under different conditions." These are
**polarities to manage**, not problems to solve — you don't pick a side, you oscillate or
locate which condition you're in.

## Why it matters
It converts the book's apparent inconsistencies from weaknesses into depth. It also gives a
reusable diagnostic: when you hit a contradiction (in the book or your life), ask *"what
dimension is missing that would make both true?"* This is the [[tolerating-conflicting-beliefs|child-phase
capacity]] to hold opposites, turned into a method — and the engine behind every synthesis
page in this codex.

## The method (how to resolve a polarity)
1. State both poles plainly (A and B).
2. Ask which **missing dimension** is hiding (stage / time / context / level).
3. Re-state each pole *with its condition* ("A when…, B when…").
4. Name the **synthesis** (how to hold both) and the **two failure modes** (too much of either pole).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Not every contradiction is a polarity.** Some are genuine errors or false claims that
  *should* be rejected, not "balanced." Polarity thinking can become a lazy "both sides"
  reflex that dodges hard judgments. The test: do both poles have real truth under some condition? If only one does, it's not a polarity.
- Over-applied, it can rationalize never committing (cf. [[tolerating-conflicting-beliefs]] tensions).
