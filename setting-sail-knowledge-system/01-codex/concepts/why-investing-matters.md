> Cash sitting still loses value to inflation; investing protects and can grow your money — but the best first investment is in your own financial education.

## Definition
Once a [[backup-fund]] is in place, you invest the surplus — because of **inflation**:
"prices slowly rise over time, and your money loses value if it just sits there. What
100 coins can buy today might only buy 90 coins' worth next year." Investing protects
money and can grow it "even while you sleep." The book's rules of prudence:
- **Diversify** — "don't keep all your eggs in one basket" (stocks, bonds, real estate, etc.). See [[dont-put-all-eggs-in-one-basket]].
- **Don't invest your backup fund** — keep it safe and accessible.
- **Don't chase quick wins** — those are high-risk; think long-term and lower-risk.

The headline lesson, from a seminar's "$1,000 portfolio": "if you have $1000 and don't
know where to invest it, then invest it in your financial education." Without
understanding fundamentals and risks, "you're not really investing; you're just
gambling." **The best first investment is in yourself.**

## Why it matters
It corrects the intuitive but costly belief that cash is "safe" (inflation makes it
slowly unsafe) and the opposite error of reckless speculation. It also subordinates
investing to *knowledge*: literacy first, returns second.

## Supporting ideas
- Inflation as the reason not to hold everything in cash.
- [[dont-put-all-eggs-in-one-basket]] — diversification as risk control.
- "Learn first. Before you start to grow your money, grow your knowledge."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Invest in education" can be a stalling tactic.** Endless learning without ever
  starting is its own failure mode; at some point small, diversified action teaches more than study.
- **Diversification reduces both risk and upside** — fine for most, but the book
  presents low-risk/long-term as nearly universal advice, glossing over individual goals and timelines.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the $1,000 strategy = invest in financial education.
- Beyond the book: a beginner reading and starting with broad, low-cost index funds rather than a hot single stock.

## Common mistakes
- Holding everything in cash and quietly losing to inflation ([[money-illusion]], forward link — Ch.7).
- Chasing quick wins / hype with money you can't afford to lose.
- Investing in things you don't understand — gambling, not investing.
