> Real balance isn't ignoring the bad to feel good, or fixating on the bad and forgetting the beauty — it's holding both joy and pain in awareness at once, and then choosing your intention.

## Definition
Using the dinner-party [[the-intention-donut|doughnut]] image: the same evening holds a
"feel-good" arrow (connection, joy, warmth) and a "feel-bad" arrow (the wine bottle in
the wrong bin, the steak's suffering, a friend's hidden struggle). "Same table. Same
people. Two completely different perspectives." Zoom out and both sit on a bigger
doughnut whose arrow is **the intention of balance** — "the arrow of equilibrium." Real
awareness means you "don't only focus on the good while pretending the bad doesn't
exist," and you "don't only see the bad and forget the beauty." You hold both, become
present, and "choose your own" intention.

## Why it matters
It is the perceptual foundation of [[intention-in-context]]: you can only choose well in
a situation you see *fully*. It inoculates against both [[toxic-positivity]] (denying
the shadow) and despair (denying the light), and it operationalizes the
[[tolerating-conflicting-beliefs|child-phase capacity to hold opposites]] from Ch.3.

## Supporting ideas
- [[yin-yang-light-and-shadow]] — "every light casts a shadow; every shadow has a spark of light."
- Carl Jung: "the brighter the light, the darker the shadow."
- [[the-intention-donut]] — the model that nests good, bad, and balance.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Balance can become passivity.** Always holding "both sides" can blur into moral
  neutrality — some situations call for clearly naming the bad and acting against it,
  not equanimously balancing it.
- **"Both are true" has limits.** Not every shadow has redeeming light; insisting it
  does can minimize real harm or injustice.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the joyful dinner that is *also* wasteful, ethically complicated, and quietly painful for someone present.
- Beyond the book: holding genuine gratitude for a job *and* clear sight of what's wrong with it, instead of pretending either away.

## Common mistakes
- Toxic positivity: focusing only on the good, suppressing the bad.
- The opposite: cynicism that sees only the broken parts.
- Mistaking "awareness of both" for indecision — it's a prelude to *choosing*, not a refusal to choose.
