> The event is neutral; your *attitude toward* the event creates the experience. The same champagne pop delights one person and annoys another.

## Definition
"The problem is not the problem; the problem is your attitude towards the problem." A
champagne bottle pops at a party — identical for everyone, yet "some cheer and get
excited, while others get upset or annoyed." The event is fixed; the *interpretation*
varies, "based on past experiences and feelings." The book illustrates with Roy Sullivan
(struck by lightning seven times and survived): "incredibly unlucky... or remarkably
lucky?" — "it all depends on your perspective." As Shakespeare put it: "there is nothing
either good or bad, but thinking makes it so."

## Why it matters
It is the cognitive core of the book's [[stoicism]] and of
[[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]]: since your reaction comes from your interpretation,
not the event, agency lives in the framing. "Life's events are like a blank painting" —
you choose the colors. This is what makes reframing, [[becoming-the-observer]], and
emotional work possible.

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-map-is-not-the-territory]] — your map (interpretation), not the territory (event), drives the reaction.
- [[minds-eye-movies]] — the "movie in your mind about what's happening" colors the event.
- Stoic/Shakespearean lineage: "thinking makes it so."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Some events are genuinely bad**, full stop — abuse, injustice, tragedy. "It's all
  perspective" can minimize real harm and slide into victim-blaming ("just reframe it").
- **Reframing has limits** in acute trauma or clinical states (cf. [[depression-vs-low-self-esteem]]); attitude is powerful but not omnipotent.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the champagne pop greeted with cheers or irritation; Roy Sullivan as lucky or unlucky.
- Beyond the book: a flight delay experienced as ruined plans vs. unexpected reading time.

## Common mistakes
- Blaming the situation instead of examining your interpretation of it.
- Weaponizing "it's just your attitude" to dismiss others' legitimate pain.
- Assuming everyone experiences the "same" event the same way.
