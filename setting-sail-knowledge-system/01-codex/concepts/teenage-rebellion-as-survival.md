> Teenage rebellion isn't just being difficult — it's an ancient drive to leave the tribe and find genetically diverse partners, hardwired because those who stayed and inbred didn't survive.

## Definition
Teenage rebellion is "as universal as puberty itself" — and the book argues it's an
*evolutionary survival strategy*. In small ancestral tribes, "staying within the tribe
meant a dangerously small dating pool"; mating with relatives "increased the risk of
genetic defects." So those who "stayed loyal, followed the rules, and didn't seek new
horizons" left weaker lineages, while "the rebels" who "challenged authority, rejected
their familiar surroundings, and ventured beyond the tribe" found "genetically diverse
partners" and passed on that rebellious streak. Hence: "when a teenager questions
authority, it echoes an ancient drive to break free from their tribe."

## Why it matters
It reframes a maddening life stage as a deep adaptive program — useful for parents and
teens alike. It also links the evolutionary inheritance stratum to the self-authorship
arc: this built-in "push to leave" is the biological seed of the [[lion-phase|lion's]]
rebellion against inherited values, and the reason [[internalizing-the-exterior|absorbed
childhood values]] start getting questioned at puberty.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **A strong just-so story.** The inbreeding-avoidance account of adolescent rebellion is
  plausible but not settled science; rebellion also has clear social/developmental causes.
- **Risks romanticizing defiance** — not all teenage rebellion is adaptive
  boundary-seeking; some is harmful, and "it's evolution" can excuse poor choices.

## Practical examples
- From the book: "when they seek independence, it's the same instinct that pushed their ancestors to leave home."
- Beyond the book: a teen suddenly rejecting the family's politics/religion — differentiation, not mere contrariness.

## Common mistakes
- Reading teenage rebellion as purely personal defiance rather than a developmental drive.
- Using the evolutionary frame to dismiss legitimate things a teen is actually right about.
