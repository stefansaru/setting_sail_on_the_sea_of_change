> The title you give a memory decides whether you replay it or stop at its lesson. Train your mind to loop helpful conclusions instead of distressing details.

## Definition
Old memories "keep playing over and over... bringing back the same emotions" unless we take
control. The lever is the **title/conclusion** assigned to a memory (from
[[diffusing-visual-memories]]'s final step): "if the title is right — if it reflects a
lesson, a resolution, or a shift in perspective — then there's no need to relive the
details. The mind sees the label, understands the takeaway, and moves on." So "instead of
looping painful moments, we train our mind to loop helpful conclusions."

## Why it matters
It explains *why* re-titling works and turns memory from a source of recurring pain into a
"library of growth — not of suffering." It's the maintenance habit that keeps the
[[memory-as-editable-film|editing]] permanent: with practice "the mind instinctively plays
the edited version."

## Supporting ideas
- [[diffusing-visual-memories]] — re-titling is its final, crucial step.
- [[hall-of-fame-for-memories]] — the gallery where well-titled memories rest.
- "Choose your titles carefully. They define whether a memory is a lingering wound or a lesson well learned."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **A premature "lesson" can be a bypass.** Slapping a tidy conclusion on a raw wound can
  suppress rather than resolve it; some memories need to be *felt* before they can be
  honestly re-titled.
- Reframes that are inaccurate ("it was all for the best") may not hold and can backfire.

## Practical examples
- From the book: "painful breakup" → "freedom and new beginnings," so the mind reads the label and lets go.
- Beyond the book: filing a failed project as "the experiment that taught me X" so recalling it brings insight, not shame.

## Common mistakes
- Re-titling without genuinely extracting/accepting the lesson (hollow label).
- Replaying details out of habit instead of trusting the conclusion.
