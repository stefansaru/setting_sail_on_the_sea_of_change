> Memories are films that replay their original emotions — so the ones you choose to watch on repeat shape how you feel right now.

## Definition
"Memories are like old films playing in the theater of your mind." When a memory
surfaces it doesn't merely remind — it "brings back the emotions, making you feel as if
it's happening right now." Like watching only the sad scenes of a film, "the ones we
replay most often shape how we feel in the present." The book's stick-not-a-snake
example: your mind "projected a moment of past experience" and produced real fear at no
real danger. (In dreams, the "logic filters" switch off entirely, letting raw
emotion/imagination run the film.)

## Why it matters
It reframes memory as an *active emotional choice*, not a passive record: "revisiting
positive memories strengthens confidence and happiness, while reliving painful ones can
keep you stuck." This is the conceptual foundation for the emotional-diffusion
techniques of Ch.6 — if memories are editable films, you can change which you replay and
how they feel.

## Supporting ideas
- [[becoming-the-observer]] — noticing *which* film is playing lets you change the channel.
- The stick-not-a-snake: fear is "a moment of past experience" projected onto the present.
- Bridge to [[diffusing-visual-memories]] (Ch.6): editing the films.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Memory isn't a fixed film to "replay."** It's reconstructive — recalled differently
  each time. The "old film" metaphor can imply a stable recording that doesn't exist
  (though this reconstructive nature is what makes editing *possible*).
- **"Just watch happier memories" under-serves trauma**, where intrusive memories aren't
  freely chosen; clinical care may be needed (see Ch.6 tensions).

## Practical examples
- From the book: the stick mistaken for a snake — real fear, no real threat.
- Beyond the book: rehearsing an old humiliation until it floods you with present shame.

## Common mistakes
- Replaying painful memories on loop and treating the revived feeling as current reality.
- Believing you have no choice over which mental films you watch.
