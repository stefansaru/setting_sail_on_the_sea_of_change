> The deepest human drive isn't pleasure or power but meaning — and lasting fulfillment comes "not in what we consume, but in what we contribute."

## Definition
Frankl's central thesis ([[viktor-frankl]]): the primary human motivation is the search
for meaning. Of [[the-three-wills]], it is the only one that *nourishes* rather than
merely fills. Its contents are "purposeful work, deep relationships, and the courage to
face challenges with resilience." The defining shift: "this is where lasting fulfillment
is found — not in what we consume, but in what we contribute."

## Why it matters
It reorients a life from *acquisition* (pleasure, status, consumption) to
*contribution* — the through-line connecting this chapter to
[[doing-what-you-love-is-loving-the-mission]] (Ch.4), [[ikigai]] ("what the world
needs"), and Ch.10's ethic of acting with regard for others. It explains why people who
"have everything" can feel empty, and why contribution reliably satisfies.

## Supporting ideas
- [[meaning-is-created-not-found]] — meaning is built through the choices the will to meaning makes.
- [[frankls-values]] — the three concrete routes (creative, experiential, attitudinal) to meaning.
- "Contribution over consumption" as the operative test.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Is meaning really *the* deepest drive?** Competing theories center attachment,
  survival, or self-determination; "will to meaning" is one influential view, not
  settled fact.
- **Contribution can be co-opted** into self-sacrifice or martyrdom (cf.
  [[giving-from-abundance-vs-sacrifice]], Ch.4) — meaning-through-contribution still
  needs the balance of a full cup.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the "meaning aisle" of purposeful work and deep relationships that "nourishes."
- Beyond the book: a retiree who finds more meaning volunteering than they ever did consuming leisure.

## Common mistakes
- Expecting consumption (pleasure) or status (power) to deliver what only contribution can.
- Treating "meaning" as a feeling to chase rather than a *direction* (toward contribution) to act in.
