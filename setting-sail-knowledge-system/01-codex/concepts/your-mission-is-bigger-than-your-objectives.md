> Hitting big goals often brings not joy but emptiness — "the goals are done, but the meaning is gone." Lasting fulfillment comes from a mission bigger than any objective.

## Definition
"A lot of people believe that hitting big goals will bring endless joy. Once they check off
their list... they are not satisfied — confusion, or even a mid-life crisis. The goals are
done, but the meaning is gone." The reason: "success isn't just about achieving things; it's
about what those things mean in the bigger picture." Beyond objectives lies your **mission —
your purpose**: "what you can bring to yourself, others, and the environment... the impact
you make and the legacy you leave behind." When objectives "align with your core values and
contribute to your mission, they bring deeper, more sustainable fulfillment."

## Why it matters
It diagnoses the "arrival fallacy" (the emptiness after achievement, mechanistically
explained by [[hedonic-adaptation]]) and prescribes the cure: orient goals toward a
values-based mission. It's the Chapter-1 seed of the whole meaning stratum
([[the-will-to-meaning]], [[ikigai]], [[doing-what-you-love-is-loving-the-mission]]) and of
[[the-journey-is-the-destination]].

## Supporting ideas
- Purpose questions: "What excites me beyond material success? What change do I want to create? What can I give — and want in return?"
- "Your purpose is what keeps you going when things get hard."
- Objectives are "moments, not your whole story."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Find your mission" pressure.** Not everyone has (or needs) a single grand mission;
  insisting on one can itself create anxiety. Many find meaning in relationships and daily
  life without a capital-M Mission (cf. [[the-journey-is-the-destination]]).

## Practical examples
- From the book: the mid-life crisis of someone who achieved everything on their list and felt empty.
- Beyond the book: the founder who sells the company and feels lost without the larger "why."

## Common mistakes
- Believing the next achievement will finally bring lasting joy (arrival fallacy).
- Pursuing objectives disconnected from values/mission.
