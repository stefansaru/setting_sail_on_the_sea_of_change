> Before birth we briefly echo our ancestors — a tail, gill-like structures, a default female blueprint — as if development fast-forwards through evolution.

## Definition
"As embryos, we briefly mirror the creatures that came before us." The book describes a
tail "just like our aquatic ancestors" that fades; structures "that look like gills,"
briefly reptile-linked traits; and a shared "female blueprint" early on, with male traits
forming only "when the Y chromosome kicks in" (the book's explanation for why "men have
nipples — because everyone starts with the same blueprint"). It frames development as "a
fast-forward version of evolution and a reminder that we're all connected."

## Why it matters
It's a vivid, personal demonstration that we carry our evolutionary history — not just in
[[vestigial-structures|leftover body parts]] but in how we *develop*. It reinforces
[[what-is-life|life's continuity]] and the connectedness theme that underlies the
inheritance stratum.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Recapitulation theory is outdated.** Haeckel's "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" is
  largely discredited; embryos resemble *other embryos*, not adult ancestors, and don't
  literally "replay" evolution. The book presents the popular version — accurate in spirit
  (shared developmental heritage) but not in the strong literal sense. **Flag this when
  using the page.** (See [[gaps]].)

## Practical examples
- From the book: the embryonic tail that fades "as we transition from water to land."
- Beyond the book: the shared early "female blueprint" explaining male nipples.

## Common mistakes
- Taking "we replay evolution in the womb" as literal, current science.
- Missing that the *valid* point is shared developmental ancestry, not literal recapitulation.
