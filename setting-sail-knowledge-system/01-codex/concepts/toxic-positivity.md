> The belief that you should stay positive and suppress negative emotions even when life is hard — a setup for disappointment, because dopamine comes in waves, not floods.

## Definition
"Toxic positivity" is "the belief that one should maintain a positive attitude and
ignore or suppress negative emotions, even in difficult situations." Society "seems
obsessed with feeling happy," amplified by curated social media (see
[[social-media-iceberg]]) that makes ordinary life feel like failure. So people "push
harder, chase dopamine, want constant highs." But "that's not how the brain works.
Dopamine is meant to come in waves. Not floods. The highs feel good. But the lows hit
harder." Chasing happiness backfires "because happiness, like everything else, has a
shadow."

## Why it matters
It names a culturally pervasive trap and explains *why* it fails biologically (dopamine
adaptation) and philosophically (every light casts a shadow — [[yin-yang-light-and-shadow]]). It is the failure mode that [[awareness-of-both]] corrects: deny the bad and
you don't get more good — you get a brittle, exhausting chase.

## Supporting ideas
- [[awareness-of-both]] — the healthy alternative (hold joy *and* pain).
- [[social-media-iceberg]] — the comparison engine that fuels it.
- Dopamine "in waves, not floods" → connects to [[hedonic-adaptation]] *(forward link — Ch.7)*.
- "Society seems to value sacrifice more often than it should" — even relentless goodness meets resistance.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Optimism is genuinely useful.** The target is *forced, suppressive* positivity, not
  a hopeful outlook; the book risks readers throwing out healthy optimism with toxic positivity.
- **"Embrace the dark path" framing** (§10.4) is provocative and easily misread — the
  point is balance, not that pursuing harm leads to good.

## Practical examples
- From the book: posting "biggest wins, best angles, filtered moments" while hiding doubts and sadness.
- Beyond the book: telling someone grieving to "just stay positive," which invalidates rather than helps.

## Common mistakes
- Suppressing negative emotions instead of acknowledging them.
- Chasing constant highs and crashing harder on the inevitable lows.
- Mistaking healthy hope for toxic positivity (over-correcting into cynicism).
