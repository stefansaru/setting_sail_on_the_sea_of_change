> Big improvements come not from big goals but from small, consistent changes that compound — and the most durable lever is identity: become the kind of person who does the habit.

## Definition
The book's summary of James Clear's *Atomic Habits* ([[james-clear]]): "it's not about
having big goals but about making small changes that add up over time." Key components:
- **The habit loop** ([[the-habit-loop]]): cue → craving → response → reward.
- **Identity over outcomes:** "habits shape identity, and identity reinforces habits."
  Align habits with *who you want to become*, not just what you want to get.
- **[[habit-stacking]]:** attach a new habit to an existing one.
- **[[two-minute-rule]]:** shrink a habit to a 2-minute version to remove resistance.
- **Environment design:** "our surroundings influence our habits more than we realize"
  — make good cues visible, bad cues invisible.
- **Patience & continuous improvement:** change is gradual; habits evolve as life changes.

## Why it matters
Habits are how you install behavior into [[system-1-and-system-2|System 1]] so it runs
automatically — making your values self-executing rather than dependent on daily
willpower. The identity framing is the deepest link to the book's self-authorship arc:
each small action "reinforces the story you are writing about yourself."

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-habit-loop]] — the four-part mechanism every habit follows.
- [[consistency-beats-restarting]] (Ch.10) — habits compound only if you don't uproot them.
- Identity: "if you aim to be a healthy person, engage in habits that support that identity."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Small habits aren't always enough.** Some change requires big, discontinuous moves
  (leaving a job, an intervention); "tiny changes" can become an excuse for never making
  the hard leap.
- **Identity-first can backfire** if the desired identity is itself unexamined (cf.
  [[internalizing-the-exterior]] — make sure it's *your* identity).

## Practical examples
- From the book: water bottle on the desk (cue) → thirst (craving) → drink (response) → refreshed (reward).
- Beyond the book: "I'm a runner" (identity) making the morning run non-negotiable.

## Common mistakes
- Chasing outcomes/goals while ignoring the daily system that produces them.
- Making habits too big to start (ignoring the [[two-minute-rule]]).
- Relying on willpower instead of designing the environment.
