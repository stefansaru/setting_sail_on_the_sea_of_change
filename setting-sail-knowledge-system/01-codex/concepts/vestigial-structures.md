> The body carries biological leftovers — a tailbone, goosebumps, wisdom teeth, the palmaris longus — relics that once served our ancestors and now mostly persist because "if it's not broken, keep it."

## Definition
Evolution follows a simple rule: "if it's not broken, keep it." So our bodies retain
**vestigial structures** — parts that once had vital functions but are now relics:
- **Coccyx (tailbone)** — remnant of an ancestral tail.
- **Body hair & goosebumps** — leftovers of insulating fur and its temperature-regulating reflex.
- **Palmaris longus muscle** — a forearm tendon absent in ~14% of people, irrelevant to grip but "treasured in surgery" for tendon grafts ("your body carries its own spare parts").
- **Ear muscles, Darwin's tubercle** — remnants of more mobile, sound-locating ears.
- **Vomeronasal organ, third eyelid (plica semilunaris)** — faded pheromone/eye-protective structures.
- **Wisdom teeth** — third molars that no longer fit our smaller, cooked-food-era jaws.

## Why it matters
Vestigial structures are tangible proof of our evolutionary history written into the
body — the physical counterpart of [[vestigial-mind-wirings|mental relics]] and the
clearest illustration of [[evolutionary-mismatch]]. (The palmaris longus is also the
personal detail the author points his daughter to in the [[legacy-the-epilogue|epilogue]].)

## Supporting ideas
- "If it's not broken, keep it" — evolution discards only what actively costs.
- [[vestigial-mind-wirings]] — the same logic applied to instincts.
- [[recapitulation-embryo]] — the developmental echo of ancestry.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Vestigial" ≠ "useless."** Several listed structures retain minor or repurposed
  functions (the coccyx anchors muscles; the palmaris longus aids surgery). "Vestigial"
  means *reduced/changed role*, not zero use — a nuance worth keeping.
- The list is a popular selection; some entries are debated in degree of vestigiality.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the palmaris longus self-test (thumb to pinky, lift the wrist, look for the raised band).
- Beyond the book: impacted wisdom teeth — an everyday cost of jaw shrinkage.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming vestigial means entirely functionless.
- Missing that these are *evidence* of shared ancestry, not random quirks.
