> The lion's fury meets the dragon's immovable tradition in an epic stalemate — proving that some battles can't be won by force, only transcended.

## Definition
When the lion ([[lion-phase]]) charges the [[thou-shalt-dragon]] "with all its
might," it meets a deadlock: "this battle isn't one that can be won through strength
alone." Defiance against tradition reaches an "epic stalemate." The image
foreshadows why the lion must transform into the child rather than keep fighting.

## Why it matters
It diagnoses a common stuck-point: pouring more force into a fight that force cannot
resolve. The resolution is not a stronger roar but a *change of frame* — turning the
battlefield into a [[world-as-playground|playground]]. This is the concrete face of
[[negation-is-not-creation]].

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Some immovable objects do yield to persistence** — sustained pressure on unjust
  systems sometimes works. The metaphor can counsel premature surrender of a worthy fight.

## Practical examples
- From the book: Cammy's endless, unwinnable battles with the dragon leading to "what's the point of fighting if nothing ever changes?"
- Beyond the book: trying to "win" an argument against an entire culture's assumptions by sheer intensity.

## Common mistakes
- Escalating force when the situation calls for a change of approach.
- Reading the stalemate as personal failure rather than a signal to transform.
