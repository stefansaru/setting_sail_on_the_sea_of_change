> A logical fallacy that persuades by stirring feelings instead of giving reasons — powerful for selling, and the edge where value-creation tips into manipulation.

## What it is
Substituting emotional pull for logical justification. In [[the-makeup-artist]] (Ch.4),
instead of giving reasons for the price, the pitch "focuses on the feelings of seeing an
ex" — emotion does the persuading.

## How it misleads
- **Sales:** selling the *feeling* ("leave your ex speechless") rather than the product's merits.
- **Politics/advertising:** fear, pride, or belonging in place of evidence.
- **Law:** vivid emotional stories swaying a jury past the facts.

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"What's the actual reason/evidence here — separate from how it makes me feel?"*
- Notice when a feeling is being used to skip the argument.
- The book's caution: persuasion "built only on emotional hooks can cross into manipulation."
