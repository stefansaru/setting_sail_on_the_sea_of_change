> The stage of carrying inherited burdens — saying yes to society's "shoulds" with a kind of honor, before ever asking if they are yours.

## Definition
The first metamorphosis. The camel loads its back with traditions, duties, and
"you shoulds" handed down by society, family, and friends — and carries them with
pride. It is the stage of conformity: an identity defined by external roles and
labels, values absorbed without reflection. The book likens it to an all-you-can-eat
buffet where you "agree to everything," and to the rat race where carrying the load
makes you "employee of the month."

## Why it matters
It names the default, unexamined life — the starting condition almost everyone is
in. Recognizing yourself as a camel is the precondition for change; you cannot put
down a burden you don't know you're carrying. It is the [[stratum/inheritance]]
stage made personal.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **The camel is not simply wrong.** Absorbing a culture's accumulated values is how
  you acquire competence, belonging, and raw material to later transcend. A society
  of pure lions and children couldn't function.
- **Duty ≠ servitude.** Some burdens carried "for others" are love, not conformity;
  the model can wrongly pathologize care.

## Practical examples
- From the book's list: difficulty saying no, fear of judgment, suppressing personal desires, perfectionism driven by external validation, "feeling trapped," burnout.
- Beyond the book: taking the prestigious career your parents wanted and calling the unease "just stress."

## Common mistakes
- Mistaking the *honor* of carrying burdens for genuine fulfillment.
- Staying a camel permanently because the load is familiar (a [[comfortable-traps|comfort trap]], forward link — Ch.8).
- Believing there is "no other way" — the camel's signature limiting belief.
