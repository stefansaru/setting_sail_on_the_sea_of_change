> Certain overused words quietly install limiting or toxic beliefs; changing the words you use — especially in your inner dialogue — reshapes how you think.

## Definition
Some words "when overused, tend to generate limiting or toxic beliefs and feelings,"
shaping mindset in ways that hold you back. They seem harmless but "influence our
perception of ourselves and the world." The book's target list, with reframes:
- **"I hate"** → drains energy, fuels resentment. *"I prefer weekends over Mondays."*
- **"bored"** → implies lack of curiosity/initiative. *"I'd like to find something interesting to focus on."*
- **"try"** → signals lack of commitment, pre-licenses failure. *"I will complete the project."*
- **"guilty"** → overused, becomes self-blame that blocks growth. *"I prioritize self-care because it helps me be at my best."*
- **"ugly"** → subjective, unkind, breeds negative self-talk. *"That dress isn't my style."*
- **"impossible"** → closes the door to problem-solving. *"Challenging, but with effort I can make progress."*
- **"always / never"** → absolutes that exaggerate and create fixed mindsets. *"I'd appreciate it if you called more often."*

## Why it matters
Language is not just a description of thought — it shapes it. Because the inner critic
speaks in words, swapping limiting words for constructive ones is a direct lever on
mindset, "fostering positivity, resilience, and personal growth." It's a small,
repeatable habit with compounding effect on self-talk.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Risk of toxic positivity / euphemism.** Renaming "I hate Mondays" can slide into
  suppressing genuine feelings rather than processing them; sometimes naming a hard
  emotion plainly is healthier than reframing it.
- **Words reflect beliefs as much as cause them.** Changing vocabulary without changing
  the underlying belief can be hollow — the book's strongest examples pair word + reframe of meaning.

## Practical examples
- From the book: replacing "I'll try" with "I will" to remove the pre-loaded permission to fail.
- Beyond the book: catching "I'm so bad at this" mid-sentence and restating it as "I'm still learning this."

## Common mistakes
- Using absolutes ("you always…", "you never…") in conflict, which escalates and distorts.
- Reframing words while ignoring the feeling underneath (euphemism, not growth).
- Policing others' words instead of starting with your own inner dialogue.
