> Most results come from a small share of causes — roughly 80% of outcomes from 20% of inputs. Find the "vital few" and give them your energy.

## Definition
"About 80% of outcomes come from 20% of inputs. The numbers don't need to be exact, but the
imbalance is real. A few things matter a lot. Most things matter less." It separates "the
vital few from the trivial many." To apply: look at your inputs (time, effort), measure what
they produce, find the 20% driving most of the result, and "focus there. Spend less time on
the rest."

## Why it matters
It's a prioritization multiplier: in a finite life, identifying the high-leverage minority
beats spreading effort evenly. The book applies it across domains — friendships ("20% of
friends give 80% of joy"), health habits, learning the core basics, wardrobe, finances,
work tasks, stress sources, and "happiness" activities.

## Supporting ideas
- [[eisenhower-method]] / [[pick-chart]] — companion tools for focusing on what matters.
- [[decluttering]] — let go of the trivial many.
- "Find the few things that make the biggest difference — and give them your energy."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **The 80/20 split isn't a law.** It's a heuristic; some domains are more even, and chasing
  it can become an excuse to neglect important "long tail" work (maintenance, relationships).
- Identifying the vital 20% requires honest measurement most people skip.

## Practical examples
- From the book: most people wear 20% of their wardrobe 80% of the time.
- Beyond the book: a tiny set of clients or tasks generating most of the value.

## Common mistakes
- Treating all inputs as equally worthy of time.
- Guessing the "vital few" instead of measuring outputs.
