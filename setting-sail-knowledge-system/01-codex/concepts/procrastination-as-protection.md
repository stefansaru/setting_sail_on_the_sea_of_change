> Procrastination isn't laziness — it's a defense mechanism. Your mind's priority isn't success; it's protecting you from the pain of past failures. The fix is small wins, not self-blame.

## Definition
"Procrastination often stands in the way — not as laziness, but as a built-in defense
mechanism. Your mind's priority isn't success; it's protection." Having logged past failures
as painful, the mind "associates similar situations with discomfort and pushes you toward
avoidance" — "why you reach for cake while on a diet, or scroll through social media when
work is pending." The remedy: "you can't just lie to your own mind... create small wins.
Success builds confidence, and confidence builds momentum." And: bring the goal closer if
"point B feels too far."

## Why it matters
It reframes procrastination from a character flaw (inviting shame, which worsens it) into a
*protective system* you can work *with* — connecting to [[most-fears-are-memories]] (the
avoidance is memory-driven) and [[breaking-a-bad-habit-but-keeping-its-benefit]] (honor the
protective intent, change the method). The lever is evidence: give the mind small successes.

## Supporting ideas
- [[mood-follows-action]] — the small first step that bypasses the defense.
- [[two-minute-rule]] / [[setting-smart-goals]] — shrink the task until success is likely.
- "If you don't believe in yourself, your mind won't either."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Not all procrastination is fear-based** — some is poor systems, unclear priorities, or a
  task that genuinely shouldn't be done. The protective-mechanism lens is powerful but not universal.
- "Bring point B closer" can become chronically lowered ambition if overused.

## Practical examples
- From the book: reaching for cake on a diet; scrolling when work is pending.
- Beyond the book: endlessly "researching" a project to avoid the vulnerable act of starting it.

## Common mistakes
- Treating procrastination as laziness and piling on self-blame (deepening avoidance).
- Setting a goal so big the mind registers only the threat of failure.
