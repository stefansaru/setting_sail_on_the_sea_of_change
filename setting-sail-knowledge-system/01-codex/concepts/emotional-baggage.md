> Unprocessed emotions don't disappear — they get stored, become automatic reactions, and quietly weigh you down until you face them.

## Definition
"Carrying emotional baggage is like dragging an overstuffed suitcase" — each experience
adds an item, some joyful, some "heavy with past pain." Emotional responses start as
deliberate [[system-1-and-system-2|System 2]] effort ("like learning a new dance") and,
with repetition, become automatic System 1 reactions. The emotions we *don't* process
"don't just disappear; they settle in." We store them in mental boxes "labeled 'do not
open'" and cover the discomfort with distractions (emotional eating, "constant
scrolling") — "like throwing a blanket over clutter and pretending the room is clean."
But "avoiding emotions doesn't resolve them. Sooner or later, they demand attention."

## Why it matters
It names *why* old pain runs current behavior, and insists the only exit is *through*:
"acknowledging emotions, rather than suppressing them, is how real healing begins." It
explicitly recalls [[camel-phase|Cammy the camel]] — the baggage is "not a life
sentence" but "the first step toward releasing it" (camel → lion). It also legitimizes
seeking support: vulnerability with trusted people or a therapist "isn't a sign of weakness."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Not all avoidance is harmful.** Some temporary setting-aside of emotion is healthy
  coping (especially in crisis); "face everything now" can overwhelm. Timing matters.
- **"Process it and heal" can understate** how much skilled help (therapy) serious
  baggage needs — the book gestures at this but the DIY framing dominates (see [[gaps]]).

## Practical examples
- From the book: the attic of "do not open" boxes; numbing with scrolling or eating.
- Beyond the book: snapping disproportionately at a small trigger because it touched an unprocessed old wound.

## Common mistakes
- Distracting/numbing instead of acknowledging emotions.
- Treating reflexive emotional reactions as fixed personality rather than learned, changeable patterns.
- Trying to "clear it all alone" when support would help.
