> Mistaking selection for cause: top swimmers aren't lean *because* they swim — people with that build are selected into elite swimming. Success often reflects who was chosen, not just what they did.

## What it is
Confusing cause and effect by ignoring selection. "Real insight sees both" training and
selection.

## How it misleads
- **Sports:** "swimming gives you a swimmer's body" — but elite swimmers already had suited bodies.
- **Education:** elite schools "cause" success — but they admit already-high achievers.
- **Business:** copying a CEO's 5 a.m. routine, ignoring their risk tolerance, connections, timing.
- **Law:** "this method guarantees success" from people who were exceptional beforehand.

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"Is this result from training, or from selection?"*
- Separate correlation from causation.
- Learn from success stories, but don't confuse traits with outcomes.
