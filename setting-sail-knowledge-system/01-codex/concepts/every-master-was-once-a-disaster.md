> Before the success was the mess — late nights, cheap coffee, more doubt than clarity. The biggest disaster is impatience: digging up the seed to check it, and killing the growth.

## Definition
"Successful individuals often promote... positive attitude, healthy foods, premium guidance.
But the early days are messy." No one "finds balance right away." The chief enemy is
**impatience**: "you plant a seed and expect fruit the next day, so you dig it up, thinking
nothing's happening" (the same image as [[the-walnut-tree]]). And discipline is "a slow build,
like stacking a house of cards — pull one out (skip one workout, break one promise) and the
whole thing falls apart." "One day off becomes two. Two becomes a week. And now you're back to
square one."

## Why it matters
It normalizes the ugly early phase of any growth (countering [[survivorship-bias]] and the
polished images of success) and pinpoints impatience as the momentum-killer. It's the
Chapter-1 statement of [[consistency-beats-restarting]] and the patience behind
[[self-esteem-is-earned]].

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-walnut-tree]] / [[consistency-beats-restarting]] (Ch.10) — don't uproot slow growth.
- [[discipline-over-motivation]] — the slow, system-built stack.
- "Stop chasing speed. Build consistency. Stack your wins."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Persistence vs. knowing when to stop.** "Never quit" can keep you in a genuinely wrong
  path ([[sunk-cost-fallacy]]); the wisdom is distinguishing slow-but-working from not-working.

## Practical examples
- From the book: Edison's "10,000 ways that won't work."
- Beyond the book: abandoning a new habit after a week because results weren't visible yet.

## Common mistakes
- Expecting fast results and quitting at the first plateau.
- Letting one missed day cascade ("two becomes a week").
