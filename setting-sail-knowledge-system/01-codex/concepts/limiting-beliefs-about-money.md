> Inherited beliefs like "money is evil" or "I'm not good with money" quietly keep people poor by stopping them from even trying — and they can be challenged and replaced.

## Definition
Beliefs about money that "keep people stuck" by foreclosing action before it begins.
The book lists common ones with rebuttals:
- "Money is the root of all evil" → money is just a tool; what matters is how you use it.
- "Rich people are greedy" → many give back and support good causes.
- "You have to work hard to make money" → working *smart* and choosing well matters more than effort alone.
- "I'll never afford what I want" → planning, saving, and investing slowly get you there.
- "I'm not good with money" → money skills can be learned like any other.
- "It's too late to start saving" → small steps still compound.
- "I don't deserve wealth" → everyone deserves financial security.
- "More money solves all problems" → it eases stress, but happiness comes from elsewhere.
- "Investing is too risky" → done wisely, it spreads risk.
- "You need luck to get rich" → discipline and planning matter far more.

## Why it matters
"Your beliefs about money shape your behavior. Change the belief, and you change the
result." A limiting belief makes you "ignore chances to grow, to save, or to invest" —
so the belief, not the bank balance, is often the true bottleneck. It is the
money-specific case of [[limiting-words]].

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Structural reality matters too.** "Beliefs shape results" can shade into a
  bootstrap myth that ignores real barriers — wages, debt, discrimination, luck. Belief
  change is necessary but not always sufficient.
- Some cautions are *true*, not limiting: investing genuinely *can* be risky; "money
  doesn't buy happiness" past a point has real support. The skill is telling a limiting
  belief from a prudent one.

## Practical examples
- From the book: "i'll never be rich" or "money is bad" stopping someone "from even trying."
- Beyond the book: avoiding investing entirely out of "it's gambling," and losing value to inflation instead.

## Common mistakes
- Treating inherited family money-scripts as facts about the world.
- Swinging from limiting beliefs to reckless ones ("I deserve wealth" → overspending/over-risk).
