> Every goal is a bridge between two points: who you are now and who you want to become. Get clear on both ends, then build the span between them.

## Definition
After writing [[the-wishlist]], you "build the bridge" by answering three questions:
- **Who am I right now?** (current state — strengths, what's holding you back)
- **Who will I become?** (the version of you who has what you want — your goal)
- **What's the gap, and what needs to change to bridge it?**

The book stresses this is gradual ("a series of small, consistent steps") and warns against
black-and-white thinking: "most things aren't 'possible or impossible' — they're just harder
or slower than expected. Progress lives in the gray area."

## Why it matters
It turns vague wanting into a workable structure (current → desired → gap), the bridge from
[[the-wishlist]] to action via [[setting-smart-goals]]. The "gray area" reframe pre-empts the
all-or-nothing thinking that fuels giving up.

## Supporting ideas
- [[defining-your-own-values]] — "who will I become?" should be values-aligned, not borrowed.
- [[setting-smart-goals]] — the bridge built in concrete steps.
- [[values-on-a-spectrum]] — progress lives in the gray, not in binaries.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Who I'll become" can import others' definitions of success** — the gap is only worth
  crossing if the far side is genuinely yours (cf. [[camel-phase]] vs. [[child-phase]]).

## Practical examples
- From the book: clarifying current strengths/blocks, then the desired self, then the change needed.
- Beyond the book: "I'm sedentary" → "I'm someone who moves daily" → gap: a small, repeatable habit.

## Common mistakes
- Defining the destination without honestly assessing the starting point.
- Black-and-white "possible/impossible" framing instead of "harder/slower."
