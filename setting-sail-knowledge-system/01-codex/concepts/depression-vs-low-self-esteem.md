> Low self-esteem is a poor self-evaluation; depression is a deeper condition that lies beyond it — and may be less a weakness than a signal for "deep rest."

## Definition
The book carefully distinguishes three states often blurred together:
- **Sadness** — regret "over something bad that happened or something good that didn't" (Jim Carrey).
- **Low self-esteem** — "a poor self-evaluation"; can fuel anxiety and unhealthy coping (smoking, drinking).
- **Depression** — "a deeper, more serious condition... persistent sadness, hopelessness, loss of interest, and both emotional and physical symptoms," extending "far beyond self-perception."

Two reframes anchor the discussion: Jim Carrey's image of depression as the body no
longer wanting to maintain the "character" you've been playing (see [[the-stage-and-the-roles]]), extended by Jeff Foster into "depressed = **deep rest**" — a signal you need a
break from the persona. And Gabor Maté's view that depression is "a normal response to
unresolved emotions from past traumatic experiences" — "not weakness or failure but a
natural biological reaction to abnormal and distressing events."

## Why it matters
Conflating these states leads to bad responses: treating clinical depression as a
self-esteem problem (or a character flaw) delays real help, while pathologizing
ordinary sadness over-medicalizes life. The "deep rest" and trauma framings reduce
shame and reframe depression as meaningful signal rather than personal defect.

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-stage-and-the-roles]] — depression as the gap between authentic self and performed role growing "too wide."
- Gabor Maté: "trauma is not what happened to you, but what happens inside you as a result of what happened to you."
- [[self-compassion]] — "be kind to yourself and others facing these struggles."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Reframes are not treatment.** "Deep rest" and "natural response" are humane
  framings, but clinical depression often needs professional care; the metaphors must
  not substitute for it (a gap the book leaves — see [[gaps]]).
- **Not all depression is trauma-rooted.** Depression also has genetic, biochemical,
  and situational causes; the trauma lens, while powerful, is partial.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the actor exhausted by maintaining roles across work, family, and friends until the gap "leads to exhaustion, dissatisfaction, or even depression."
- Beyond the book: recognizing that persistent loss of interest (not just a bad week) warrants support, not just "trying harder."

## Common mistakes
- Treating depression as low self-esteem you can "confidence" your way out of.
- Calling ordinary sadness "depression," or dismissing real depression as weakness.
- Using "deep rest" to avoid seeking professional help when it's needed.
