> Shared core values let partners grow together; diverging values pull them apart — and you cannot force alignment that no longer exists.

## Definition
Long-term compatibility rests on aligned core values, not initial attraction. The
book highlights three high-leverage dimensions — education level, financial mindset,
spiritual beliefs — plus love and lifestyle. When values stay aligned, the
relationship "naturally progresses." When they diverge ("people grow apart as their
core values change"), the healthiest move is to "calmly present your decision to step
away," because "how they think and feel is not within your control."

## Why it matters
It reframes relationship success away from luck or effort-against-the-odds toward
*alignment*. It also applies the book's master principle to love:
[[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind|you can't control another person]], so forcing
change is futile; you can only choose your own response.

## Supporting ideas
- [[defining-your-own-values]] — you must know your own values to test alignment.
- "Mismatches in core values become burdens over time" — initial attraction masks them.
- Location matters: bigger cities widen the pool of value-compatible partners.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Values can be negotiated and co-evolved**, not just matched — the framing can be
  too static, treating divergence as automatic grounds to leave.
- **Tension with commitment.** "Step away when values diverge" sits uneasily beside
  the long-term ideal of working through difficulty ([[relationship-as-brickwork]]).
  When is divergence a dealbreaker vs. a season to weather? The book under-specifies.

## Practical examples
- From the book: if a partner "doesn't see an issue with the shift in values, forcing change won't help."
- Beyond the book: two partners whose views on children/money diverge over a decade despite genuine love.

## Common mistakes
- Ignoring value mismatches during attraction-heavy dating.
- Trying to *force* a partner to change their values (outside your control).
- Confusing a temporary rough patch with permanent value divergence.
