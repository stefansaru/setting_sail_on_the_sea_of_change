> Everything is created twice — first in the mind, then in reality. Design the first creation deliberately, or someone else's expectations will design it for you.

## Definition
Stephen Covey's Habit 2, as the book presents it. Core ideas:
- **Mental creation before physical creation** — "you visualize it before you build it. If you don't consciously design that first creation, someone else will do it for you — through expectations, pressure, or circumstance."
- **Personal mission statement** — "a compass, not a map"; it defines what matters most and keeps you aligned "when the path changes." (See [[write-your-eulogy-and-mission]].)
- **Values before goals** — "goals can be achieved and still feel empty if they don't align with your core values."
- **Act with purpose, not habit** — daily choices should move you toward who you want to become, "not just keep you busy."
- **Reflection and recalibration** — periodically check you're still heading the right way.

## Why it matters
It supplies the *long-horizon* counterpart to [[intention-in-context]]'s present-moment
focus: the end you hold in mind is the compass that makes each present choice coherent.
Without a consciously chosen end, your life is authored by default — others' expectations
and circumstance. It reinforces [[define-values-before-setting-course]] (Ch.3) at the
scale of a whole life.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Over-planning vs. emergence.** A fixed "end" can blind you to unforeseen, better
  paths; some lives are well-lived by discovery, not design. The book softens this with
  "the map changes... but the compass stays true."
- **Tension with present-moment intention** ([[intention-in-context]]): hold the end
  lightly enough that you can still respond to *now*.

## Practical examples
- From the book: "write your eulogy today" to find your "compass' north."
- Beyond the book: choosing a career move by asking "does this move me closer to the life I want?" rather than by prestige.

## Common mistakes
- Letting expectations/pressure design your "first creation" by default.
- Pursuing achievable goals that don't align with values (empty wins).
- Treating the mission as a rigid map rather than a compass.
