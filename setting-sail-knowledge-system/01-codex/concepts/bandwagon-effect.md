> Doing something just because everyone else is — popularity *feels* like proof, but it isn't.

## What it is
The bandwagon effect is following the crowd because the crowd is large. "Popularity feels
like proof, but it isn't." A vivid case of [[cognitive-bias]] and a cousin of conformity
([[internalizing-the-exterior]]).

## How it misleads
- **Politics:** voting for whoever's "leading in the polls" — popularity ≠ competence.
- **Marketing:** "best-seller," "most people choose this."
- **Social/fashion:** watching the hit show or copying a trend "to be part of the talk."
- **Law/debate:** "everyone knows this to be true" in place of evidence.

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"Would I make the same choice if no one else did?"*
- Look for actual reasons or data, not headcounts.
- Remember "history is full of popular beliefs later proven wrong."

## Related concepts
- [[false-consensus]] — overestimating how many agree with *you*.
- [[groupthink]] — agreement prized over correctness inside a group.
