> **Synthesis page — not in the book.** Resolves the book's sharpest unresolved tension (and gap G1): when do you *repair* a relationship vs. *let it go*? The missing variable is the **type of misalignment** — friction or divergence.

## The polarity (C2) + the gap (G1)
The book says both:
- **Repair / persist** — build "brick by brick," address conflicts before they stack ([[relationship-as-brickwork]]).
- **Release / let go** — when values diverge, "letting go is the healthiest option" ([[values-alignment-in-relationships]]).

…but gives **no rule** for telling which situation you're in ([[gaps|G1]]).

## The missing dimension: friction vs. divergence (context)
- **Friction** = *same destination, different route.* You share core values but clash on
  execution (how to spend money, divide chores, communicate). → **Repair.** This is the
  brickwork: same compass heading, just a rough patch of road.
- **Divergence** = *different destinations.* Your core values/direction genuinely differ
  (one wants monogamy, the other doesn't; one wants children, the other never will). →
  **Release** (or accept a permanent gap). Repair can't fix a compass pointing elsewhere.

**The test:** *Are we disagreeing about how to get somewhere we both want to go (friction),
or about where we're going (divergence)?*

## The synthesis
> **Repair when values align; release when values diverge.**

Most "should I stay?" pain comes from misclassifying: trying to *repair a divergence*
(endless effort, no movement) or *releasing over mere friction* (leaving a good thing for a
fixable rough patch).

## Why it matters
It turns an agonizing, vague question into a diagnostic — and it generalizes beyond romance
to friendships, business partnerships, and careers. It also operationalizes
[[change-what-you-can-accept-what-you-cant]] in relationships: execution is changeable
(friction); another person's core direction is not yours to change (divergence).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **A third category: realignment.** Values aren't always fixed — sometimes a divergence is
  a *temporary season*, and partners can co-evolve toward a shared direction. So "divergence
  → release" shouldn't be reflexive; first ask whether the compass can genuinely be re-shared.
- **Mislabeling is easy and self-serving** — it's tempting to call a hard repair a
  "divergence" to justify leaving (or to call a real divergence "just friction" to avoid loss/
  [[sunk-cost-fallacy]]). Honesty is required.

## Practical examples
- Friction: two people who both value family argue about finances → repair (same destination).
- Divergence: one wants a quiet rural life, the other a high-travel city career, and neither will yield → release or accept the gap.

## Common mistakes
- Pouring years into "repairing" a genuine divergence (the compass won't move).
- Abandoning a relationship over friction that brickwork would have fixed.
- Skipping the *realignment* check before concluding "divergence."
