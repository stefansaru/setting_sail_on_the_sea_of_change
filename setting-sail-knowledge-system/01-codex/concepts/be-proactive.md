> Between stimulus and response lies the freedom to choose. Proactive people spend their energy on what they can influence — and their circle of influence grows.

## Definition
Covey's Habit 1 ([[stephen-covey]]): "being proactive means taking full responsibility for
your actions, emotions, and outcomes. Between stimulus and response lies the freedom to
choose how you react." Key ideas:
- **Circle of concern vs. circle of influence** — most people waste energy on what they
  can't control (concern); proactive people focus on what they *can* (behavior, skills,
  attitude), and "their circle of influence expands."
- **Language of proactivity** — replace "there's nothing I can do" with "let's find a way";
  "I can't" with "I choose."
- **Focus on solutions, not blame**; "responsibility precedes results."

## Why it matters
It's the action-oriented partner of [[change-what-you-can-accept-what-you-cant]] and the
practical engine of [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]]: by acting only within the circle of
influence — and using proactive language — you actually expand what you can affect over time.

## Supporting ideas
- Circle of influence grows as you act responsibly within it.
- Proactive language as a mindset lever ("I choose" vs. "I can't").
- [[the-story-of-joe]] — the parable on *recognizing* the opportunities you're given.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Can imply everything is within reach.** Structural constraints and bad luck are real;
  "expand your circle of influence" must not become blame-the-victim for what's genuinely outside it.

## Practical examples
- From the book: a colleague misses a deadline — reactive blames; proactive asks "what can I do now to keep the project on track?"
- Beyond the book: focusing on your résumé and skills (influence) instead of the job market (concern).

## Common mistakes
- Spending energy on the circle of concern (news, others' opinions, the economy).
- Reactive language ("that's just how I am") that forecloses change.
