> Believing you can influence outcomes that are actually random — a false sense of power over chance.

## What it is
Overestimating personal control over chance events. "Wisdom is knowing where your influence
ends" — the bias-chapter cousin of [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]] and [[stoicism]].

## How it misleads
- **Gambling:** blowing on dice, picking "lucky" numbers.
- **Investing:** sure your strategy can predict markets driven by unpredictable forces.
- **Health:** thinking vitamin C "completely" prevents colds.
- **Workplace:** micromanaging as if tight control guarantees results.

## How to counter it
- Separate what you **can** influence from what you can't.
- Focus on preparation and good decisions, not total control.
- Accept uncertainty as part of life.
