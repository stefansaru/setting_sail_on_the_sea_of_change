> The frightened part of you behind your fear — soothe it as you would a scared child, and you can move into the courage and creativity of the child phase.

## Definition
The book locates fear not in "you" but in an *inner child* — a vulnerable internal
part. The reframe is operational: instead of "I am afraid," ask "who is really
frightened? is it me or my inner child?" Then speak to that part "softly and kindly,"
as you would reassure a scared child. Caring for the inner child is named as the
*precondition* for entering the [[child-phase]].

## Why it matters
It converts the abstract principle [[courage-is-acting-despite-fear]] into a concrete
move. By externalizing fear as a part to be parented, you gain distance from it and
agency over it, rather than being fused with it.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Not a substitute for therapy.** Deep trauma may need more than a self-administered
  "we've got this"; the book presents a light-touch version of a clinical idea.
- **Risk of infantilizing real fear.** Some fear is accurate threat-detection, not a
  child to be soothed (see [[courage-is-acting-despite-fear]] tensions).

## Practical examples
- From the book: a quiet "we've got this" to your frightened inner child before a daunting step.
- Beyond the book: before a hard conversation, naming "the scared kid in me is worried about rejection" and reassuring it.

## Common mistakes
- Suppressing the inner child instead of comforting it.
- Treating the technique as a one-time fix rather than an ongoing relationship with that part of yourself.
