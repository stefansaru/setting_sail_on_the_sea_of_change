> Looking only at the winners and forgetting the failures — which makes success look far easier and more likely than it is.

## What it is
Drawing conclusions from survivors while the failures are invisible (they "didn't return").

## How it misleads
- **Business:** "Apple/Google made it, so can we" — ignoring the thousands of dead startups.
- **Investing:** fund track records look strong because the weak funds already closed.
- **History:** WWII planes — reinforcing the bullet holes on *returning* planes; the fatal hits were on the ones that never came back.
- **Law/debate:** presenting only favorable cases.

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"What am I not seeing? What's missing from this picture?"*
- Study the failures, not just the success stories.
- Balance inspiration with realism.
