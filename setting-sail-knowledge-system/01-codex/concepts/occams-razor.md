> When two explanations fit the facts, the one with fewer assumptions is usually right — cut away the noise.

## What it is
A reasoning heuristic (not a bias, but listed in the chapter as a thinking tool): prefer the
simplest explanation that still fits all the facts. "Simple doesn't mean shallow — just
clear and direct."

## How it applies
- **Science:** fever + sore throat → likely a common infection, not a rare tropical disease.
- **Business:** falling sales → weak marketing/product, not a competitor conspiracy.
- **Personal:** an unanswered message → they're busy, not secretly hostile.
- **Law:** juries often trust the clear, direct explanation over an elaborate theory.

## How to use it well
- Ask: *"What's the simplest explanation that still fits all the facts?"*
- Don't add assumptions unless you truly need them.
- Caveat: simplest ≠ always true; use with evidence, not as a shortcut to skip it.
