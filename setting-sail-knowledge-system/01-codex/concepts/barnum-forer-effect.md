> Vague statements feel personal and specific even though they fit almost everyone — why horoscopes and "personality" quizzes seem uncannily accurate.

## What it is
Accepting generic descriptions as uniquely true of you. "Flattery and vagueness often
masquerade as insight."

## How it misleads
- **Astrology:** "you sometimes doubt yourself but have a strong side too" — fits nearly everyone.
- **Marketing:** quiz results like "you value honesty but can be cautious."
- **Psychics:** "a big change is coming" — change happens to everyone.
- **Law/politics:** "the people want fairness," "you care about your family."

## How to counter it
- Check whether the statement could apply to *anyone*, not just you.
- Ask for clear, measurable specifics instead of generalities.
- Notice when flattery is doing the persuading.
