> Intention isn't visualizing far-off goals — it's how you choose to act in the situation you're in right now. The book's capstone tool for staying aligned.

## Definition
The book deliberately rejects the popular notion of intention as "visualizing goals,
repeating affirmations, or putting yourself in a trance." Instead: intention is "how
you choose to act in the situation you are in right now" — "an abstraction over your
current situation... your selected course of action, the internal direction you align
yourself with, in the present moment." In short: **"less about dreaming. More about
choosing."** It is the book's culminating, reusable tool — the lens through which every
prior idea (values, emotions, habits, biases) is applied in the moment, modeled via
[[the-intention-donut]] (intention = the hole; context = the ring).

## Why it matters
It is the synthesis the whole book builds toward: a "flexible tool you can keep using
for the rest of your life." Where [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]] says agency lives
in your response, intention-in-context is the *practice* of exercising that agency —
present-moment, situation-specific, value-aligned action rather than autopilot or
fantasy.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Present-moment intention vs. long-horizon mission.** This chapter also urges
  "start with the end in mind" ([[start-with-the-end-in-mind]]) and consistency toward a
  lifelong mission. So intention is *both* radically present *and* aimed at a distant
  end — a tension the book holds rather than resolves (the donut's hole is "now," the
  road trip has a "destination").
- **Purely personal utility.** The book insists models need not be "logical to others —
  just useful to you," which risks unfalsifiable, self-serving framings.

## Practical examples
- From the book: making a donut for "your morning routine. one for tough conversations. one for your creative process. one for managing stress."
- Beyond the book: before a hard meeting, naming the situation, how you want to act, the emotions that might interfere, and the value to keep central.

## Common mistakes
- Confusing it with goal-visualization or affirmations (exactly what the book rejects).
- Treating it as a one-time insight rather than an ongoing, per-situation practice.
- Skipping awareness of the context and jumping to a reflexive reaction.
