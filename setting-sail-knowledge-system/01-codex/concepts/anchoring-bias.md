> The first number or idea you encounter "anchors" all later judgments — you adjust from it, even when it's irrelevant.

## What it is
Over-relying on an initial reference point. In [[the-makeup-artist]] (Ch.4 §4.19): by
"centering the conversation around the ex," the pitch shifts the anchor from price to an
emotional story, so the cost is judged relative to *that*, not to the money itself.

## How it misleads
- **Sales:** a slashed "was $300, now $100" anchors you to $300 so $100 feels cheap.
- **Negotiation:** the first offer drags the final price toward it.
- **Everyday:** any salient first figure (a salary, an estimate) skewing what follows.

## How to counter it
- Notice the anchor and set your *own* reference (independent research, market value).
- Ask: *"What would I judge this to be with no number suggested?"*
- Beware anchors that conveniently favor the other party.
