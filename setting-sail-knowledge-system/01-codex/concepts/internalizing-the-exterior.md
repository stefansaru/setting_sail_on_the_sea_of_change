> As children we don't think — we copy. The values we absorb before we could question them stick for life, even the toxic ones. Growth requires detoxing them.

## Definition
A developmental account of where inherited values come from. "As kids, we don't really
think — we copy. We follow the crowd, because fitting in feels safe." This isn't
weakness, "it's wiring": for most of human history, children who didn't blend into the
tribe risked exclusion, and exclusion meant not surviving to reproduce. So we descend
from the conformers. Childhood "is all about absorbing... what's praised, what's
punished. We imitate what we see and internalize it as truth." Then puberty triggers
[[teenage-rebellion-as-survival|rebellion]] (an evolutionary push to leave the birth
tribe), and parenthood mellows us into building our own tribe. **But the early,
unquestioned values "don't just disappear. They stick. Even if they're toxic."** Hence
the task: "figure out which values are truly yours, and which were planted when you were
too young to know better. You can't grow if you don't detox first."

## Why it matters
It supplies the *mechanism* behind the [[camel-phase]] (Ch.3) and [[the-inner-critic]]
(Ch.4): both are made of internalized exterior voices. It reframes value-cleaning not as
ingratitude but as necessary maintenance — and grounds the whole self-authorship project
in evolutionary psychology (linking forward to Ch.2).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Not all inherited values are toxic.** Absorbed culture also transmits genuine wisdom,
  skills, and belonging; "detox" must keep the good, not purge everything old (cf. the
  [[camel-phase]]'s legitimate function).
- **Evolutionary "just-so" risk.** The tidy adaptive story (conformity → rebellion →
  parenthood) is plausible but presented more confidently than the evidence strictly supports.

## Practical examples
- From the book: childhood imitation hardening into "truth" you never chose.
- Beyond the book: catching yourself enforcing a rule on your kids that you never actually agreed with — just inherited.

## Common mistakes
- Assuming your values are "yours" without auditing their origin.
- Over-correcting: discarding *all* inherited values, including the sound ones.
