> Logical fallacies are errors in reasoning; cognitive biases are shortcuts in thinking. Both feel like accuracy but lead us off course. Knowing them doesn't make you immune — but awareness lets you pause, check, and choose better.

## Definition
The chapter's organizing idea. **Logical fallacies** are "errors in reasoning" — the link
between claim and proof is weak even when it "sounds convincing." **Cognitive biases** are
"shortcuts in thinking" — fast [[system-1-and-system-2|System 1]] heuristics that "help us
act fast but often lead us off course." Together they "shape how we argue, decide, and see
the world," and "politicians use them to win votes, marketers to sell, lawyers to
persuade." The book then catalogues ~40 of them (see the [[bias-catalog]]).

## Why it matters
"The illusion of accuracy" is the danger: these shortcuts *feel* like clear thinking. The
remedy isn't perfection but **awareness**: "knowing these patterns doesn't make you immune,
but it makes you aware. And awareness is power." When you catch "everyone agrees with me"
or feel pressure to act fast, you can pause and check. This is the
[[becoming-the-observer|observer stance]] applied to your own reasoning — the heart of the
liberation stratum.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Awareness has limits.** The [[blindspot-bias]] means we see others' biases, not our own;
  merely *knowing* a bias rarely debiases us — structure and feedback matter more than insight.
- **Heuristics are often *good*.** Many "biases" are efficient and usually-right shortcuts;
  labeling all fast thinking as error overcorrects (cf. expert intuition).

## Practical examples
- From the book: catching yourself thinking "this must be true because it's popular" ([[bandwagon-effect]]).
- Beyond the book: pausing on a "limited time only!" pitch ([[reactance]]/scarcity) before buying.

## Common mistakes
- Believing that knowing the list makes you unbiased (it doesn't — see [[blindspot-bias]]).
- Using bias-spotting as a weapon against others rather than a check on yourself.
- Trying to eliminate all fast thinking instead of knowing *when* to switch to System 2.
