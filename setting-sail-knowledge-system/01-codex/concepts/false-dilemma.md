> A logical fallacy that presents only two options when more exist — forcing a choice between "this or that" while hiding the rest.

## What it is
Framing a situation as a binary when there are really many options. Introduced in the
[[the-makeup-artist]] story (Ch.4): the pitch frames it as "pay 800 and impress your ex, or
pay 420 and settle" — "in reality, the client had many more options" (another artist, a
different use of budget).

## How it misleads
- **Sales:** "buy the premium version or get an inferior experience."
- **Politics:** "you're either with us or against us."
- **Everyday:** "we do it my way or the whole thing fails."

## How to counter it
- Ask: *"Are these really the only options?"* — look for the hidden third (and fourth) choice.
- Reject the framing, not just the answer.
- Beware binaries that conveniently favor the person presenting them.
