> Every habit runs the same four-step cycle: cue → craving → response → reward. Understand the loop, and you can build good habits or dismantle bad ones.

## Definition
The mechanism underlying every habit:
1. **Cue** — a trigger that signals the start of the habit.
2. **Craving** — the motivation or desire to act.
3. **Response** — the action itself.
4. **Reward** — the benefit that reinforces the loop.

The book's example: a water bottle on your desk (cue) → thirst (craving) → drinking
(response) → feeling refreshed (reward). Repeated, "this cycle strengthens into an
automatic behavior." Habits stick only when **all four** steps are present; remove any
one and the loop weakens — the key to [[build-and-break-habits]].

## Why it matters
It turns habits from mysterious forces of willpower into an engineerable system. Knowing
the four parts lets you *design* habits that stick (good) and *disrupt* loops that don't
serve you (bad) — by acting on the specific step that's easiest to change.

## Supporting ideas
- [[build-and-break-habits]] — the build method strengthens each step; the break method attacks each step.
- [[the-habit-penguins]] — Pip (forming), Pop (breaking), Smokey (quitting) dramatize the loop.
- [[system-1-and-system-2]] — a well-worn loop runs in System 1, automatically.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **The neat 4-step loop is a model.** Real habits involve overlapping cues, emotional
  states, and contexts; the loop is a useful lens, not a complete mechanism.
- **Reward timing matters** in ways the simple loop hides — rewards delayed (health) vs.
  immediate (junk food) explain why bad habits out-compete good ones (cf. [[hyperbolic-discounting]], forward link — Ch.7).

## Practical examples
- From the book: Pip smelling cocoa (cue) → wanting it (craving) → ordering (response) → the warm sip (reward).
- Beyond the book: phone buzz (cue) → urge to check (craving) → checking (response) → novelty hit (reward).

## Common mistakes
- Trying to change a habit by willpower alone instead of editing a loop step.
- Removing the response but leaving the cue and craving intact (white-knuckling).
- Building a good habit with no reward, so it never reinforces.
