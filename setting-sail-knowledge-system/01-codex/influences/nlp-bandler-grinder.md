> Neuro-Linguistic Programming and its founders supply the book's emotional toolkit — memory "submodality" editing, anchoring, the six-step reframe, and "the map is not the territory."

## Who
**Richard Bandler** and **John Grinder**, co-founders of **Neuro-Linguistic Programming
(NLP)** in the 1970s — a model of how language, perception, and mental representations
shape experience and can be deliberately changed.

## What the book borrows
- **Submodality editing** ([[memory-as-editable-film]], [[diffusing-visual-memories]],
  [[diffusing-audible-memories]]): changing a memory's brightness, color, size, sound,
  etc. to change its felt impact.
- **[[anchoring]]**: linking a state to a trigger.
- **The six-step reframe** ([[breaking-a-bad-habit-but-keeping-its-benefit]]): explicitly
  credited to Bandler and Grinder.
- **"The map is not the territory"** ([[the-map-is-not-the-territory]], Ch.5) — an NLP
  presupposition (originally Korzybski).

## How it is used (and bent)
- The book repackages NLP through friendly metaphors (the [[the-operations-room|operations
  room]], an internal "team," contact lists) and pairs it with mindfulness and Kahneman.
- **Important caveat:** NLP's empirical support is weak and contested in mainstream
  psychology; many techniques are anecdotal. The book presents them confidently as tools —
  useful framing for self-help, but worth flagging as not established science (see [[gaps]]).
