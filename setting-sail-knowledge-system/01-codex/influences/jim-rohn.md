> The American motivational speaker whose teaching "if you will change, everything will change for you" underwrites the book's sailor-not-the-wind principle.

## Who
Jim Rohn (1930–2009), American entrepreneur and motivational speaker, mentored as a
young man by businessman **John Earl Shoaff**. The book recounts Shoaff's advice to
the 25-year-old Rohn.

## What the book borrows
- The line **"if you will change, everything will change for you"** (Shoaff to Rohn), used as the seed of [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]].
- The broader Rohn-style stance that personal responsibility and self-development, not circumstances, determine outcomes — feeding the book's recurring [[theme/self-authorship]].

## How it is used (and bent)
- The book fuses Rohn's self-development optimism with the **sailor/wind** maritime metaphor that titles the book, and with Stoic control/acceptance — a synthesis broader than Rohn's original framing.
- Rohn's adjacent ideas surface elsewhere (forward links): "discipline is easier than motivation" and "mood follows action" (Ch.8) echo the same lineage.
