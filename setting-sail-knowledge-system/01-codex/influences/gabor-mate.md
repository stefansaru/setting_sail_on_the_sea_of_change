> The physician and trauma expert whose definition of trauma — "not what happened to you, but what happens inside you" — and Big-T/little-t distinction shape the book's view of emotional wounds.

## Who
Gabor Maté (b. 1944), Hungarian-Canadian physician and author, known for his work on trauma,
addiction, stress, and childhood development.

## What the book borrows
- **The definition of trauma** ([[depression-vs-low-self-esteem]], Ch.4): "trauma is not
  what happened to you, but what happens inside you as a result of what happened to you."
- **Big-T vs. little-t trauma** ([[trauma-big-t-little-t]], Ch.8): severe life-altering events
  vs. smaller hurts that accumulate.
- The framing of **depression as a response to unresolved emotion/trauma** — "not weakness or
  failure but a natural biological reaction to abnormal events."

## How it is used (and bent)
- The book uses Maté to *destigmatize* emotional pain and to motivate processing it
  ([[emotional-baggage]], [[most-fears-are-memories]]).
- It leans on his compassionate framing but pairs it with light-touch self-help tools; Maté's
  own work emphasizes deeper therapeutic and relational healing — a gap the book underplays
  (see [[gaps]] on self-processing vs. professional care).
