> The psychologist whose *Thinking, Fast and Slow* gives the book its model of the mind as two systems — and the basis for understanding cognitive bias.

## Who
Daniel Kahneman (1934–2024), psychologist and Nobel laureate in economics, author of
*Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011) and a founder, with Amos Tversky, of the study of
cognitive biases and behavioral economics.

## What the book borrows
- **System 1 / System 2** ([[system-1-and-system-2]]): the fast/automatic vs.
  slow/deliberate model, used as the engine for habits, perception, and emotion.
- The implicit basis for **cognitive biases** — System 1's shortcuts misfiring — which
  the book develops fully in Ch.7 ([[cognitive-bias]] forward link).

## How it is used (and bent)
- The book uses System 1/2 as a practical, almost characterological framework (two
  "agents"), where Kahneman treated them as a convenient shorthand for processes, not
  literal entities.
- It pairs Kahneman with mindfulness ([[becoming-the-observer]]) — a move Kahneman
  didn't make — to argue you can *watch* the two systems and choose between them.
- It emphasizes System 1's *errors* more than Kahneman's nuanced account of when
  intuition is reliable (expertise with good feedback).
