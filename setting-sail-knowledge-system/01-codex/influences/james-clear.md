> The author of *Atomic Habits*, source of the book's habit framework: tiny changes compounding, the habit loop, identity-based habits, stacking, and the two-minute rule.

## Who
James Clear (b. 1986), American author of *Atomic Habits* (2018), one of the best-selling
books on habit formation.

## What the book borrows
- **Small changes, big results** ([[atomic-habits]]): improvement comes from tiny,
  compounding adjustments, not big goals.
- **The habit loop** ([[the-habit-loop]]): cue → craving → response → reward.
- **Identity-based habits**: align habits with who you want to *become*.
- **[[habit-stacking]]** and the **[[two-minute-rule]]**.
- **Environment design** and patience/continuous improvement.

## How it is used (and bent)
- The book renders Clear's framework through its own metaphors — the bookshelf, and the
  [[the-habit-penguins|penguins]] Pip, Pop, and Smokey — making it narrative and memorable.
- It integrates Clear's ideas with [[system-1-and-system-2]] (habits as System 1
  programs) and with the broader self-authorship arc (identity habits ↔ creating your values).
