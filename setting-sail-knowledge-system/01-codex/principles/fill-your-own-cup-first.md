> You cannot help anyone if you are not okay yourself — secure your own oxygen mask before assisting others.

## Definition
Caring for yourself is the precondition for caring for others, not its opposite. The
book anchors this in the airplane safety instruction — put on your own oxygen mask
before helping others — and in the question "are you in your own top five?" Looking
after yourself "is not selfish, it's necessary." Self-care is reframed as the
*infrastructure* of generosity rather than a competitor to it.

## Why it matters
Most depletion comes from a hidden category error: treating self-sacrifice as the
highest form of love. The book argues the reverse — that giving from depletion
breeds resentment, irritability ("we get cranky and snap at people"), and a silent
expectation of repayment, while giving from fullness is effortless and free. If you
run yourself empty, you eventually have nothing to give *and* become a burden
yourself. Filling your own cup first is therefore the most pro-social thing you can do.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Can rationalize selfishness.** "Self-care first" can become a permanent excuse to
  never sacrifice — yet genuine love sometimes *does* require giving past comfort
  (see [[giving-from-abundance-vs-sacrifice]] tensions). The principle is about sustainability,
  not exemption from sacrifice.
- **Privilege blind spot.** Some people (caregivers, parents of infants, the poor)
  genuinely cannot "fill their cup first" on demand; the principle assumes a degree of
  slack that not everyone has.
- **Tension with duty.** In emergencies, helping others before yourself can be exactly right.

## Practical examples
- From the book: "when you start using just your surplus energy to power your relationships, you become a source of energy to others from a point of abundance, not from the point of a draining battery."
- Beyond the book: a parent who guards sleep and exercise becomes more patient, not less available, to their children.

## Common mistakes
- Equating self-care with selfishness and feeling guilty for it (the book targets the word "guilty" directly — see [[limiting-words]]).
- Waiting for others to recharge you instead of taking charge of your own energy.
- Giving until empty, then resenting the people you gave to.
