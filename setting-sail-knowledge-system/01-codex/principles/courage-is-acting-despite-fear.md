> Courage is not the absence of fear but the willingness to move forward while afraid.

## Definition
Courage is reframed not as fearlessness but as forward motion in the presence of
fear. Fear is treated as a normal, expected companion ("like having popcorn at the
movies"), not a signal to stop. The book ties courage specifically to two acts:
*letting go* of the old and *moving toward* the new — the transitions between every
phase of the [[three-metamorphoses]].

## Why it matters
If courage required the absence of fear, almost no one would ever act, because every
meaningful transition (saying no, leaving, creating) is frightening. Redefining
courage as *action despite fear* makes it available to everyone, all the time.

## Supporting ideas
- [[inner-child]] — locating the fear ("is it me or my inner child who's frightened?") makes it manageable.
- [[reassure-your-inner-child]] — the technique that converts this principle into a usable move.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Fear is sometimes accurate.** Not all fear is an inner child to soothe; some is
  data about real danger. Courage must be paired with judgment, or it becomes
  recklessness.
- **Repeated "push through" can mask burnout** — the camel also "courageously"
  carries burdens it should drop ([[camel-phase]]).

## Practical examples
- From the book: even the camel "has the courage to journey across the desert"; the lion and child show courage navigating their paths.
- Beyond the book: speaking up in a meeting with a pounding heart — acting before the fear subsides.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting to "feel ready" / unafraid before acting (readiness rarely arrives first).
- Confusing courage with the suppression of fear rather than coexistence with it.
- Applying courage indiscriminately, ignoring fear that is genuinely protective.
