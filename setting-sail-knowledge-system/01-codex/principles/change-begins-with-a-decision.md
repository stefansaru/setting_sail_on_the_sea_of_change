> No book, person, or technique can change you until you decide to change — for yourself, not for others. Real change starts when you say "enough" and mean it.

## Definition
"The first step is deciding if you actually want to change. Not for others. Not because
someone told you to. But because you're tired of staying stuck." The book is honest about
the trap: "many people say they want to grow, but deep down they're waiting for someone else
to fix things. Or they want the reward without the effort." The remedy is brutal honesty —
"admit it to yourself. Honesty is where change begins" — and a genuine decision: "real
change starts when you say 'enough,' and mean it."

## Why it matters
It's the precondition for everything else in the book: tools and insight are inert without
an owned decision to use them. It guards against passive self-help (collecting books and
quotes while waiting to be fixed) and connects to [[taking-responsibility]] (grab the wheel)
and [[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]] (the response is yours).

## Supporting ideas
- Honesty first: admit if you're waiting to be rescued or want reward without effort.
- "You don't need to be perfect. Just willing to take the next step, however small" ([[mood-follows-action]]).
- The book "can only walk with you if you're ready."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Decision alone isn't enough** — the book itself stresses consistency over time
  ([[every-master-was-once-a-disaster]]); a single decision must be renewed daily.
- "Just decide" can underplay genuine barriers (depression, circumstance) that make deciding hard.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the moment you stop waiting for someone else to fix things and own the choice.
- Beyond the book: the difference between "I should quit smoking" and the day you actually decide to.

## Common mistakes
- Wanting the reward without the effort (and not admitting it).
- Changing "for others" rather than from your own decision (it won't hold).
- Mistaking reading/consuming self-help for the decision to act.
