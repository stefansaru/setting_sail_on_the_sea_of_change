> Values are the deeper, near-constant parameters of a life; set them first, because every goal that fights your values will fail.

## Definition
Values are not opinions or moods — they are the stable guiding parameters that
remain mostly constant across a lifetime and shape every decision. The principle:
clarify your values *before* committing to goals, projects, or a "course," because
a goal misaligned with a value will quietly sabotage itself.

## Why it matters
A life is a mission made of many projects, each with objectives. When a project
contradicts a value, you "naturally struggle to overcome obstacles" — willpower
leaks out through the misalignment. Defining values first is upstream leverage: it
prevents you from succeeding at the wrong things.

## Supporting ideas
- [[defining-your-own-values]] — the *how*: strip external expectation, listen to the [[inner-voice]].

- [[child-phase]] — value-*creation* (not acceptance or rejection) is the mature act this principle demands.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Values are partly discovered through action, not only before it.** You often
  learn what you value by trying things and noticing what resonates — so "define
  first" and "act to discover" are in productive tension (see [[activity-journal]]).
- **Values drift.** The book itself admits people "grow apart as their core values
  change over time" ([[values-alignment-in-relationships]]) — so "near-constant" is
  an approximation, and the list needs periodic re-examination.

## Practical examples
- From the book: planning a lifelong mission of multiple projects — "the key is to first define your values before setting your course."
- Beyond the book: choosing a job offer — a higher salary that violates a freedom/autonomy value predicts burnout despite the "win."

## Common mistakes
- Borrowing values wholesale from family, culture, or peers (this is the [[camel-phase]] error).
- Writing values so rigidly (pure [[values-on-a-spectrum|binary values]]) that real life can't fit them.
- Setting ambitious goals while never having articulated what they're *for*.
