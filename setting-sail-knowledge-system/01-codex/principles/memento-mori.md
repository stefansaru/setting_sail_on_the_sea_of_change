> "You will die." Not morbid — a call to action. Time is finite, so live now, with urgency and purpose.

## Definition
Gary Vaynerchuk's blunt "shortest motivational quote": **"you will die."** The book ties it
to the ancient **memento mori** ("remember you will die") — not fear or despair, but "a call
to action, a push to live with urgency and purpose." Death is "the great equalizer" (the
reaper's scythe, an agricultural tool, "doesn't discriminate"). The takeaway: stop waiting —
"take the calculated risk, chase the dream, say what needs to be said."

## Why it matters
Finitude is the ultimate reframe of priorities: knowing time will run out makes the
trivial fall away and the meaningful urgent. It powers the whole chapter's call to *act now*
([[mood-follows-action]], [[the-ruler-of-life]]) and grounds [[meaning-is-created-not-found]]
— you have a limited window to paint the canvas.

## Supporting ideas
- [[the-ruler-of-life]] / [[estimate-your-available-time]] — making finitude visual and concrete.
- "Memento mori" across philosophy, art, and history.
- "Be who you are and say what you feel, because people who mind don't matter and people who matter don't mind."

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Urgency can become anxiety.** "You will die, so hurry" can fuel frantic productivity and
  fear of "wasting" time — the opposite of the book's [[theme/journey-over-destination|presence]] message.
- Constant death-awareness suits some temperaments and distresses others.

## Practical examples
- From the book: the scythe that levels "tall or short, strong or weak."
- Beyond the book: a health scare reordering someone's priorities overnight.

## Common mistakes
- Reading it as morbid rather than mobilizing.
- Letting it create panic/hustle instead of clarity and courage.
- Knowing time is finite yet still deferring what matters ("someday").
