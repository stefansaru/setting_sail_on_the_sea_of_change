> Motivation is unreliable — it fades with mood and energy. Discipline means designing your life so the right choice is easier than the wrong one. It's about systems, not willpower.

## Definition
"Most of us wait to feel motivated before we act. But motivation is unreliable — it fades
with your mood, your energy, and your circumstances. Discipline is different. It doesn't
depend on how you feel... Discipline means designing your life so the right choice is
easier than the wrong one. It's not about willpower or toughness — it's about **systems**."
A key mechanism is **decision fatigue**: every choice drains your "self-control battery,"
which is why evenings collapse into snacking and scrolling.

## Why it matters
It reframes self-control from a heroic trait (that you either have or don't) into an
*engineering problem*: reduce choices, prepare in advance, and design the environment so
good habits are obvious and bad ones are hard. This makes consistency achievable for
ordinary willpower — the practical backbone of [[atomic-habits]] and
[[consistency-beats-restarting]].

## Supporting ideas
- [[mood-follows-action]] — its companion: act first; discipline removes the need to feel like it.
- Decision fatigue — protect energy by cutting trivial choices (Obama's two suit colors; Jobs's turtleneck).
- "Make the good habits obvious, the bad ones hard" (environment design, see [[atomic-habits]]).

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Systems can't fully replace motivation** — deep purpose ([[the-will-to-meaning]]) sustains
  effort that no environment design can manufacture; the strongest lives have both.
- Over-systematizing can become rigid; some flexibility and spontaneity matter too.

## Practical examples
- From the book: athletes preparing gear the night before; "put fruit on the counter and hide junk food."
- Beyond the book: automating savings so "discipline" requires no monthly willpower.

## Common mistakes
- Waiting for motivation to strike before acting.
- Relying on willpower at the end of a decision-fatigued day.
- Designing goals without designing the *environment* that makes them easy.
