> Your body and mind were built for a hunter-gatherer world that no longer exists. Most modern struggles are ancient survival software misfiring in an environment it never evolved for.

## Definition
"Even though technology has advanced very rapidly, our biology still remains tied to a
hunter-gatherer lifestyle." We evolved for "small social groups, a plant-based diet with
occasional meat, and constant movement" — "we are not built for sedentary office work,
processed foods, or modern excesses." Evolution "takes time — lots of it," and the
modern world arrived in "an evolutionary blink." The result is *mismatch*: instincts
that were adaptive in scarcity and danger now misfire in abundance and safety. The
book's emblem is [[insects-and-artificial-light|the insect spiraling into a lightbulb]]:
"just like insects weren't built for artificial light, humans weren't built for modern
life."

## Why it matters
It is the unifying explanation for a huge range of human suffering — chronic stress,
overeating, status anxiety, jealousy, social-media pain. Reframing these as *misfiring
ancient software* rather than personal defects is both compassionate and empowering:
"recognizing when your mind is overreacting helps you stay calm, regain control." It
grounds the whole [[stratum/inheritance|inheritance stratum]] — the programming the
[[camel-phase|camel]] carries is partly evolutionary.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **"Just-so stories" risk.** Evolutionary-psychology explanations are often
  plausible-but-unfalsifiable; the book presents adaptive narratives confidently where
  the science is debated.
- **Not everything is mismatch.** Some modern struggles are cultural, structural, or
  learned — attributing them all to evolution can excuse or oversimplify.
- **Mismatch isn't destiny.** Knowing an instinct is ancient doesn't automatically
  defuse it (the rest of the book is about *how* to override the programming).

## Practical examples
- From the book: the deadline that triggers the same response as a lion ([[fight-or-flight]]).
- Beyond the book: sugar cravings adaptive in scarcity, harmful amid abundant processed food.

## Common mistakes
- Treating misfiring instincts as personal failings ("what's wrong with me?").
- The opposite: using "it's just evolution" as an excuse not to change behavior.
- Over-applying the lens to explain everything.
