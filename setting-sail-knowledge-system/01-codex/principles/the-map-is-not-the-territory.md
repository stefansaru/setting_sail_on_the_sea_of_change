> Your mind's model of the world is not the world — it's a simplified, subjective version built from your senses, memories, and language. Useful, but never the thing itself.

## Definition
Perception is interpretation, not recording. The brain filters an overwhelming sensory
stream into "a carefully constructed interpretation — one shaped by biology, past
experiences, and subconscious biases." The book gathers several expressions of this:
- **[[boxed-in-with-peepholes|"boxed in with peepholes"]]** — each of us sees through narrow openings (senses, beliefs, history); "1000 monks, 1000 religions."
- **Magritte's [[ceci-nest-pas-une-pipe|"this is not a pipe"]]** — the image is not the object.
- **NLP's "the map is not the territory"** — your mental map is "useful but incomplete," and misleads if mistaken for reality.
- **Lao-Tzu** — "the path that can be walked is not the eternal path"; reality exceeds words.
- **Covey** — "we see the world, not as it is, but as we are."

## Why it matters
It is the epistemic humility at the root of the book's psychology: if everyone navigates
a different map, then conflict is often a clash of *maps*, not of truth (→
[[explain-dont-force]]), and your own certainty deserves suspicion (→ the cognitive
biases of Ch.7). It also empowers: since you build the map, you can [[becoming-the-observer|notice and revise]] it.

## Supporting ideas
- [[system-1-and-system-2]] — System 1's instant filtering *is* the map-making.
- The double-slit experiment: "the act of looking affects what we see... what we focus on shapes what becomes real to us."
- [[the-problem-is-not-the-problem]] — your map of an event, not the event, drives your reaction.

## Opposing ideas & tensions
- **Relativism risk.** "Everyone has their own map" can slide into "all views are equally
  valid" — but some maps predict reality far better than others. Maps differ in *accuracy*,
  not just perspective.
- **The double-slit / "observation shapes reality" framing** is a popular-science
  stretch; quantum measurement doesn't straightforwardly mean "focus makes things real" in daily life.

## Practical examples
- From the book: witnesses to the same event interpreting it in "entirely different ways."
- Beyond the book: two people remembering the same argument with incompatible "facts" — different maps.

## Common mistakes
- Mistaking your map for the territory ("this is just how things are").
- Assuming others see what you see, then judging them for "getting it wrong."
- Sliding from humility into "nothing is knowable."
