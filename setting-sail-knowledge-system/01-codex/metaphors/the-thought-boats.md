> Stand on a dock and watch your thoughts pass like boats. You don't have to climb aboard. You're not your thoughts — you're the one watching them drift by.

## The image
"See each thought as a boat. You're standing on a dock. The sea is your mind. Boats come
and go all day long." Label them by cargo: "here comes the 'I'm not good enough' boat,"
"the 'what if everything goes wrong' ferry," "the 'I should check my phone' kayak." Some
are familiar; "they go in circles." The key: "you don't have to get on... you stay on the
dock." If you notice you've climbed aboard, "gently return to the dock."

## What it teaches
A practice-metaphor for [[becoming-the-observer]]: thoughts are passing objects, not
truth or commands, and you can decline to board them. "You're not your thoughts. You're
the one observing them. Your power is in choosing whether to sail with them — or stay
grounded."

## Why it sticks
Naming and labeling intrusive thoughts as specific little boats ("the doubt dinghy") is
both vivid and faintly comic, which itself loosens their grip — and the dock gives the
observer a concrete place to stand. (The book notes you can swap boats for clouds or
trains.)
