> René Magritte painted a pipe and labeled it "this is not a pipe" — because the image is not the object. Your mental picture of reality is never reality itself.

## The image
Magritte's famous painting shows a pipe over the caption "ceci n'est pas une pipe."
Though it depicts a pipe, "what we see is only an image, not the object itself." As
Magritte explained, "you could not fill this pipe with tobacco. It's only a
representation."

## What it teaches
A crisp artistic statement of [[the-map-is-not-the-territory]]: every representation —
image, word, mental model — is a stand-in, never the thing. The book pairs it with NLP's
"the map is not the territory" and Lao-Tzu's "the name that can be spoken is not the
eternal name."

## Why it sticks
The painting is iconic and the paradox is delightful: a picture of a pipe insisting it
isn't one. It makes an abstract epistemology lesson instantly memorable and even witty.
