> Life's challenges are video-game levels; each one you beat adds a polished layer to your self-esteem — like a superhero building armor one battle at a time.

## The image
Challenges are "the toughest levels in a video game" — and there's "more than one way
to beat them": the "stealthy ninja" who dodges, or the "fearless warrior" who charges
in. Either way, "each time you overcome an obstacle... you add a new, polished layer to
your self-esteem." You're "leveling up in the game of you," and "like superheroes
designing their own battle suits, you build your armor one challenge at a time."

## What it teaches
The core image for [[self-esteem-is-earned]]: confidence is accumulated XP, gained by
*overcoming*, not granted. It also legitimizes different *styles* of overcoming (wit,
persistence, willpower) — you don't have to be the warrior to level up.

## Why it sticks
Gaming and superhero imagery make an abstract psychological process (earning
confidence) feel concrete, gradual, and even fun — every hurdle is XP, every win a new
piece of armor.
