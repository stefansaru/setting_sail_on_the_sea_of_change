> Helping others drains your battery; if you never recharge, you run flat — cranky, snapping, with nothing left to give.

## The image
"Think of your energy like a battery. When you help others, you use some of this
energy. If you don't recharge, and just wait for others to do this for you, you will
often end up feeling drained and tired." A low battery makes us "cranky" and prone to
"snap at people." Recharging yourself means you can "give even more to others in the
long run."

## What it teaches
The mechanism behind [[fill-your-own-cup-first]] and
[[giving-from-abundance-vs-sacrifice]]: generosity has an energy cost, and the source
of your charge (self-managed vs. waiting on others) determines whether you give from
abundance or depletion.

## Why it sticks
Everyone has watched a phone die. The battery is an instantly legible model for an
invisible inner resource — including the warning that *waiting* for others to charge
you leaves you stranded.
