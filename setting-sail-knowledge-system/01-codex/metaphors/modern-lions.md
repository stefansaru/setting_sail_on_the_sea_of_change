> The lions our ancestors fled had claws and teeth; ours are deadlines, emails, and worries — same fierce stress response, no actual predator.

## The image
"Just like ancestors faced a lion's fierce gaze, we face our modern lions in countless
ways. No claws or teeth, yet the pressure is there, in work, in life, stress lurking
everywhere... the lions we face are not of flesh and bone, but worries and deadlines, in a
modern tone." A tight deadline triggers the identical surge a real lion once did — "just
an email."

## What it teaches
The memory hook for [[fight-or-flight]] and [[evolutionary-mismatch]]: our acute stress
response can't distinguish a predator from a pressure, so harmless "modern lions" keep us
in costly chronic alert. Naming a stressor "a modern lion" is itself a calming,
[[becoming-the-observer|observer]] move.

## Why it sticks
The contrast — a literal lion vs. an email triggering the same panic — is darkly funny and
instantly memorable, and "what's my modern lion right now?" is a usable self-check.
