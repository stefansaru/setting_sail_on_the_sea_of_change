> Every shared moment is a brick: positive ones reinforce the wall, unresolved negative ones become weak bricks — and relationships collapse at their weakest clusters.

## The image
"A relationship is like building a house, brick by brick — each moment in time, each
shared experience adds to its structure." A strong positive moment is "a solid
brick"; an unresolved negative experience is "a weak, crumbling brick." Relationships
rarely collapse all at once — "they break down at the weakest points, where clusters
of negative experiences form fragile walls."

## What it teaches
Long-term love is the cumulative result of many small interactions, not grand
gestures or luck. It echoes the book's habit material: outcomes are the compounding of
repeated small actions. The practical implication: address conflicts before they
"stack up," and balance challenges with reinforcing positive experiences.

## Why it sticks
The brick/wall image makes an invisible process (relational erosion) physical and
inspectable: you can picture *which* bricks are weak and where the wall is thin.
