> A long inner hallway hung with your framed memories — each with a title and a lesson. The good ones glow; the hard ones rest in dim light, processed and respected, no longer pulling you down.

## The image
"Imagine walking through a long, quiet hallway in your mind — your personal hall of fame.
The walls are lined with framed memories, each displayed like a piece of art." Each has "a
title above it, and a short conclusion written clearly underneath." The good ones are "lit
up — bright, warm, full of color." The harder ones "are here too — but in dim light,
softened by time... still part of your story, but no longer pulling you down. Just
acknowledged. Respected." It holds "not everything — just the turning points."

## What it teaches
The destination image for [[memory-as-editable-film]] and [[mindful-memory-loops]]: a
curated inner gallery where edited memories are *filed* with their titles and lessons. It
makes the goal tangible — not erasing pain, but giving each memory "a home" and a purpose.
(It's also the device used in the elevator-phobia case: hang the reframed photo "you're
ok, you're safe" on the wall.)

## Why it sticks
An art gallery of your life — bright triumphs and softly-lit scars side by side — is a
dignified, peaceful image that reframes even painful memories as respected exhibits rather
than ambushes.
