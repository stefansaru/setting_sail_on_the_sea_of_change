> For millions of years insects navigated by the moon; then humans invented bulbs, and a flawless instinct became a death trap. We're the insects, and modern life is the artificial light.

## The image
Flying insects "relied on celestial lights — the moon and stars — as fixed points to
navigate." Then humans "filled the world with artificial lights," which are "much closer
and far more confusing." Expecting the light to be far above, the insect "spirals around
the source," "trapped in an evolutionary loophole... until exhaustion or death." The
problem: "evolution takes time," and artificial light arrived in "an evolutionary blink."
The punchline: "just like insects weren't built for artificial light, humans weren't built
for modern life."

## What it teaches
The definitive metaphor for [[evolutionary-mismatch]]: a once-perfect adaptation becomes
self-destructive when the environment changes faster than evolution can. Our cravings,
stress responses, and social instincts are our "artificial lights" — ancient guidance
systems disoriented by a novel world.

## Why it sticks
The moth-to-flame is a familiar, slightly tragic sight, here given a precise mechanism
(celestial navigation hijacked). It makes "we're mismatched to modernity" visceral and
hard to forget.
