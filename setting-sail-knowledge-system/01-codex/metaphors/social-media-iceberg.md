> What you see on social media is just the tip of the iceberg — curated wins and filtered moments, with all the doubt and sadness hidden beneath the surface.

## The image
"On social media, everyone looks perfect. People post their biggest wins, their best
angles, their filtered moments... but this isn't real life. What you see is just the tip
of the iceberg. A glimpse of a moment, out of context. You don't see the doubts, the
sadness. You don't see the full picture." The result: comparison makes "many people feel
like they're not enough."

## What it teaches
The mechanism driving [[toxic-positivity]] and eroding [[self-esteem]]: we compare our
full, messy interior to others' curated exterior — an unfair comparison by construction.
It's a vivid case for [[awareness-of-both]] (the hidden underwater "bad" is always there)
and connects to the [[self-esteem]] chapter's warning about social-media comparison.

## Why it sticks
The iceberg is a classic, instantly-grasped image of "most of it is hidden," and maps
perfectly onto the gap between posted highlight-reels and real life.
