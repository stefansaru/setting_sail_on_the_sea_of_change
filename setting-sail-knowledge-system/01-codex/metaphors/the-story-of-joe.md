> A stranded man prays for rescue and refuses a boat, a plane, and a helicopter — each time saying "God will save me." In the afterlife he learns: "I sent you a boat, a plane, and a helicopter."

## The image
Joe, stranded on an island, prays to be saved and trusts divine rescue. A boat comes — "no
thank you, God will save me." A plane drops a parachute — refused. A helicopter lands —
refused. Joe dies and asks God why he was abandoned. God replies: "my child, I sent you a
boat, a plane, and a helicopter."

## What it teaches
A parable about [[be-proactive|proactivity]] and [[taking-responsibility]]: help and
opportunity often arrive in unglamorous, unexpected forms, and *recognizing and accepting*
them is your responsibility. Passive waiting — even faithful waiting — squanders the very
means of rescue. (Pairs with [[change-what-you-can-accept-what-you-cant]]: act on what's offered.)

## Why it sticks
The escalating rescues (boat → plane → helicopter) and the deadpan punchline make a serious
point about self-sabotaging passivity land with humor and a sting.
