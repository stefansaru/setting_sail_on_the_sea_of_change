> A car in motion without a destination just burns fuel. Decide where you're going first, and every turn, stop, and refuel makes sense.

## The image
"You can think of life like a long road trip. Before you start the engine, decide where
you're going. A car in motion without a destination only burns fuel. When you know the
destination, every turn, stop, and refuel makes sense. The map changes, the weather
shifts, but the compass stays true. The end you hold in mind is what keeps your hands
steady on the wheel."

## What it teaches
The image for [[start-with-the-end-in-mind]]: a chosen destination makes every smaller
decision coherent, and distinguishes purposeful movement from busy fuel-burning. The
"compass, not a map" distinction lets the route change while the direction holds.

## Why it sticks
The road trip is universally relatable, and "a car in motion without a destination just
burns fuel" is a sharp one-line indictment of busy, directionless living.
