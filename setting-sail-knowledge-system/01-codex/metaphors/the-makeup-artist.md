> A makeup artist learns to stop defending her price and start selling the feeling — "is it worth 380 rand to leave your ex speechless?" — showing that people buy meaning, not products.

## The image
Entrepreneur Vusi Thembekwayo coaches a makeup artist who charges 800 rand. When
clients push back, she says "no, that's my time, that's my skill" — which "ends the
conversation and pushes the client away." Vusi reframes: he asks where she's going (a
wedding), whether she has an ex (yes), and "what's it worth to you to make sure your ex
sees you looking the best you've ever looked?" The 380-rand gap between budget and price
now sells itself: "is it worth 380 rand to feel incredible and leave your ex
speechless?"

## What it teaches
The headline lesson for [[creating-value]] and [[money-follows-value]]: "people don't
just buy makeup. they buy confidence, pride, and the feeling of being unforgettable."
**Value is in what the product *means* to the client.** The book then turns the story
into a *cautionary* lesson: it dissects the pitch's [[false-dilemma]],
[[appeal-to-emotion]], [[anchoring-bias]], and [[loss-aversion-bias]] — persuasion
"built only on emotional hooks can cross into manipulation."

## Why it sticks
A concrete, slightly cheeky scene (impressing an ex) makes an abstract principle
(value > product) vivid — and doubles as a worked example of biases in action, linking
the money and cognitive-bias chapters.
