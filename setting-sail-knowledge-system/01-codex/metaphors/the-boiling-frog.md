> Drop a frog in boiling water and it leaps out; put it in cool water and slowly raise the heat, and it stays until it's too late. We're often that frog.

## The image
"If you drop a frog into boiling water, it jumps out instantly. But put it in cool water and
slowly turn up the heat? It stays there, adjusting — until it's too late." When change "hits
like a tidal wave, we react"; but when it "creeps in slowly — one compromise, one routine,
one 'just for now' at a time — we might not even notice that we're settling into something
we'd clearly disagree to." The move: "pay attention to the 'temperature' of your life…
leap before the water boils."

## What it teaches
The central image for [[comfortable-traps]]: gradual decline is invisible precisely because
it's gradual. It warns that adaptability — usually a virtue — can quietly become a trap, and
prescribes vigilance over slow change.

## Why it sticks
The (apocryphal but vivid) frog is an unforgettable picture of slow, unnoticed danger —
"check the temperature of your life" becomes a usable self-prompt.
