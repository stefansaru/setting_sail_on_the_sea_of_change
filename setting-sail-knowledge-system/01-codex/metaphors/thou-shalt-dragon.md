> A gold-scaled dragon whose every scale is a "thou shalt" — the embodiment of inherited societal commandments, demanding conformity and forbidding "I will."

## The image
As Cammy the lion travels, society's enforcers (vultures) keep blocking her with
commands — "thou shalt not pass," "thou shalt pay water tax" — each transforming into
an unbreakable **golden shield**. Massed together, the shields assemble into a
"tremendous dragon," the mighty **Thou-Shalt Dragon**, which "slithered through the
sands of tradition, breathing fire made of societal expectations." Each scale glitters
with a "thou-shalt"; its eyes glow "with the insistence of conformity." It demands
Cammy return to her "baggage duties."

## What it teaches
The dragon is inherited morality and societal expectation made flesh — the
collected "shoulds" of the [[camel-phase]] that the [[lion-phase]] must confront.
Drawn directly from Nietzsche's *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* (the dragon on whose scales
"all values of all things glitter"). It teaches that the values we inherit can feel
like an inviolable external law until we dare to say "I will."

## Why it sticks
A dragon armored in golden "thou shalts" is a single unforgettable image for an
abstract, diffuse force (social pressure). The detail that its scales are *shields*
made of others' commands makes the metaphor mechanically satisfying.
