> A ruler stretched across your lifespan. Mark your achievements and obstacles on it; shade what's lived and what's expected — and see the blank stretch that's still yours to fill.

## The image
"Imagine a ruler stretching across the timeline of your life" (the book marks 122 — Jeanne
Calment's age — for perspective). Everything you've experienced "lands somewhere on the
ruler of life." You mark your top achievements (§8.2–8.4) and the obstacles you've overcome
(§8.5); each challenge "adds a new mark." Later you shade from birth to your current age and
from the far end back to life expectancy — and "what's left in the middle is your available
time… your playground" (see [[estimate-your-available-time]]).

## What it teaches
A single device that carries several lessons: your life is a finite line ([[memento-mori]]),
your achievements are earned marks ([[self-esteem-is-earned]]), and the blank space is yours
to author ([[meaning-is-created-not-found]]). It turns abstract time into something you can
literally look at.

## Why it sticks
A ruler you can mark and shade makes time tangible and personal — the shaded "already lived"
section and the finite "blank middle" land with quiet force.
