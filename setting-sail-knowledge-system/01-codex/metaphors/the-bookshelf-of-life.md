> Your time and energy are a bookshelf; you're the librarian. Each book is a passion, commitment, or habit — and you can't add a new one without making room.

## The image
"Think about your time and energy like a bookshelf. Every book represents a part of your
life… and you're the librarian. Want to add something new? Something has to go. You can't
cram an extra book into an already overflowing shelf." The closing poem extends it: "our
shelf is our story, each item a page, yet too many chapters — becoming a cage… let some
books depart, which are already closer to someone else's heart."

## What it teaches
The image for [[decluttering]] and [[declutter-your-time]]: a life has finite shelf space,
so every "yes" requires a "no." It reframes letting go as *curation*, not loss — and casts
you as the one in charge of the collection. (It's also how [[atomic-habits]] frames building
habits: choosing which books to keep within reach.)

## Why it sticks
Everyone knows a shelf has limits and that you're the one who arranges it — making the
abstract scarcity of time concrete and the act of removal feel responsible, even generous
("closer to someone else's heart").
