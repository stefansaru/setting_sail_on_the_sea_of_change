> Quitting halfway is like planting a walnut and digging it up days later to check it — you end up with no tree and just one walnut. Growth needs you to leave it in the ground.

## The image
"It's like planting a walnut, then digging it up a few days later because you don't see
results. You end up with no tree, and just one walnut. Real growth takes time. You have
to leave the walnut in the ground. Water it. Wait. That's how you get more walnuts — not
instantly, but eventually." A companion lesson: also "plant faster-growing crops" so
you're not waiting on one slow tree alone.

## What it teaches
The core image for [[consistency-beats-restarting]]: results lag effort, and impatience
that uproots progress guarantees you never see the harvest. The "plant fast crops too"
addendum ties to [[diversify-for-resilience]].

## Why it sticks
The absurd, vivid futility of digging up a seed to "check on it" makes impatience
visible — everyone instantly sees why it's self-defeating, and the image transfers to
any slow-compounding effort.
