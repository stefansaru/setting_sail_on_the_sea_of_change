> Giving from sacrifice is a tree straining and bending under the weight of its own fruit — noble, but bought with weariness and quiet resentment.

## The image
"Giving from a place of sacrifice is akin to a tree that bends under the weight of its
own fruit, straining to support the abundance it bears." Though "noble in its
intention," it costs "the weariness felt after stretching too thin, the hollowness of
waiting for a thank-you that comes too late, and the quiet resentment that builds when
the scales of give-and-take are imbalanced." It casts "a shadow of obligation over
relationships."

## What it teaches
The cautionary half of [[giving-from-abundance-vs-sacrifice]]: the same generous act,
when drawn from depletion rather than overflow, slowly damages both giver and
relationship. Paired with [[river-overflowing-its-banks]].

## Why it sticks
A fruit tree bowing and cracking under its own bounty is a quietly tragic image —
generosity becoming a structural strain — and it names the tell-tale "silent hope of
reciprocity."
