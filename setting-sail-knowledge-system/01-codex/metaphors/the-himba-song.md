> In a (likely allegorical) story, each Himba child has a song chosen before birth; when they err, the village gathers and sings their song — anchoring them to who they are, not what they did wrong.

## The image
A story told of the Himba people of northern Namibia: "each Himba child has a unique song
chosen before birth." The mother hears it under a tree, teaches it to the father and the
village, and "that song belongs to the child for life." When the child later makes
mistakes, "the community doesn't rush to punish. Instead, the people gather around and sing
the child's song" — reminding them "not of the failure, but of their true identity." The
book is explicit that this is "more allegory than fact" with "no reputable sources," but
keeps it for the idea.

## What it teaches
[[anchoring]] applied to *identity*: "when you reconnect with your own 'song' — your values,
your mission, the things that define you — you find direction again. Mistakes lose their
weight. Belonging replaces blame." It models how anchoring can be "a tool for healing
rather than blame."

## Why it sticks
The image of a whole village singing someone back to themselves is moving and unusual — a
powerful counter-model to shame-based correction.
