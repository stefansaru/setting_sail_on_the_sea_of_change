> A supermarket of ways to live — pleasure, power, and meaning on the shelves, Viktor Frankl as your guide — where you choose what fills the cart and what actually nourishes.

## The image
"Welcome to the existential supermarket. Shelves full, lights bright, options endless."
You enter with a vague shopping list "written not in ink, but in the quiet questions
you carry inside" (the [[the-existential-void|existential void]]). Your guide is
[[viktor-frankl]], "the meaning specialist." The aisles are [[the-three-wills]]:
pleasure (bright, "empty calories"), power ("a foundation of sand"), and meaning ("less
flashy, but filled with depth"). At the **checkout of reflection** you ask whether your
cart holds "items that truly satisfy our deeper hunger, or have we been distracted by
the bright lights and flashy deals?"

## What it teaches
It is the unifying conceit for the whole meaning chapter — turning abstract
philosophies of motivation into a relatable shopping trip. It encodes the central
lesson that we *choose* what to pursue, that not everything on offer nourishes, and
that "what we put in our carts today will shape the lives we live tomorrow."

## Why it sticks
A supermarket is mundane and universal; mapping Freud/Adler/Frankl onto aisles, and
distraction onto "flashy deals," makes a dense philosophical debate instantly legible
and even funny.
