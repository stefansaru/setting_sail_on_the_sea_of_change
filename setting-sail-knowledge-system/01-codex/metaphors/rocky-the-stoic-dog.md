> A dog tied to a cart called "life," pulled by a wild horse called "circumstance," has two choices: resist and get dragged through the mud, or run alongside with dignity.

## The image
"Imagine stoicism as Rocky the stoic dog, a dog tied to a cart called 'life,' pulled by
a wild horse named 'circumstance.' The road ahead is rough and unpredictable. Rocky has
two choices: resist and get dragged through the mud, or run alongside with dignity,
adapting to the journey." Rocky can't control the cart's direction, "but he could
control how he moved with it."

## What it teaches
The definitive image for [[stoicism]] and the dichotomy of control: you don't choose
your circumstances, only your response to them. It is the canine cousin of
[[you-are-the-sailor-not-the-wind]] and the basis of [[outcome-independence]].
(The image descends from the ancient Stoic metaphor of the dog tied to the cart,
attributed to Zeno/Chrysippus.)

## Why it sticks
The vivid, slightly comic binary — dignified trot vs. dragged through mud, with the
*exact same cart and horse* — makes the abstract idea of "accept what you can't
control" physical and unforgettable.
