> Life is a giant tree (cells the roots, species the branches, each being a leaf, evolution the gardener) — and a flame passed candle to candle, never restarting from zero.

## The image
**The tree:** "the seed... planted in an ancient world"; roots = single-celled life; trunk
= multicellular life; branches = species; "every leaf is a living being." The tree outlives
any leaf, and "evolution is the unseen gardener... cutting what doesn't work, keeping what
does." **The flame:** life "doesn't start from zero" — "like one candle lighting another,
the flame moves forward." A parent's cells "become part of the next generation," so "the
first cells that ever lived are still dividing... inside us."

## What it teaches
The twin images carry [[what-is-life]]: evolution as patient gardener (selection) and life
as continuous flame (heredity/continuity). The tree shows *change across time*; the flame
shows *unbroken transmission* — together, descent with modification, made vivid.

## Why it sticks
Two of the most universal images — a tree and a fire — capture two hard biological ideas
(common descent; genetic continuity) in pictures a child can hold. "Carrying the torch"
gives the abstract a felt, almost sacred weight.
