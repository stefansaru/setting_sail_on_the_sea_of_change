> A weary camel who looks into a magical mirror, transforms into a defiant lion, battles the Thou-Shalt Dragon, and is reborn as a playful child — the book's mascot for the whole journey of becoming yourself.

## The image
Cammy starts as a sad, overburdened camel "marching slowly on the sand dunes,
mumbling complaints." At a hidden oasis she meets [[the-magical-mirror]], which tells
her the burdens are too heavy and that she carries them only because she keeps
choosing to. After a vision and collapse, she sheds her baggage, roars "no means
no!", and transforms — mane, jaw, claws — into a lion. As "the leader of her own
revolution," she leaves the desert and battles the [[thou-shalt-dragon]]. Exhausted
by unwinnable fights, her roar softens to laughter and she is reborn as **"Cammy the
Kid,"** seeing the world as a playground.

## What it teaches
Cammy is the single narrative thread that carries the entire
[[three-metamorphoses]]: [[camel-phase]] → [[lion-phase]] → [[child-phase]]. She
makes an abstract Nietzschean model concrete, emotional, and memorable.

## Why it sticks
A named character with a visible physical transformation (camel → lion → child)
gives the philosophy a face and an arc. The continuity of *one* character through
three forms encodes the crucial idea that these are stages of *the same self*, not
different people.
