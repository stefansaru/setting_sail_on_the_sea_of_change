> Shakespeare's image of jealousy as a green-eyed monster that "feeds on doubt and insecurity, consuming its victims from within."

## The image
From *Othello*: "beware, my lord, of jealousy; it is the green-eyed monster." The book
notes Shakespeare "paints jealousy as a creature that feeds on doubt and insecurity,
consuming its victims from within" — green long linked to "illness and unease," a fitting
symbol for "an emotion that can spiral out of control."

## What it teaches
The literary face of [[jealousy-as-evolutionary-relic]]: jealousy as a devouring internal
creature. Pairing the centuries-old metaphor with the evolutionary explanation shows the
*same* emotion described by a poet (a monster within) and by biology (misfiring
mate-guarding software).

## Why it sticks
"Green-eyed monster" is already lodged in common speech; anchoring the chapter's
evolutionary argument to a phrase everyone knows makes the science memorable.
