> We each play many roles — professional, parent, friend. When the gap between who we are and the role we perform grows too wide, the body may refuse to keep up the act: depression as "deep rest."

## The image
Borrowing Shakespeare's "all the world's a stage, and all the men and women merely
players," the book frames us as actors adapting to each setting. "When the gap between
who we are and who we pretend to be grows too wide, it can lead to exhaustion,
dissatisfaction, or even depression." Jim Carrey's extension: depression can feel like
"your body no longer wants to maintain the 'character' or role you've been playing" —
and Jeff Foster's reframe: "depressed" as "**deep rest**," a signal to step out of the
persona.

## What it teaches
The central image of [[depression-vs-low-self-esteem]]: distress as a symptom of
*role–self misalignment*. It reframes depression from defect to signal — the body
forcing a break from a performance that has drifted too far from the authentic self.

## Why it sticks
"Deep rest" hidden inside the word "depressed" is a memorable reframe, and the
stage/actor image gives a gentle, non-pathologizing way to talk about a heavy subject.
