> At first you're just a silhouette in someone else's painting, shaped by circumstance. Then you realize nothing stops you from stepping out of the frame, picking up the brush, and painting your own masterpiece.

## The image
"At first, you're just a silhouette in someone's painting, shaped by past experiences and
circumstances. But then, you realize — nothing is stopping you from becoming the painter.
You step out of the frame, pick up the brush, and start creating your own masterpiece,
exactly the way you envision it." "You're the artist of your life… you get to choose the
colors, the depth, the themes."

## What it teaches
The image for [[taking-responsibility]] and self-authorship: you can move from being
*painted by* circumstance to *painting* your life. It rhymes with the [[child-phase]]
(creation), [[blank-canvas]] (Ch.9, meaning is painted on), and [[becoming-the-observer]]
(stepping out of the scene to direct it).

## Why it sticks
The shift from "silhouette in someone's painting" to "the painter holding the brush" is a
vivid, dignifying picture of reclaiming authorship — easy to recall at moments of feeling stuck.
