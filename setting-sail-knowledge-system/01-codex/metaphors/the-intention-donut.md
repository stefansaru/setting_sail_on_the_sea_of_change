> A donut whose hole is your intention and whose ring is the context — with an "arrow" of feeling running through it. Stack the joy-donut and pain-donut on a bigger one, and its arrow is balance.

## The image
Introduced at a joyful dinner: picture the good elements (connection, warmth, flavor) as
toppings on a doughnut, with a "feel-good arrow" through the middle. Now picture the bad
elements (waste, an animal's suffering, a friend's hidden struggle) as toppings on a
second doughnut with a "feel-bad arrow." Stack both on a *bigger* doughnut — its arrow
is **balance** ([[awareness-of-both]]). In the finale (§10.11) the donut becomes the
universal tool: **hole = intention** (how you want to act), **ring = context** (people,
mood, tools, risks, constraints).

## What it teaches
The signature image of the whole book's capstone, [[intention-in-context]]: it makes an
abstract method tangible and lets you nest perspectives (good / bad / balanced). The
hole-and-ring structure cleanly separates *what you choose* (intention) from *what you're
in* (context).

## Why it sticks
A donut is friendly, concrete, and slightly absurd — and the hole-as-intention pun is
memorable. It scales from a single situation to a whole life of "donuts," making it a
reusable mental object.
