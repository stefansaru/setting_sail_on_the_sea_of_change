# Ch.3 — nietzsche’s journey from submission to creation

*( the three metamorphoses )*

## §3.1 the camel phase

our journey begins with what we'll call the camel phase. it's a bit like showing up at a free all-you-can-eat buffet for the first time—you end up agreeing to everything!
the world around you—society, friends, family—loads up your plate with expectations, rules, and a lot of "you shoulds." you become a yes-camel, marching through the desert dunes of doing what's expected, carrying all these weights with a kind of honor.
> like the camel, that, when laden, hastens into the desert, so speeds the spirit into its desert. — friedrich nietzsche
welcome to the rat race, where finding comfort while carrying your load makes you the employee of this month.
the camel phase represents the stage of life where we take on the burdens of traditions, and societal expectations.
we're weighed down by duties, obligations, and values imposed from the outside, rarely stopping to ask if they truly resonate with us or reflect who we are.

## §3.2 challenges faced by the camel

- overburdened by societal expectations: struggling under the weight of what society expects them to be or achieve.
- lack of personal identity: having a sense of self that's heavily defined by roles, titles, or societal labels rather than individual essence.
- fear of non-conformity: the anxiety associated with stepping out of societal norms and expectations.
- difficulty in saying 'no': a tendency to take on more responsibilities, unable to refuse for fear of disappointing others.
- suppressing personal desires: putting one's own passions and interests aside to meet external demands.
- moral and ethical conformity: adhering strictly to societal moral codes without questioning their relevance to personal growth.
- resisting change: sticking to the familiar, even when it's not fulfilling, due to fear of the unknown.
- living for others: prioritizing the happiness and approval of others over personal fulfillment.
- ignoring inner voice: the inner self is silenced by the loud demands of external obligations.
- perfectionism driven by external validation: striving for an unattainable standard of perfection to satisfy societal views.
- loss of creativity: the creative self is stifled by the need to conform and perform specific roles.
*( dunes of duty )*
- dependency on external guidance: reliance on societal norms and expectations for making life decisions.
- fear of judgment: worrying excessively about being judged negatively by others for one's choices or failures.
- self-sacrifice: constantly sacrificing personal needs and well-being to carry the burdens placed by others.
- lack of personal goals: goals are often defined by what is expected or deemed respectable by society, not by personal passion or interest.
- struggle with authenticity: the challenge of being true to oneself while fulfilling the role of the camel.
- avoiding self-reflection: engaging in constant activity and responsibility to avoid confronting personal desires or dissatisfaction.
- materialistic pursuits: valuing material success and possessions as defined by societal standards over personal growth and happiness.
- feeling trapped: the sensation of being stuck in a cycle of obligations, with the belief that there is no other way.
- burnout: emotional, mental, and physical exhaustion from attempting to meet every expectation and demand.

## §3.3 camel phase crisis

"but wait—what's this? why am i carrying all this stuff?" the camel asks, noticing cracks in the stories it has been told.
this is where the journey deepens. the camel begins to look inward. questions appear. doubts surface. the burdens once carried with pride now feel heavy, perhaps even unnecessary.
in this crisis, individuals start to question the values they have taken from society without reflection. is this really mine, or something i’ve been told to carry?
questions pondered by the camel
- am i living my life or someone else’s idea of it?
- what dreams have i given up on?
- what fears are holding me back?
- how can i start living more authentically?
- what steps can i take to initiate change?
this is a good moment to pause and observe yourself. journaling can help. take a notebook and write down the activities you did yesterday. next to each one, note how you felt while doing it, and how you felt afterwards.
use a simple scale—bad to good, 1 to 5, or 1 to 10. the numbers don’t matter. what matters is to begin noticing.
some examples:
- going to the gym: maybe it feels like a 2 while lifting weights, but afterwards it rises to a 4.
- scrolling social media: it might feel like a 5 while you’re doing it, but drops to a 2 when you put the phone down—tired, scattered.
- eating healthy food: maybe only a 3 while chewing, but at least a 4 afterwards, feeling light and steady.
- eating fast food: a 5 during, but later a 2, heavy and regretful.
try this for a few days. don’t judge what you see. just observe. the act of noticing is already the beginning of change.

## §3.4 cammy the tired camel

imagine a sad camel, burdened with lots of "shoulds" and "musts," marching slowly on the sand dunes, mumbling complaints about the weight she carries. the camel, known to her friends as cammy, has become very tired of carrying all these burdens.
one quiet summer afternoon, under the hot sun, cammy stumbled upon a mystical oasis, which wasn’t marked on any map. here, on the ground close to her, among the palm trees stood an ancient magical mirror. driven by curiosity, cammy approached the mirror and as cammy looked into the mirror, it whispered, "the burdens you carry, my child, are too heavy."
surprised yet intrigued, cammy replied to the mirror, "i am so tired of carrying these burdens, but i just can’t let them go."
"and why can’t you let go of these burdens?" the mirror calmly asked cammy.
cammy believed that carrying these burdens was a sign of strength to others. "if i didn't carry any burdens, then everyone would think i'm weak."
the mirror sparkled softly, and it whispered "these burdens are yours only because you keep choosing to accept them. "
cammy closed her eyes for a brief moment, and listened to the mirror's gentle voice.
"my child, you care how others judge you, but just how much do others care about you when they place their burdens on you...?" the mirror asked as cammy closed her eyes.
time seemed to stand still, and when cammy opened her eyes again, the sun had set far below the horizon, the moon had risen shining a dim light over the desert. the oasis and the mirror had vanished. there were no palm trees in sight.
cammy realized she had experienced a mirage, a vision that perhaps carried a deeper truth, right before she passed out.

## §3.5 the lion phase

as cammy rested, she noticed all her baggage had fallen down beside her, scattered and covered with sand. a vulture, spotting cammy in this vulnerable state, landed nearby and asked her, "are you okay?" to which she replied, "yes, i'm okay."
the vulture, sensing her hesitation, asked again, "are you sure?" cammy admitted, "actually, no. not really. no."
the vulture offered, "let’s get you up." with the assistance of the vulture, cammy got back on her feet, expressing her gratitude.
the vulture saw a bloody wound on cammy's back and asked, "how did you end up hurting yourself?"
cammy, unsure, answered "i don't know."
as the vulture attempted to reload the baggage onto her back, cammy protested, "no, wait."
but when a bag accidentally touched her wound, she roared in pain, "i said no! no means no!" which scared the vulture, and after mumbling "sorry, i didn’t mean that," it flew away into the quiet night.
fueled by a surge of adrenaline, cammy roared a defiant "no!" into the night sky and started to kick all the baggage around.
during this rage episode under the moon light, cammy heard a cracking noise and realized she had stepped on a broken mirror, just like the one in her vision. it turns out she had been unknowingly carrying this mirror part of its baggage, and now it lay cracked in a spider web pattern beneath her feet.
looking once more into the shattered mirror, cammy no longer recognized herself; she had transformed, now having a mane and resembling a lion, with brown fur, a strong jaw and sharp teeth.
like a whisper from a distant dream, the gentle voice of the mirror reached her ears once more,  whispering "my child, you're not part of the crowd anymore. you're the leader of your own revolution."
right after that, the mirror broke into lots of small pieces. as this happened, cammy looked towards the sky and yelled "no! wait!" she didn't want the mirror to go away; she still needed more advice.

## §3.6 what is the lion doing?

the lion phase represents a significant metamorphosis, where cammy the lion uses her newfound strength and voice to assert her individuality, freedom, and the power to create her own destiny.
- abandoning approval-seeking behaviors: no longer striving to gain the approval or validation of others.
- breaking free from conformity: actively resisting the pressure to conform to group norms or trends.
- rejecting societal norms: courageously opposing societal expectations that do not align with personal values.
- challenging cultural expectations: critiquing and moving beyond the expectations and limitations imposed by one's culture.
- rejecting guilt and obligation: refusing to be motivated by guilt or a sense of obligation in making life choices.
- abandoning unhealthy relationships: cutting connections that demand self-sacrifice or compromise of integrity.
- questioning moral absolutes: reevaluating moral and ethical standards that were previously accepted without question.
- revoking passive acceptance: actively disputing rather than passively accepting the status quo.
- defeating fear of change: overcoming the fear that previously limited new challenges and opportunities.
- abandoning perfectionism: refusing the unattainable standards of perfection imposed by oneself or society.
- revolutionizing self-perception: transforming the way one views oneself, moving away from societal definitions of worth.
- creating personal meaning: declining externally imposed meanings of life and purpose, in favor of creating one’s own
- defying traditional roles: refusing to conform to roles traditionally assigned by family, culture, or society.
- questioning authority: challenging the legitimacy of authority figures and the rules they impose.
*( not refusing to say no )*
- discarding unwanted duties: letting go of responsibilities that were taken on to please others or due to societal pressure.
- denouncing materialism: rejecting the pursuit of material wealth as the primary measure of success.
- embracing authentic desires: prioritizing personal passions and interests over what is deemed practical or acceptable by others.
- refusing to compromise: standing firm on personal beliefs and deep rooted values, even when faced with opposition.

## §3.7 encountering the dragon

as cammy the lion mourned the loss of the ancient mystical mirror, she began a quest for another mirror, one that could perhaps offer more guidance or reflection on her new identity.
leaving the desert behind, she noticed her old camel friends didn't even recognize her anymore in this new and majestic lion form.
just as she was about to leave the desert's edge, the vulture from before swooped down, blocking her path with a stern, "cammy, wait! thou shalt remain here where you belong."
but cammy, with newfound determination, replied firmly, "no! get out of my way." when the vulture refused to move, cammy swiped at it with her powerful lion paw, only to watch in astonishment as the vulture transformed into a shiny golden shield bearing the inscription, "thou shalt not pass." despite her best efforts, cammy couldn't break the shield. her sharp lion claws couldn’t even scratch its golden surface. she chose to continue on her journey, leaving the shield behind as she ventured into the savannah.
feeling thirsty, cammy soon sought water and found a river. but her path was once again block by two other vultures, declaring, "cammy! you must pay for the water; it's not free. thou shalt pay water tax." unwilling to comply, cammy responded, "i'm not paying any water tax. step aside, you two." in an instant, these vultures also transformed into two golden shields, each inscribed with "thou shalt pay water tax", blocking her access to the river. despite her struggles, cammy managed only a quick sip of water before she had to flee, leaving these shields behind.
approaching the end of the savannah, cammy found a forest across a crocodile-infested river. a not-so-welcoming committee of vultures landed on the crocodiles, shouting, "cammy! thou shalt not cross the river without paying us the river crossing tax." frustrated, cammy declared, "what? again with the taxes? no, thank you," and prepared to confront both the crocodiles and the vultures. though she managed to scare off the crocodiles, the vultures grouped together, transforming into hundreds of golden shields, each inscribed with a commandment of "thou shalt." cammy watched this surreal scene and uttered 'what... what are you?’ as the shields assembled, a tremendous dragon appeared, proclaiming itself the mighty "thou shalt dragon" and demanding, "cammy the camel! thou shalt return at once to your baggage duties! it is the way of the desert you seek."
but cammy, no longer a camel but a lion with the heart of a warrior, roared back a defiant "no! i’m not cammy the camel." and leaped to challenge the dragon.
this wasn't any ordinary dragon. the thou-shalt dragon was a beast that slithered through the sands of tradition, breathing fire made of societal expectations and spewing smoke laced with the heavy weight of "what must be done."
each of its scales were shields which shimmered with the "thou-shalt” phrases of limitation, and its eyes glowed with the insistence of conformity.
"cammy the camel," hissed the dragon, its voice a cacophony of commands. "you dare defy the ways of the desert? thou shalt conform to the desert laws."
but cammy, with a heart forged in the trials of transformation, stood tall. "i was once a camel," cammy replied, the memories of burdens borne and identities shed shimmering in the heat. "and now, i am a lion, not by fate but by choice. my roar, my song, my path—they are mine to choose."
> thou-shalt, lies in his path, sparkling with gold- a scale-covered beast; and on each scale glitters a golden "thou-shalt!" the values of a thousand years glitter on those scales, and thus speaks the mightiest of all dragons: "all values of all things glitter on me. all value has long been created, and i am all created value. verily, there shall be no more 'i will'.  — friedrich nietzsche

## §3.10 mastering the art of saying no

in the camel phase, you say yes to everything. you carry all the burdens placed on you. you do what’s expected, not what’s right for you. saying yes becomes a habit — even when it hurts.
you do it to fit in. to be helpful. to avoid conflict. but over time, it wears you down. you wake up tired, overloaded, and disconnected from what actually matters to you.
the lion phase is when things start to shift. you find your voice. you learn to say no — not out of defiance, but out of clarity. no becomes a boundary. a filter. a choice.
a simple no can sometimes save you hours, days, or even years of frustration. you don’t need to justify every no with a 10-minute explanation. and you don’t have to be harsh. in fact, the most effective no is often the softest one — clear, kind, and firm.
saying no doesn’t mean you’re difficult. it means you’ve decided to stop doing things that drain you or distract you. it means you’re protecting the limited time and energy you have. not everything deserves a yes.
when you say no to what’s wrong for you, you make space for what’s right. more rest. more focus. better relationships. better work.
learn to say no in a way people understand. you can refuse without being rude. you can decline without burning bridges:
- “i’m not the right fit for this.”
- “thanks for thinking of me, but i’ll pass.”
- “i can’t take this on right now.”
you don’t owe people your time just because they ask. you owe yourself honesty. you owe yourself peace.
and remember — the lion doesn’t need to roar at everything all the time. sometimes the lion can just calmly walk away.

## §3.13 be alone for a while

sometimes the best way to find yourself is to step back. retreat. be alone for a while.
life is noisy. people, work, and endless distractions can drown out your own voice. solitude gives you the chance to listen again.
when you sit in quiet, the clutter fades. you start noticing what you really think and feel. you see what matters to you, not just what the world demands.
this is how you uncover your values. not by chasing more, but by pausing. not by asking others, but by asking yourself.
you don’t need a monastery or a mountain. you can simply turn off your phone, close the door, and sit with your thoughts. write them down. breathe. ask yourself simple questions: what do i care about? what drains me? what gives me energy?
spending time in nature helps too. a walk under the trees can clear your mind in ways a crowded room cannot.
retreat is not escape. it’s a return—to yourself.
a simple retreat routine
1. choose your space
find a quiet spot where you won’t be disturbed. it can be your room, a park bench, or a walk in nature.
1. disconnect
put your phone on silent or leave it behind. remove distractions so your attention turns inward.
1. breathe
sit comfortably. close your eyes. take slow breaths. let your body settle.
1. listen
notice your thoughts without judgment. don’t push them away. just observe what shows up.
1. ask gently
pose simple questions to yourself:
– what matters most to me right now?
– what do i need less of?
– what do i need more of?
1. write it down
jot down anything that feels true. keep it short and honest. this helps you see patterns over time.
1. return slowly
when you’re done, ease back into your day. carry one insight with you, even if it’s small.

## §3.14 from roar to rebirth and laughter

the lion represents a stage of development where one has the strength to say "no" to external authorities and the established values that nietzsche refers to as the "thou shalts" symbolized by the dragon. however, the lion's power is still reactive rather than creative; it can reject, but it cannot yet define its own values.
the transition to the child phase is triggered by the realization that mere negation is not enough. while the lion can resist and deny, it cannot properly build new values or innovate new ideas.
but just when all seems lost, a new desire flickers to life: a yearning not to destroy, but to create. it's in this moment of existential contemplation that the lion discovers a new path, one that leads to rebirth.
cammy begins to see the world not as a battlefield but as a playground, vast and ripe with possibilities. the once ferocious roar softens into sparks of laughter, a joyful acknowledgment of life's absurdities.
in the metamorphosis from the lion to the child, the "sparks of creation" serve as the guiding light.
the child phase represents the stage of innocence and a re-birth of creativity where one can say "yes" to life, create new values, and playfully engage with the world in a manner that is free from the dogma and moral codes of the past.
this phase is marked by the lion's courageous decision to let go of its rebellious roar against the "thou-shalt" mandates of the world, transitioning towards the child's state of boundless creativity and joyful affirmation of life.
these “sparks” ignite a profound metamorphosis within, where defiance gives way to the innocence of creation, and resistance melts into the playful embrace of the perks of your own existence.

## §3.16 the courage to let go, and to move forward

courage isn't just a nice quality to have; it's essential for experiencing life fully. even the camel, with its humps, has the courage to journey across the desert. the lion and the child also show courage as they confidently navigate their paths.
remember, courage isn't about being fearless. it's about facing your fears and moving forward despite them. everyone feels scared sometimes—it's a natural part of life, like having popcorn at the movies.
here's a tip—sometimes, all it takes is a gentle reminder to your frightened inner child, a quiet "we've got this," and you'll find yourself feeling braver than before.
ask yourself, "who is really frightened? is it me or my inner child?"
speak softly and kindly to that scared part of yourself, just as you would to a frightened child who needs reassurance and comfort.
only by caring for your inner child, can you truly transition into the child phase.

## §3.17 the child phase

and so, the child phase begins. cammy, our once fierce lion, now reborn as "cammy the kid”, sees the world with fresh eyes.
the ‘thou-shalt’ dragon? no longer an enemy, but a magnificent playground waiting to be explored. this stage isn't about going back to childhood innocence; it's about advancing to a state of spiritual maturity driven by unchained creativity.
the child now enjoys the realm of possibility, crafting new worlds with the pure joy of imagination.
- why does the child phase sound like a beginning, when it's actually the final phase?
the child symbolizes a rebirth into a state of innocence and wonder, reminiscent of the beginning of life. it represents a fresh start, unburdened by the dogmas and dictates that previously confined the individual.
this rebirth is not a regression but a forward movement into creating a life based on self-affirmed values.
in the child phase, the emphasis is on creation and playfulness, qualities often associated with the start of new endeavors. the individual, now free from the constraints of "thou-shalt," engages in the world with creativity and joy, akin to the way a child explores and interacts with their surroundings uninhibitedly.
the child phase is the foundation upon which new values are built. unlike the camel, which accepts external values, and the lion, which rejects them, the child creates new values. this creative act is a beginning in itself, laying the groundwork for a life guided by personal authenticity and freedom.

## §3.19 what comes after the child phase?

the übermensch (overman) is an idealized figure who has managed to overcome the passive, herd mentality that nietzsche critiques in modern society. cammy the child has grown beyond the metaphorical "house on the lake" where even though life is comfortable, it lacks deeper meaning or purpose.
by discarding the comfort of the „known”, the übermensch embrace the risk of venturing in the „unknown” and the responsibility of shaping an authentic existence. the übermensch become masters of their own fate, no longer puppets swayed by the strings of tradition or dogma.  the übermensch dare to create new values, through creative self-affirmation
friedrich nietzsche's concept of the three metamorphoses—camel, lion, and child—from "thus spoke zarathustra," does not mention any other phase beyond the child. the child phase represents the pinnacle of this philosophical journey, embodying a return to innocence, a rebirth of creativity, and the establishment of one's own values and meanings in the world.
it signifies the achievement of a state of being where one is capable of creating new values and affirming life joyfully, without the baggage of societal norms and expectations.  this phase is about living in the present, and engaging in the world with a sense of wonder and playfulness.

## §3.20 defining your own values

in order to define your own values, simply think about what something means to you—and you alone—not to someone else.
strip away external expectations, societal norms, and family pressures. just listen. the answer comes from your inner voice, not from what the world says is "right" or "wrong."
there is no universal template to follow, no ideal way to live. it’s just about how you do your own thing, in a way that makes sense to you.
financial values
- risk vs. stability: some people value financial security above all, saving diligently and avoiding risky investments. others prioritize high-risk, high-reward ventures like entrepreneurship or stock trading.
- materialism vs. minimalism: some define success by what they own, while others feel burdened by possessions and prefer a simpler lifestyle.
- work-life balance and earnings: some prioritize high earnings even at the cost of personal time, while others value a lower salary if it means less stress and more freedom.
social values
- friendship and loyalty: some prioritize deep, long-lasting friendships, while others may prefer independence and socializing in a more casual way.
- altruism vs. self-interest: some feel fulfilled by helping others and contributing to society, while others prioritize self-growth and personal achievements.
- community vs. individualism: some find purpose in being part of a larger group, while others prefer a more self-reliant approach to life.
health and lifestyle values
- physical fitness vs. comfort: some people make fitness and an active lifestyle a core part of their identity, while others prioritize relaxation and indulgence.
- longevity vs. enjoyment: some focus on extending life expectancy through careful choices, while others believe life is short and should be lived fully, even if it involves some risk.
- mental vs. physical well-being: some invest heavily in mental health (meditation, therapy), while others focus more on diet and exercise.
knowledge and learning values
- practical knowledge vs. intellectual pursuits: some prioritize skills that bring immediate results (technical training, job skills), while others enjoy knowledge for its own sake (philosophy, history).
- experience vs. education: some believe real-world experience is more valuable than formal education, while others see academic achievements as a foundation for success.
freedom and security values
- routine vs. spontaneity: some thrive in structured, predictable environments, while others need excitement and change.
- freedom vs. responsibility: some prefer complete independence, while others value stability, even if it comes with obligations.
- conformity vs. uniqueness: some find comfort in following social norms, while others define themselves by going against the grain.

## §3.21 the importance of values

your values are the foundation of your life’s direction. unlike fleeting opinions or changing circumstances, values are the deeper parameters that remain mostly constant throughout your life. they act as guiding principles, shaping your decisions and helping you navigate challenges.
if you want to plan a lifelong, fulfilling mission—one that consists of multiple projects, each with its own objectives—then what happens when these projects and objective fail to align with your values? where something doesn't fit your values, you will naturally struggle to overcome obstacles. the key is to first define your values before setting your course.

## §3.22 listen to your inner voice

you can of course take a look at the previous examples and think of some values that are meaningful to you. then, go a step further—ask yourself, "how do i stand?" and listen carefully. the answer may be a quiet whisper from within, barely audible at first. but the moment you hear an answer that resonates deeply, one that you wholly agree with—that’s your value. that’s your truth. and it doesn’t need to make sense to anyone else but you.

## §3.23 binary values

but please don’t define all values in rudimentary black and white. some values may be absolute—certain things that are crystal clear to you, where a simple yes or no is enough. but most values exist in the shades in between. life is rarely that simple, and neither are your guiding principles. think of what you can tolerate, what you absolutely cannot, and what might depend on context. be as objective as possible.
a purely binary value is a predefined yes or no to some question. but in reality, very few things are so clearly defined. thus, an effective value should be more than a rigid answer—it should exist on a spectrum, a scale that allows for nuance. it is okay to include compromises, dealbreakers, or exceptions to underlying rules, as long as they remain true to your core beliefs.
for example, meritocracy as a black-and-white value:
"i value hard work and success that is earned. i do not value success that comes from inheritance, winning the lottery, or sheer luck. i can’t respect someone who was simply lucky enough to be placed in a position they are legally entitled to, but not actually competent for."
now consider financial literacy as a grayscale value:
"i value maintaining a healthy financial life and dedicate time to learning and improving my understanding of money management. however, i don’t take financial advice from someone with less money than me. i prefer insights from those in a similar financial situation, who are facing similar challenges. and i often seek advice from highly successful individuals who have built their net worth through hard work, passion and dedication."
your values, written in the present tense, form your personal code of conduct. this is how you roll. this is how you do business, how you love, how you connect with family and friends. your values define your actions, your mindset, and ultimately, the direction of your life.

## §3.24 love and relationships

before prioritizing love, it's important to strengthen other areas of life. poor health, financial instability, or emotional insecurity can strain a relationship. on maslow’s hierarchy, love rests on a foundation of safety and stability. to build a lasting connection, you must be ready to welcome love at the right time.
attraction is increased when you have a fulfilling life beyond your relationship. if you become the main focus of your partner’s hopes and dreams, it can feel more like a responsibility than a connection—almost like being a caretaker rather than an equal.
> women like men to have that sparkle in their eyes, but they don’t want to be that sparkle. — caleb jones
if someone is overly dependent on you for their happiness, they may need to focus on their personal growth first.
- by the way, if you encounter such people, please consider recommending this book to them before they dive into their love life
every relationship starts with the dating phase—a time of excitement, discovery, and intense emotions. during this stage, decisions may feel more emotional than logical. this is a biological response, prioritizing reproduction when a partner is found. however, this rush doesn’t last forever, and your feelings will likely change over time.
at the beginning, the partners make a conscious effort to meet, spend time together, and impress each other. but as a relationship transitions into the long-term phase, daily routines replace intentional effort. what was once a deliberate choice to be together gradually becomes an expectation, a habit, a shared life.
some people stay in the dating loop for quite a long time, constantly chasing the thrill of new relationships. over time, however, most grow tired of dating and begin to seek long-term stability, prioritizing deeper connections over fleeting excitement.
finding the right partner
in the dating phase, it’s important to identify key values that matter most to you. beyond physical attraction, compatibility usually depends on:
- education level – shared intellectual curiosity and communication styles.
- financial mindset – similar views on spending, saving, and stability.
- spiritual beliefs – alignment in worldview and life philosophy.
mismatches in core values become burdens over time. while initial attraction may overlook these factors, long-term relationships require deeper alignment.
location also plays a role. bigger cities offer more opportunities to meet like-minded partners, while small towns may limit options. expanding your social environment increases your chances of finding someone truly compatible.
- patience and selection
choosing the right partner takes time. desperation leads to poor choices, while patience allows you to wait for a connection that truly feels right.
to filter out the wrong candidates, consider:
✽ short initial meetings – meet in a public place for no more than an hour to avoid unnecessary investment.
✽ keeping options open – lining up multiple first dates prevents overinvesting in one person too soon. knowing you have other options helps reduce neediness, which is naturally unattractive.
- growing apart
people sometimes grow apart as their core values change over time. when this happens, the best course of action is to say goodbye and move on. if your partner doesn’t see an issue with the shift in values, forcing change won’t help—how they think and feel is not within your control.
it’s often difficult and sad, but the only real choice is to calmly present your decision to step away. forcing alignment where it no longer exists only leads to frustration. sometimes, letting go is the healthiest option, allowing both individuals to move forward in a direction that truly suits them.
- growing together
when partners share the same core values, and those values remain aligned over time, the relationship naturally progresses into the long-term phase. this phase brings a deep sense of fulfillment, as you build a life together. however, maintaining a long-term relationship requires consistent effort and intentional actions.
key principles for a healthy long-term relationship
- learn each other’s love language – love is expressed differently by everyone. understanding how your partner gives and receives love is essential for long-term harmony.
- plan quality time together – in the dating phase, spontaneity happens naturally. in a long-term relationship, it must be intentional. set aside time in your busy schedules to do things you both enjoy.
- communicate openly – during dating, emotions are heightened, and partners instinctively tune into each other’s needs. over time, this fades. don’t expect mind-reading—be clear about your needs, and listen to theirs.
- argue constructively – disagreements are inevitable, but partners are not emotional punching bags. address issues calmly, respectfully, and with a problem-solving mindset.
> when one of you yells, the other listens. — unknown
- heal emotional wounds – unresolved emotional wounds can strain a relationship. seeking professional guidance is not a sign of failure—if something feels broken, consider therapy. relationships sometimes need maintenance too.
- there is no such thing as a perfect relationship, even if social media makes it seem that way. what you see online is often a highlight reel, not reality. every relationship has challenges, disagreements, and moments of doubt.
as tolstoy wrote in anna karenina:
> all happy families are alike; each unhappy family is unhappy in its own way. — leo tolstoy
healthy relationships follow certain universal, common-sense principles—respect, communication, shared goals—while unhealthy ones fall apart in unique ways.
do things right, and harmony follows.
ignore the essentials, and dissatisfaction becomes inevitable.
- brick by brick 🧱
a relationship is like building a house, brick by brick—each moment in time, each shared experience adds to its structure.
- A strong, positive moment becomes a solid brick, reinforcing the foundation.
- A negative experience, if unresolved, becomes a weak, crumbling brick that threatens stability.
most relationships don’t collapse all at once—they break down at the weakest points, where clusters of negative experiences form fragile walls. if the same mistakes are repeated without resolution, those weak walls eventually crumble and fall.
to build a lasting relationship, be mindful of what kind of bricks you’re laying. address conflicts before they stack up, learn from past mistakes, and balance the challenges with positive, reinforcing experiences.
a strong and successful relationship isn’t about luck, and it isn’t about striving for perfection—it’s built on awareness, dedication, and the ability to repair and strengthen what matters.

## §3.25 you’re not the wind, you’re the sailor

when jim rohn was 25 years old, his mentor told him:
> “if you will change, everything will change for you.” – john earl shoaff
stop cursing the wind. stop waiting for perfect weather. the wind is part of nature. some days it’s soft. some days it knocks you over. and sometimes it doesn't blow at all. what matters is the sail you set. your mindset. your attitude. your plan. your response.
the wind is everything outside your control.
it’s the economy. politics. war. the job market. other people’s moods. traffic. weather. trends. luck.
you don’t choose the wind. it doesn’t ask for your opinion.
sometimes the wind pushes you forward. sometimes it knocks you down. sometimes it doesn’t blow at all. and that’s life.
the sailor is you. your mindset. your habits. your actions. your expectations. your reactions.
you don’t get to control what happens. but you get to decide how you show up when it does.
do you adapt? or complain? do you learn? or freeze?
do you grow bitter? or grow better?
if the wind is harsh, you can lower the sail.
if it’s calm, you can row.
if it shifts, you can shift too.
this is the difference between drifting and navigating.
people hoping for the wind to change are waiting.
waiting for better leaders. better markets. better luck.
people focused on the sail are adjusting. learning. moving.
you can’t control the wind. but you don’t have to. you’re not the wind. you’re the sailor. and you set the sail.
